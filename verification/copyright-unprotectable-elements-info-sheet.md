# Confidentiality & IP, new document: What Copyright Does Not Protect — Ideas, Facts, Merger, and Scenes a Faire — Information Sheet

Part of wave 164 (Confidentiality & IP category; wave 164 covers the
same four categories as waves 146/148/.../162: Confidentiality & IP,
Ending employment, Business Formation, Real Estate).

## Why this document

The task brief listed several candidate topics. Reading the full
Confidentiality & IP title list in `data/documents.json` (85 documents)
showed that most of them already exist as whole documents: Code Civ.
Proc. § 2019.210 (`trade_secret_pre_discovery_identification_info_sheet`),
the § 16601/§ 16602.5 sale-of-business and dissolution exceptions
(`sale_of_business_dissolution_noncompete_exceptions_info_sheet`), CIPA
(`california_invasion_of_privacy_act_info_sheet`), the CUTSA injunction
standards (`trade_secret_injunctive_relief_standards_info_sheet`), and
employee non-solicitation after AMN Healthcare (already discussed inside
`employee_nonsolicit`). The search therefore went further afield, to the
boundary question that sits underneath every copyright document in the
corpus: what a copyright does *not* reach.

Grepped both data files (at `HEAD`, before the merge) for every element
of this topic before drafting: `Feist` (0 hits), `Baker v. Selden` (0),
`Satava` (0), `Skidmore` (0), `Ets-Hokin` (0), `scenes a faire` (0),
`merger doctrine` (0 — the 217 clause-file hits for "merger" are all
corporate, nonprofit, and entity mergers), `fact/expression` (0),
`sweat of the brow` (0), `17 U.S.C. § 102` (4 occurrences, all in the
copyright preemption sheet's references to §§ 102-103 as defining
copyright's subject matter), `103(b)` (9 occurrences, none of them the
copyright section — they are Civil Code, Labor Code, and Probate Code
subdivisions), `thin copyright` (5 apparent hits, every one a false
positive from the string "within copyright's subject matter"),
`virtually identical` (4 hits, all in an estate-planning document about
two nearly identical wills), `idea/expression` (3 hits in one clause,
see below). The one nearby clause is
`copyrightreg_subject_matter_and_idea_expression` inside
`copyright_registration_info_sheet`. That clause does quote both
§ 102(a) and § 102(b) in one paragraph explaining what registration
covers, so `cunp_statute_102` deliberately overlaps with it on the bare
statutory text — that text is the premise of everything else in this
document. The existing clause stops there: it cites no case law at all,
and it does not address originality, the fact/expression dichotomy,
compilations under § 103(b), merger, scenes a faire, combinations of
unprotectable elements, or the thin/broad infringement standards, which
are the subject of the other thirteen authority clauses here.
Checked the other copyright documents in the category
(`copyright_fair_use_info_sheet`, `copyright_first_sale_doctrine_info_sheet`,
`copyright_preemption_doctrine_info_sheet`,
`joint_authorship_copyright_coownership_info_sheet`,
`copyright_termination_right_info_sheet`,
`idea_submission_doctrine_info_sheet`) — none states the § 102(b)
exclusion or the doctrines built on it.

## What this document covers

17 clauses: 3 drafting (purpose/declaration, relationship to sibling
documents, signature/acknowledgment) and 14 authority clauses, citing
17 U.S.C. §§ 102(a), 102(b), 103(a), 103(b); Baker v. Selden, 101 U.S.
99 (1879); Feist Publications, Inc. v. Rural Telephone Service Co., 499
U.S. 340 (1991); Ets-Hokin v. Skyy Spirits, Inc., 225 F.3d 1068 (9th
Cir. 2000); Satava v. Lowry, 323 F.3d 805 (9th Cir. 2003); Mattel, Inc.
v. MGA Entertainment, Inc., 616 F.3d 904 (9th Cir. 2010); and Skidmore
v. Led Zeppelin, 952 F.3d 1051 (9th Cir. 2020) (en banc):

- **`cunp_statute_102`** (§ 102(a), (b)) — the eight categories of works
  of authorship and the exclusion of any idea, procedure, process,
  system, method of operation, concept, principle, or discovery,
  "regardless of the form in which it is described, explained,
  illustrated, or embodied in such work."
- **`cunp_statute_103`** (§ 103(a), (b)) — compilations and derivative
  works are within the subject matter of copyright, but the copyright
  extends only to the material the later author contributed and implies
  no exclusive right in the preexisting material.
- **`cunp_baker_book_versus_art`** (Baker) — the clear distinction
  between the book and the art it illustrates; explanation versus use;
  why an exclusive right in the art is the province of letters-patent.
- **`cunp_baker_necessary_incidents`** (Baker) — the merger ancestor:
  methods and diagrams that cannot be avoided in using the art are
  necessary incidents given to the public with it; blank account books
  are not the subject of copyright.
- **`cunp_feist_originality_requirement`** (Feist) — the two
  propositions in tension; independent creation plus a minimal degree of
  creativity; originality is not novelty; originality as a
  constitutional requirement.
- **`cunp_feist_facts_creation_discovery`** (Feist) — creation versus
  discovery; census takers; the rejection of "sweat of the brow";
  copyright rewards originality, not effort.
- **`cunp_feist_thin_compilation_copyright`** (Feist) — facts do not
  become original through association; the copyright in a factual
  compilation is thin; only selection and arrangement may be protected
  while the raw facts may be copied at will.
- **`cunp_etshokin_merger_scenes_a_faire`** (Ets-Hokin) — the Ninth
  Circuit's statements of merger and scenes a faire, and its holding
  that both are defenses to infringement rather than bars to
  copyrightability.
- **`cunp_satava_ideas_standard_elements`** (Satava) — ideas and
  standard elements denied to artists so they cannot monopolize what
  belongs to the public; the jellyfish applications; ideas first
  expressed by nature as the common heritage of humankind.
- **`cunp_satava_combination_of_unprotectable_elements`** (Satava) — not
  every combination qualifies; the "numerous enough / original enough"
  rule; the resulting thin copyright against virtually identical
  copying.
- **`cunp_mattel_thin_versus_broad`** (Mattel) — filtering; the breadth
  of possible expression; broad protection and substantial similarity
  versus thin protection and virtual identity.
- **`cunp_mattel_filtering_application`** (Mattel) — filtering applied:
  the concept of a young, fashion-forward doll with exaggerated features
  is an unprotectable idea; the expression is highly constrained.
- **`cunp_skidmore_building_blocks`** (Skidmore, en banc) — common or
  trite musical elements and genre-rooted commonplaces belong in the
  public domain; authors borrow from predecessors.
- **`cunp_skidmore_selection_and_arrangement`** (Skidmore, en banc) — a
  selection and arrangement copyright is infringed only by the same
  combination in substantial amounts; a plaintiff cannot reconstitute a
  work as a set of unprotectable elements.

## Genuine corrections and findings

- **A quote failed the substring check and was traced to my own
  extraction, not the source.** An early markup-stripping pass replaced
  HTML tags with a space, which made Cornell's rendering of § 102(b)
  read "process , system". A draft gap note accordingly said Cornell
  "inserts spaces around hyperlinked defined terms." Re-extracting with
  tags removed rather than replaced showed Cornell's text is in fact
  identical to the Office of the Law Revision Counsel's
  ("process, system"), and the § 102 and § 103 gap notes were corrected
  to say the second host's rendering contains the identical statutory
  words. No quote text changed.
- **Baker v. Selden is quoted from Cornell, not CourtListener.**
  CourtListener's copy of 101 U.S. 99 is scan-derived and carries OCR
  damage inside two of the three passages used ("the-book",
  "-therewith", "book-lceeper", "cannot1"). Both Baker clauses therefore
  cite `law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/101/99` and their gap fields
  disclose that CourtListener served only as the second retrieval path.
  Two of the three quotes in `cunp_baker_necessary_incidents` are
  present verbatim only in the Cornell text for that reason; the third
  is present in both.
- **Feist's OCR artifacts were steered around and disclosed.**
  CourtListener's United States Reports text contains "posséss",
  "reeognize[d]", a citation misprinted as "650 P. 2d", and — inside the
  passage this document uses — "idea/ expression" with an inserted
  space. Every Feist quote was selected to avoid those spans, and the
  affected clause gaps name the artifact.
- **Curly versus straight apostrophes across the two Feist hosts.** Two
  Feist quotes ("another's publication", "the compiler's selection")
  match CourtListener verbatim with U+2019 and match Cornell's text only
  after apostrophe normalization. Rather than silently normalizing, the
  quotes reproduce the CourtListener characters exactly and
  `cunp_feist_thin_compilation_copyright`'s gap states that the two
  renderings differ in punctuation characters while carrying the same
  words.
- **Ets-Hokin's accents are wrong in the reporter text, and the quote
  preserves them.** The retrieved Federal Reporter text spells the
  French phrase inconsistently — "scénes á faire" in some sentences and
  "scenes á faire" in the sentence quoted here. The citation reproduces
  the source exactly ("scenes á faire"), the clause body and the
  document title use the plain ASCII form, and the gap field discloses
  the discrepancy.
- **Mattel's OCR artifacts.** The retrieved text contains "hah- styles"
  for "hair styles" and a word split across a page break as "Mat tel";
  quotes were selected to avoid them and the gap says so.
- **A Skidmore mismatch that turned out to be an extraction artifact,
  not a textual difference.** Two Skidmore quotes were initially not
  found in the slip opinion PDF. Inspection showed the PDF text
  extractor inserts spurious intra-word spaces in justified lines
  ("partic ular", "t hat", "sub stantially", "Me tcalf"); the same
  sentences are present. Separately, CourtListener's rendering inserts
  the running page header ("SKIDMORE V. LED ZEPPELIN 45") mid-sentence
  in places. Both gap fields now describe both artifacts and state that
  the quotes follow the CourtListener rendering with the court's hard
  line breaks normalized to single spaces.

## Honest gaps disclosed

- **No retrieval path wholly independent of CourtListener exists in this
  environment for the three older Ninth Circuit opinions** (Ets-Hokin,
  Satava, Mattel). `cdn.ca9.uscourts.gov` is refused by the egress proxy
  (403 on CONNECT), `govinfo.gov`'s USCOURTS path for the Mattel docket
  redirects to its error page, and `storage.courtlistener.com` has no
  slip PDF for any of the three (404). Each of those five clause gaps
  says so expressly, and records what was done instead: two fetches with
  two distinct browser header sets (identical extracted text) plus
  phrase-level confirmation through CourtListener's public search index
  restricted to the opinion's cluster.
- **Phrase-search coverage is uneven, and the gaps say which.** All
  three Ets-Hokin quotes and all six Satava quotes were individually
  phrase-confirmed (`cluster_id:7081237`/`781339` AND "<phrase>", each
  returning count=1). For Mattel, three representative phrases were
  confirmed (`cluster_id:151321`) before CourtListener's public search
  endpoint hit its 125-requests-per-day limit, which is shared across
  parallel agents; the two Mattel gap fields state that the remaining
  phrase searches could not be run for that reason. An initial attempt
  using phrases containing parentheses returned count=0; re-running the
  same passages without the parenthetical returned count=1, so the
  earlier zeros were a query-syntax effect, not a text mismatch.
- **Skidmore's second path is CourtListener's own storage host.** The
  slip opinion PDF at
  `storage.courtlistener.com/pdf/2020/03/09/michael_skidmore_v._led_zeppelin.pdf`
  is the court's typeset document rather than a second publisher's text,
  but it is served by the same organization; the gaps describe it
  accurately as such.
- **Ets-Hokin's first retrieval returned an empty 202.** The page was
  then retrieved successfully twice with two distinct header sets and
  the extracted texts were identical; the clause gap records the empty
  first response.
- **Nimmer, Patry, Ginsburg, Denicola, Patterson & Joyce, Harper & Row,
  Burrow-Giles, The Trade-Mark Cases, Aliotti, Apple Computer, Metcalf,
  CDN v. Kapes, Landsberg, See v. Durang, Herbert Rosenthal, L.A.
  Printex, and Rentmeester** are relied on only as the retrieved
  opinions describe or quote them; no clause cites any of them directly.
- **Scope.** The document covers copyrightable subject matter and the
  scope of protection only. It does not address infringement elements,
  access and copying, registration as a precondition to suit, statutory
  damages, the useful article doctrine, software abstraction-filtration-
  comparison, or patent, trademark, and trade secret protection; the
  relationship clause places those out of scope and points to the
  sibling documents that cover adjacent ground.
- Three of the decisions used (Ets-Hokin, Satava, Mattel) and Skidmore
  are Ninth Circuit law, which is the federal circuit covering
  California; the clause bodies and gaps say so rather than presenting
  them as national rules, and `cunp_etshokin_merger_scenes_a_faire`'s
  gap notes the opinion's own statement that courts disagree about
  whether merger and scenes a faire go to copyrightability or operate as
  defenses.

## Method

- Fetched 17 U.S.C. §§ 102 and 103 from `uscode.house.gov` (Office of
  the Law Revision Counsel, prelim edition, "Text contains those laws in
  effect on September 11, 2026") and independently from
  `law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/17/102` and `.../103`, each under a
  distinct User-Agent. After markup stripping and whitespace
  normalization, all four statutory quotes were confirmed present in
  both hosts' texts.
- Located the five opinions through CourtListener's public,
  unauthenticated `api/rest/v4/search/?type=o` endpoint (outside the MCP
  tool quota), which returned cluster ids 112568 (Feist, 499 U.S. 340),
  90097 (Baker, 101 U.S. 99), 7081237 (Ets-Hokin, 225 F.3d 1068), 781339
  (Satava, 323 F.3d 805), 151321 (Mattel, 616 F.3d 904), and 4733619
  (Skidmore, 952 F.3d 1051). No CourtListener MCP tool call was made at
  any point.
- Fetched each opinion page twice over independent requests with two
  distinct browser User-Agent / Accept / Accept-Language header sets and
  a `Referer: https://www.courtlistener.com/` header; extracted the
  `<article>` body; the two extractions were character-identical for
  every opinion. Feist and Baker were additionally retrieved from
  Cornell's Supreme Court collection
  (`law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/499/340` and `/101/99`), and
  Skidmore from the slip opinion PDF on `storage.courtlistener.com`
  (73 pages, parsed with pypdf).
- Verified every `citations[].quote` by substring match against the
  retrieved texts after collapsing all whitespace runs to single spaces
  and nothing else (no quote-character or hyphenation normalization):
  38 quotes, 0 failures, re-run against the merged `data/clauses.json`
  after the merge and after the gap edits.
- Blocked or unavailable in this environment, and not used:
  `cdn.ca9.uscourts.gov` (proxy 403), `govinfo.gov` USCOURTS paths for
  the Mattel docket (redirect to error page), and the authenticated
  CourtListener REST endpoints (401 without credentials).
- Fields: `preparerName`, `workDescription` ("Description of the work"),
  `dateSigned`, and `cityState` match the most common existing corpus
  definitions exactly; one new document-specific field,
  `elementsAtIssue`. All fields are
  required because the app leaves a blank field's `{{placeholder}}`
  visible in the output.
- Authority clauses carry exactly `{id,title,kind,status,checkedDate,
  body,gap,citations}`; drafting clauses exactly `{id,title,kind,body}`;
  citations exactly `{case,cite,url,quote}`; the document exactly
  `{id,title,description,categories,clauseOrder,fields}`.
- Corpus-wide validation after merge (`validate.py`): both JSON files
  parse and round-trip byte-identically in the repo's two-space,
  non-ASCII-preserving format; no duplicate clause or document ids;
  every id in every document's `clauseOrder` resolves to an existing
  clause; every `{{placeholder}}` in every document's clause bodies and
  gap notes resolves to a declared field on that document.
