# Estate Planning, new document: Substituted Judgment — Court-Authorized Estate Planning for a Conservatee — Information Sheet

Part of wave 169 (Estate Planning category; wave 169 continues the same
category set as waves 145/147/149/151/153/155/157/159/161/163/165/167:
Hiring, During employment, Estate Planning, Family Law).

## Why this document

The Estate Planning category held 87 documents before this one. Every
Estate Planning title in `data/documents.json` was listed, and each
candidate topic was grepped across both data files (clause bodies,
gaps, citation quotes and titles) before choosing:

- **Substituted judgment (Prob. Code §§ 2580-2586)**: zero hits for
  the phrase "substituted judgment" anywhere in `data/clauses.json`
  or `data/documents.json`. The 16 clauses matching "2580" were all
  Family Code § 2580-2581 (the community-property joint-title
  presumption), the Education Code "eraser law" and unrelated
  numbers, in Family Law, Real Estate and Consumer documents. Every
  raw substring hit for "2581" through "2586" belongs to a different
  code — Business and Professions Code §§ 22580-22582 (Privacy
  Rights for California Minors) and Government Code §§ 12581-12586.1
  (the charitable trust registry) — and none is a Probate Code
  reference. Prob. Code § 6100(b) (a conservator making a will) was
  likewise uncovered: the only leginfo section URLs in the corpus
  under that number point to § 6100.5 (testamentary capacity), never
  to § 6100 itself.
  `adult_conservatorship_petition_info` covers the appointment
  petition, the two kinds of conservatorship, the investigator, the
  proposed conservatee's hearing rights and limited conservatorship —
  it says nothing about estate planning by the conservator.
- Candidates checked and rejected as already covered or duplicative:
  the effect of divorce on a will (§ 6122 — already the subject of
  the `will_revocation_by_divorce` clause in the Last Will and
  Testament document); nonprobate transfers to a former spouse
  (§ 5040 — already quoted in `payable_on_death_account_info_sheet`
  and `joint_bank_account_survivorship_info_sheet`, with § 5042 in
  `joint_tenancy_severance_info_sheet`); the California statutory
  will (§ 6240), the revocable transfer on death deed (§ 5600 et
  seq.), the small-estate affidavit (§ 13100 et seq.), the spousal
  property petition (§ 13650), trustee removal (§ 15642), powers of
  appointment, guardianship nomination for minor children, and the
  trustee's duty to diversify (inside the Uniform Prudent Investor
  Act sheet) — each already has its own document or clause.
- Gifts to interested witnesses (§ 6112) remains a viable future
  topic; it is still mentioned only once, in a no-contest clause.

Chosen topic: the whole of Probate Code Article 10 of Chapter 6 of
Part 4 of Division 4 — "Substituted Judgment," §§ 2580-2586 — the
proceeding by which a probate court authorizes or requires a
conservator of the estate to make gifts, create or fund trusts, change
beneficiary designations, make a will, or make or revoke a transfer on
death deed for a conservatee; together with Prob. Code § 6100 (the
companion will provision) and Prob. Code §§ 1301 and 1310 (appeal and
stay), and the two leading published decisions.

## What this document covers

20 clauses: 2 drafting (declaration, signature block) and 18 authority
clauses, with 41 verified citation quotes drawn from 10 Probate Code
sections (2580, 2581, 2582, 2583, 2584, 2585, 2586, 6100, 1301, 1310)
and two published California Court of Appeal decisions —
Conservatorship of Hart (1991) 228 Cal.App.3d 1244 (filed on
CourtListener under the caption *Wells Fargo Bank v. Keresey*) and
Murphy v. Murphy (2008) 164 Cal.App.4th 376:

- **`subjudg_what_it_is`** (§ 2580(a); Hart at p. 1250; Murphy at
  p. 394) — the three purposes for which the conservator or other
  interested person may petition; the statute permits the court to
  substitute its judgment for the conservatee's; the article codifies
  a common law doctrine.
- **`subjudg_actions_listed`** (§ 2580(b)(1)-(4), summarizing
  (5)-(14)) — the non-exclusive list of fourteen kinds of action.
- **`subjudg_trusts`** (§ 2580(b)(5)-(6); Murphy at p. 408) —
  creating revocable or irrevocable trusts that may extend beyond the
  conservatee's disability or life; the § 3600 special needs trust
  carve-out; transferring unintentionally omitted property into an
  existing trust.
- **`subjudg_revocable_trust_limit`** (§ 2580(b)(11)) — the three
  situations in which the court "shall not" authorize revocation or
  modification of a revocable trust.
- **`subjudg_will_by_conservator`** (§ 2580(b)(13); § 6100) — making
  a will, and § 6100(b)'s preservation of a mentally competent
  conservatee's power to revoke, amend or supersede it.
- **`subjudg_beneficiary_designations_tod`** (§ 2580(b)(8), (14)) —
  insurance, annuity, dividend-plan and retirement-plan rights,
  including changing beneficiaries; and the revocable transfer on
  death deed.
- **`subjudg_notice`** (§ 2581; Hart at p. 1263) — the five
  categories entitled to notice, including testamentary beneficiaries
  and intestate heirs, and the holding that unborn potential
  beneficiaries are not among them.
- **`subjudg_required_determinations`** (§ 2582) — the two
  determinations the court must make before any order.
- **`subjudg_relevant_circumstances`** (§ 2583(a)-(e), (k)-(m); Hart
  at p. 1267) — the directive to consider all relevant circumstances,
  the thirteen enumerated ones, and the narrow reading of the
  subdivision on the conservatee's wishes.
- **`subjudg_weighing_the_circumstances`** (Hart at pp. 1265, 1268;
  Murphy at p. 398) — not every enumerated circumstance is expected
  in every case; the will and estate plan are two circumstances among
  several.
- **`subjudg_prudent_person_standard`** (Hart at pp. 1264, 1270;
  Murphy at p. 383) — what a reasonably prudent person in the
  conservatee's position would do, including on an unopposed
  petition.
- **`subjudg_complete_information`** (Hart at pp. 1254, 1260-1261,
  1266) — the burden of informing the court fully and fairly; the
  better practice of requiring proof even when unopposed; the limit
  Hart placed on its own nondisclosure holding.
- **`subjudg_conservatee_interests`** (Hart at p. 1253) — whom the
  statute protects, and the appointment of a guardian ad litem where
  the stakes are high.
- **`subjudg_after_hearing`** (§ 2584; Murphy at p. 398; Hart at
  p. 1261) — approve, modify and approve, or disapprove; when a
  contested evidentiary hearing is required; a reviewing court will
  not exercise the discretion in the trial court's place.
- **`subjudg_estate_plan_documents`** (§ 2586(a)-(c)) — the
  definition of the conservatee's estate plan, production of those
  documents notwithstanding the lawyer-client privilege, the
  nondisclosure rule, and contempt.
- **`subjudg_no_duty_to_propose`** (§ 2585) — no duty on the
  conservator to propose any action, and no liability for not doing
  so.
- **`subjudg_appeal_and_stay`** (§§ 1301, 1310(a)-(b)) — orders under
  Article 10 are appealable by name; the appeal stays the order; the
  injury-or-loss exception and the validity of acts taken under it.
- **`subjudg_effect_in_later_litigation`** (Murphy at pp. 383, 402,
  408) — a substituted judgment order collaterally estopped a later
  action alleging fraud and undue influence in the instruments it
  authorized.

Every clause states what the law is; none instructs the reader what to
do about any conservatorship, any conservatee, or any proposed action.

## Independent double-fetch

- **Statutes** (leginfo.legislature.ca.gov, via `curl` through the
  environment proxy): all 10 sections were fetched by two independent
  paths — the individual section page
  (`codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=PROB&sectionNum=…`) with a
  Chrome-on-Windows User-Agent, and the page for the whole article or
  chapter containing the section (`codes_displayText.xhtml`: Division
  4, Part 4, Chapter 6, Article 10 for §§ 2580-2586; Division 6,
  Part 1, Chapter 1 for § 6100; Division 3, Part 3, Chapters 1 and 2
  for §§ 1301 and 1310) with a Firefox-on-Linux User-Agent. The
  section text extracted from the two paths was identical for all 10
  sections after non-breaking-space conversion and whitespace
  collapsing (`diff.py` in the session scratchpad: "MATCH" for each).
  Four fetches were reset mid-transfer by the proxy
  (`SSL_ERROR_SYSCALL` / connection reset) and were retried until
  they returned 200.
- **Case law** (CourtListener opinion HTML, via `curl` through the
  environment proxy, with `Referer: https://www.courtlistener.com/`
  and browser Accept headers): each opinion was fetched twice with
  two distinct User-Agent/header sets (Chrome on Windows for the
  first fetch, Safari on macOS with a different Accept-Language for
  the second). The extracted text of the two responses was
  byte-identical for each opinion — Hart SHA-1
  `25c4874d4a82b65815fde37e76c0df2f0d6511bd`, Murphy SHA-1
  `fe9a21f4d8091b65c9fd6d83e0f35ce78aac090e`. No 202 bot-challenge
  body was returned on any attempt.
- **Third, index-level check (ran successfully this time).** A
  cluster-restricted exact-phrase query against CourtListener's
  public search index was run for one quoted phrase from each
  opinion: `cluster_id:2179927 AND "In essence the statute permits
  the court to substitute its judgment for that of a conservatee"`
  returned `count: 1` (Wells Fargo Bank v. Keresey), and
  `cluster_id:2252916 AND "barred by principles of collateral
  estoppel"` returned `count: 1` (Murphy v. Murphy). The two queries
  were spaced 25 seconds apart to stay inside the shared rate limit,
  and no 429 was returned.
- **Second database not available.** Justia, FindLaw, Google Scholar
  and case.law are egress-blocked from this environment, and no
  slip-opinion PDF exists in CourtListener storage for a 1991 or 2008
  Court of Appeal opinion, so the two HTML fetches of each opinion
  are of the same underlying record. This limitation is disclosed in
  the `gap` field of every clause that quotes an opinion.
- **No CourtListener MCP call was made.** All case work went through
  `curl` to the public JSON search endpoint and to opinion HTML
  pages, so the shared MCP quota was not consumed by this task.

## Quote-by-quote verification

All 41 citation quotes were re-checked **after** the merge, reading
them back out of `data/clauses.json` and matching them against the
fetched sources (`reverify.py`). Matching normalizes only benign
artifacts — curly quotation marks and apostrophes to straight ones,
en/em dashes to hyphens, non-breaking spaces to spaces, and runs of
whitespace (including leginfo's and CourtListener's line wrapping) to
a single space.

- Against the **primary** fetch path: **ok=41, bad=0**.
- Against the **second, independent** fetch path (article/chapter
  pages for the statutes, second header set for the opinions):
  **ok=41, bad=0**.

Each stored quote is the exact source substring with the source's own
characters preserved (leginfo and CourtListener both use U+2019
apostrophes and U+201C/U+201D quotation marks, and those are kept in
the stored quotes); only line-wrap whitespace was collapsed. No quote
was edited, and no defect required correction.

Artifacts identified and handled rather than silently normalized:

- CourtListener's copies of both opinions are scans with
  optical-character-recognition defects elsewhere in each document
  (Hart renders "affirm" as "aifirm", "effect" as "elfect", "(Id." as
  "{Id." and hyphenates "superi- or"; Murphy renders "nunc pro tunc"
  as "nunc pro tone", "modern" as "modem" and single quotation marks
  as "£"). Every quoted passage was checked character by character
  and none contains such a defect; passages that do contain one —
  including Hart's sentence about express findings under § 2583 —
  were deliberately not quoted. This is disclosed in the `gap` of
  each clause that quotes an opinion.
- Italicized words inside two quoted passages (Hart's "beneficiaries"
  and "potential" at p. 1263; case names in surrounding text) appear
  on their own lines in CourtListener's markup; the stored quotes
  restore ordinary sentence spacing and change nothing else.
  Disclosed in the `gap` of `subjudg_notice`.
- **Section 2581 contains what looks like a typographical error in
  the official text as posted**: its opening sentence reads "in the
  manner provided in Chapter 3 (commencing with Section 1460) or
  Part 1", where subdivision (a) of the same section and the
  published opinions read "of Part 1". Both independent fetches
  return "or", so the quote reproduces leginfo's text rather than
  correcting it, and the discrepancy is disclosed in the `gap` of
  `subjudg_notice`.
- Pin cites are derived from the star-pagination markers embedded in
  CourtListener's text. The two Murphy passages that precede the
  first marker (`*384`) are cited to p. 383 on that basis; this is
  disclosed in the `gap` of the clauses that use them.
- Historical limits disclosed rather than papered over: Hart (1991)
  described Conservatorship of Romo (1987) 190 Cal.App.3d 279 as
  having narrowly held that the substituted-judgment statute did not
  then authorize a conservator to write a will, while the current
  § 2580(b)(13) lists "Making a will" and § 6100(b) authorizes it;
  the sheet quotes only the sections currently in force and states in
  the `gap` of `subjudg_will_by_conservator` that the legislative
  history of those provisions was not traced. Likewise, Hart's
  discussion of a stay pending appeal rested on former Prob. Code
  § 2751, a section that no longer exists in the code (leginfo
  returns an empty section page for it); the sheet quotes only
  §§ 1301 and 1310 and discloses this in the `gap` of
  `subjudg_appeal_and_stay`. Hart's reference to "11 categories" in
  § 2583, which now lists thirteen, is disclosed in the `gap` of
  `subjudg_relevant_circumstances`.
- Sections referred to but not fetched or quoted — Prob. Code
  §§ 1460-1469 (notice period), 2423, 3600 et seq., 13502, 13503,
  Part 15 (commencing with § 700) of Division 2, Evid. Code § 950 et
  seq., and Code Civ. Proc. § 373.5 — are named as the quoted text
  names them, and each omission is disclosed in the relevant clause's
  `gap`.
