## What this document covers

13 clauses — 3 drafting (purpose/scope, relationship to the project's
other IP documents, preparation/acknowledgment) and 10 authority clauses
citing 17 U.S.C. §§ 101, 102(a), 113(a)-(c), 1301(a)(1)-(2), 35 U.S.C.
§ 171(a), and three decisions: Star Athletica, L.L.C. v. Varsity Brands,
Inc., 580 U.S. 405 (2017); Mazer v. Stein, 347 U.S. 201 (1954); and
Inhale, Inc. v. Starbuzz Tobacco, Inc., 755 F.3d 1038 (9th Cir. 2014).
Document id `useful_article_separability_info_sheet`; clause prefix
`uas_`; fields `partyName`, `articleDescription`, `preparerName`,
`dateSigned`.

- **`uas_statutory_framework`** (§ 102(a), § 102(a)(5), § 101) — the
  pictorial, graphic, and sculptural works category, the "works of
  artistic craftsmanship … form but not their mechanical or utilitarian
  aspects" sentence, and the separability proviso, including the two
  textual features that drive the case law ("only if, and only to the
  extent that" and the conjunctive pair of conditions).
- **`uas_useful_article_definition`** (§ 101; Star Athletica) — the
  intrinsic-utilitarian-function definition, the rule that a part
  normally belonging to a useful article is itself one, the Court's
  statement that the statute does not protect useful articles as such,
  and the replica corollary (a cardboard model of a car may be
  copyrightable but yields no rights in the car).
- **`uas_star_athletica_two_part_test`** (Star Athletica) — the holding
  as restated in Part III, the "not onerous" separate-identification
  step, the harder independent-existence step, the consequence for a
  feature that cannot exist separately, and the counterfactual framing
  of the ultimate question.
- **`uas_physical_conceptual_distinction_abandoned`** (Star Athletica) —
  why the useful article need not remain after imaginative separation,
  and the express abandonment of the physical/conceptual distinction.
- **`uas_scope_of_what_separability_protects`** (Star Athletica) — no
  right to prohibit a uniform of identical shape, cut, and dimensions;
  the test does not reach shape, cut, or physical dimensions; and
  two-dimensional applied art may track the contours of the article.
- **`uas_rejected_considerations`** (Star Athletica) — the designer's
  artistic judgment and the marketability of the feature both rejected
  as ungrounded in the text; perception, not design process, controls.
- **`uas_section_113_rights_in_applied_works`** (§ 113(a)-(c)) —
  reproducing a protected work on any kind of article, the frozen
  pre-1978 rule for works portraying useful articles, and the
  advertising/commentary/news-report allowance for photographs.
- **`uas_mazer_mass_production`** (Mazer) — intended use or use in
  industry does not bar or invalidate copyright, and the "dichotomy … not
  beauty and utility but art for the copyright" framing of the
  copyright/design-patent line.
- **`uas_other_design_protection_regimes`** (35 U.S.C. § 171(a);
  17 U.S.C. § 1301(a)(1)-(2); Star Athletica) — design patents, the
  mask work chapter, the vessel-hull chapter, and the Court's statement
  that design patent and copyright are not mutually exclusive.
- **`uas_ninth_circuit_container_shape`** (Inhale) — the Ninth Circuit
  rule that binds federal district courts in California: distinctiveness
  of shape does not affect separability, a container's shape is not
  independent of its containing function insofar as the shape
  accomplishes it, and any part of a container that merely accomplishes
  the containing is not copyrightable.

## Verification and findings

- **Every statute was fetched twice, from two publishers.** The Office
  of the Law Revision Counsel (`uscode.house.gov`, prelim edition, whose
  retrieved pages state text "in effect on September 12, 2026") and,
  independently, Cornell LII (`law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/17/101`,
  `/102`, `/113`, `/1301`, and `/uscode/text/35/171`), each under a
  distinct User-Agent. The two renderings of every quoted sentence are
  word-for-word identical; they differ only in typography (Cornell uses
  curly quotation marks and em dashes in subsection headings where the
  Law Revision Counsel uses straight marks and hyphens). Quotes
  reproduce the Law Revision Counsel characters.
- **All 31 `citations[].quote` values in the merged document were
  re-verified by substring match against the fetched sources after the
  merge; 0 failures.** The check reads the quotes back out of
  `data/clauses.json`, not out of the drafting notes.
- **Star Athletica was read in two independent renderings.** The
  CourtListener HTML opinion page (cluster 4377349, HTTP 200 with a
  browser User-Agent and `Referer: https://www.courtlistener.com/`) and
  the Supreme Court's own slip opinion PDF,
  `supremecourt.gov/opinions/16pdf/15-866_0971.pdf` (51 pages, text
  extracted with pypdf). The quotes follow the CourtListener rendering
  at the cited URL; each Star Athletica clause's `gap` says so and
  records that the slip opinion uses em dashes and line-break
  hyphenation where CourtListener uses hyphens.
- **The slip-opinion comparison was done hyphenation- and
  whitespace-insensitively, and the two sentences that failed a literal
  match were resolved rather than dropped.** The PDF text layer breaks
  words across lines ("protecta-/ble") and inserts intra-word spaces
  ("indu strial", "us eful"); two candidate quotes additionally spanned
  a page break, so the running header "STAR ATHLETICA, L. L. C. v.
  VARSITY BRANDS, INC. / Opinion of the Court" fell inside them. Those
  two were verified in halves against the PDF (each half matching
  exactly) and one of them — the Part II holding — was then replaced in
  the document by the Court's own restatement of the same holding in
  Part III, which sits on a single page and matches both sources
  literally. No quote text was altered to force a match.
- **A superseded opinion was caught by the double-fetch.** Inhale exists
  on CourtListener as both the original opinion, 739 F.3d 446 (cluster
  2648678), and the amended opinion that supersedes it, 755 F.3d 1038
  (cluster 8441795). The sentence quoted in this document differs
  between them: the original reads "**because** the shape accomplishes
  the function" and the amended reads "**insofar as** the shape
  accomplishes the function," and the amended opinion adds the summary
  paragraph ("any part of a container that merely accomplishes the
  containing is not copyrightable") that the original lacks. The
  document quotes the amended opinion only, cites it as 755 F.3d 1038,
  and `uas_ninth_circuit_container_shape`'s `gap` states the difference
  and the reason.
- **All three Inhale quotes were phrase-confirmed** through
  CourtListener's public search index with
  `cluster_id:8441795 AND "<exact phrase>"`; each returned `count=1`

- **Case location used no CourtListener MCP quota.** The public,

  Exactly one MCP call was made in this task, for Mazer, for the reason

## Honest gaps disclosed

- **Mazer could not be fetched from its CourtListener opinion page.**
  `/opinion/105200/mazer-v-stein/` and the lead-opinion page
  `/opinion/9421048/…` returned an empty HTTP 202 on three attempts with
  three different browser header sets (including full `Sec-Fetch-*` and
  `sec-ch-ua` headers), while Star Athletica and Inhale returned 200 with
  the same technique. The Library of Congress United States Reports PDF
  (`tile.loc.gov/…/usrep347201.pdf`), `openjurist.org`, and
  `www.copyright.gov` are all refused by the egress proxy at CONNECT
  (403), as is `cdn.ca9.uscourts.gov`; Justia, FindLaw, Google Scholar
  and case.law are blocked by the task rules. Both Mazer quotes were
  therefore verified through two independent CourtListener interfaces:
  (a) exact-phrase queries against the public search index restricted to
  `cluster_id:105200`, each returning `count=1`, and (b) one
  `search_document` MCP call that returned the surrounding text of the
  lead opinion (opinion 9421048) containing both sentences. The
  `uas_mazer_mass_production` gap states this limitation in full.
- **Mazer is a 1909 Act decision.** The separability language now in
  § 101 was enacted in 1976. The clause says what Mazer held about
  industrial use and how Star Athletica treats it; it does not claim
  that every part of the 1954 reasoning survives.
- **Inhale predates Star Athletica** and uses the conceptual-separability
  vocabulary the Supreme Court later abandoned. The clause body and gap
  say so and do not assert that its reasoning survives in every respect;
  no post-2017 Ninth Circuit separability decision was located in the
  research for this document, and none is cited.
- **Copyright Office materials were not retrieved.** The 2008 opinion
  letter and Compendium II § 505.03 that Inhale found persuasive, and
  the current Compendium (Third) chapter 900, are not quoted beyond what
  appears inside the Inhale opinion; `copyright.gov` is unreachable from
  this environment. Two gaps say so.
- **Ets-Hokin v. Skyy Spirits, Inc. is described only as Inhale
  describes it**, was not separately retrieved, and is not quoted; the
  gap notes that this project's unprotectable-elements document cites
  the same case for different propositions.
- **Bleistein v. Donaldson Lithographing Co. was not retrieved** and is
  not quoted; it appears only as the authority the Star Athletica opinion
  cited when rejecting the marketability factor, and the
  `uas_rejected_considerations` gap says so.
- **Questions the document does not answer, each named in the relevant
  gap**: how the threshold question whether an object is a useful article
  is resolved in a contested case (Star Athletica addressed separability,
  and Justice Ginsburg's separate opinion would not have reached the test
  at all); what "utilitarian aspects" means; how the test applies to
  three-dimensional shaping, to color, and to features serving both
  decorative and functional ends; what pre-1978 law § 113(b) freezes;
  and how overlapping design patent and copyright claims interact.
- **Scope.** The document addresses copyrightable subject matter for the
  design of useful articles only. Originality, the § 102(b) exclusions,
  merger and scenes a faire, registration, fair use, first sale, joint
  authorship, preemption, moral rights, design patent standards, trade
  dress, and mask work protection are each left to the sibling document
  that covers them, and a dedicated drafting clause places each one.

  no duplicate clause or document ids; every `clauseOrder` id resolves;
  every `{{placeholder}}` in the new clauses resolves to a declared field
  and every declared field is used; the new authority clauses use the
  exact key order `id,title,kind,status,checkedDate,body,gap,citations`
  with a non-empty `gap` and citation keys `case,cite,url,quote`, and the
  drafting clauses use exactly `id,title,kind,body`. Totals after the
