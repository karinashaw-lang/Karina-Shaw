# Real Estate, new document: Private Nuisance Between Neighbors — Information Sheet

## Why this document

Real Estate slot of wave 168 (the round-robin's Confidentiality & IP /
Ending employment / Business Formation / Real Estate rotation). The
corpus already held 87 Real Estate documents, and the obvious
neighbor-dispute angles were taken: boundary trees (Civ. Code §§ 833,
834; *Booska v. Patel*), the Good Neighbor Fence Act, the Solar Shade
Control Act, boundary line agreements, prescriptive easements and
easements by necessity, adverse possession, and quiet title. Several
candidate topics suggested for this wave were ruled out by grep as
already covered — the homestead exemption (a full Estate Planning
document, `homestead_exemption_info_sheet`, on Code Civ. Proc.
§ 704.710 et seq.), agency disclosure in residential sales
(`real_estate_agency_disclosure_info_sheet`), mechanics lien procedure
(preliminary notice and waiver/release documents), and easement by
necessity.

What was genuinely open was **general private nuisance law** — the
body of doctrine every one of those specific neighbor statutes sits
on top of. Corpus greps against the pre-merge files returned zero hits
for "3479," "3480," "3483," "3484," "3493," "Covalt," and
"Burbank-Glendale." The apparent hits were checked one by one and are
false positives or bare cross-references: every "3501," "3502," and
"3503" hit is to a different code (Corp. Code §§ 3501-3503, Prob. Code
§§ 13502-13503) or to § 23501; the single "3482" hit is to Civ. Code
§ 3482.5, the right-to-farm section, named in a document description;
and the four "3481" hits are all in the Solar Shade Control Act sheet,
which notes that Pub. Res. Code § 25982 declares a violation a private
nuisance "as defined in Section 3481 of the Civil Code" — a
cross-reference inside one specific statutory scheme, not a statement
of general nuisance law. The 31 pre-existing hits on the word
"nuisance" all traced
to other documents using the term in passing (just-cause eviction
grounds, the Solar Shade Control Act's private-nuisance designation,
boundary trees, mobilehome termination grounds), and none of them
states the general definition, the two extra elements, the statutory
remedies, or the continuing/permanent distinction.

## What this document covers

12 clauses: 2 drafting (purpose/parties, factual record) and 10
authority clauses, with 20 verified citations drawn from 12 statutory
sections and 2 published California Supreme Court opinions.

- **`privnuis_statutory_definition`** — Civ. Code § 3479's categorical
  definition of nuisance, including the 1996 addition of the illegal
  sale of controlled substances.
- **`privnuis_public_versus_private`** — § 3480 (public nuisance
  defined by breadth of effect), § 3481 (private nuisance defined
  residually), § 3493 (a private person's action for a public nuisance
  specially injurious to that person).
- **`privnuis_two_extra_elements_substantial`** — *San Diego Gas &
  Electric Co. v. Superior Court* (1996) 13 Cal.4th 893 on private
  nuisance as a nontrespassory interference, the two elements beyond
  interference, and the objective "persons of normal health and
  sensibilities" standard for substantiality.
- **`privnuis_two_extra_elements_unreasonable`** — the same opinion's
  gravity-of-harm-versus-social-utility test for unreasonableness.
- **`privnuis_remedies_civil_action`** — Civ. Code § 3501's two
  remedies and Code Civ. Proc. § 731 on who may sue and what the
  judgment may do.
- **`privnuis_self_help_abatement`** — §§ 3502 and 3503, and the
  breach-of-the-peace, unnecessary-injury, and notice limits written
  into them.
- **`privnuis_successive_owner_liability`** — § 3483.
- **`privnuis_abatement_does_not_bar_damages`** — § 3484.
- **`privnuis_statutory_authority_limit`** — § 3482.
- **`privnuis_continuing_versus_permanent`** — *Baker v.
  Burbank-Glendale-Pasadena Airport Authority* (1985) 39 Cal.3d 862 on
  the continuing/permanent classification, successive actions, and the
  one-action rule, with Code Civ. Proc. § 338(b)'s three-year period
  for an action for trespass upon or injury to real property.

## Genuine findings

- **A source rendering artifact disclosed rather than smoothed over**:
  leginfo displays Civil Code § 3480's section number inside square
  brackets, as `[3480.]`, and carries the note "(Amended by Stats.
  2025, Ch. 67, Sec. 36. (AB 1170) Effective January 1, 2026.)" The
  `privnuis_public_versus_private` gap reports both facts as displayed,
  states that the bracketed number is not part of the quoted text, and
  does not speculate about why the source renders it that way or
  compare the text against the pre-2026 version.
- **A disclosed line-wrap defect corrected in a quote**: the fetched
  Code Civ. Proc. § 731 page contains a raw newline mid-sentence
  between "Section 3480" and "of the Civil Code." The quotation
  normalizes it to a single space, and the `privnuis_remedies_civil_action`
  gap says so. The same gap discloses that § 3501's three source
  paragraphs are reproduced as three lines with the non-breaking
  spaces after the numerals normalized.
- **A disclosed secondary-source chain, not treated as independent
  corroboration**: both *San Diego Gas & Electric* element clauses
  note that the Supreme Court's "substantial actual damage," objective-
  standard, and gravity-versus-utility formulations are drawn from and
  cite the Restatement Second of Torts, Prosser & Keeton, and Harper
  et al.; none of those secondary authorities was fetched, so the
  clauses rely only on the opinion's own text and do not enumerate the
  Restatement factors the opinion merely cross-references.
- **A disclosed holding-versus-dictum posture**: *San Diego Gas &
  Electric* resolved the case on the Public Utilities Commission's
  exclusive jurisdiction, so its elements discussion is its statement
  of governing law rather than the ground of decision, and it
  expressly declined to decide whether fear of future harm supports a
  private nuisance claim. *Baker* was decided over a dissent, arose
  from a public entity's airport operations (with a shorter claim-
  presentation period under Gov. Code § 911.2 and federal limits on
  enjoining flights), and the quotes used are from the majority only.

## Honest gap(s) disclosed

This document states no numeric threshold for substantiality, does not
enumerate the Restatement's unreasonableness factors, does not report
how any court has construed the "express authority of a statute" limit
in § 3482 (no such case was fetched), does not define "breach of the
peace," "unnecessary injury," or "reasonable notice" under §§ 3502-3503,
does not resolve which limitations statute governs any particular
nuisance claim, and does not evaluate whether any specific condition,
activity, or claim is a nuisance, is substantial, is unreasonable, or
is continuing rather than permanent. Two sections quoted here (§ 3493,
§ 3503) retain gender-specific 1872 pronouns, which is disclosed in the
relevant gaps.

## Method

**Statutes.** Civ. Code §§ 3479, 3480, 3481, 3482, 3483, 3484, 3493,
3501, 3502, 3503 and Code Civ. Proc. §§ 731 and 338 were each fetched
twice from leginfo.legislature.ca.gov by curl through the environment
proxy with two distinct browser User-Agent strings (Chrome/Windows and
Firefox/Linux) and different Accept headers. Each section's extracted
text was compared across the two fetches; all twelve pairs were
identical.

**Case law.** The CourtListener MCP tools were not used at all — the
shared quota was already exhausted (the public search API returned
`Request was throttled. Rate limit exceeded: 125/day`). Both opinions
were instead reached through CourtListener's citation-lookup URLs,
which are ordinary HTML pages outside the API quota:
`/c/Cal.%204th/13/893/` redirected to
`/opinion/5607852/san-diego-gas-electric-co-v-superior-court/` and
`/c/Cal.%203d/39/862/` redirected to
`/opinion/1115893/baker-v-burbank-glendale-pasadena-airport-authority/`,
each fetched with a real browser User-Agent, `Referer:
https://www.courtlistener.com/`, and full Accept headers (HTTP 200 both
times). Each opinion was then fetched a second time, directly at its
resolved opinion URL and with a different User-Agent (Safari/macOS) and
Accept-Language header; the extracted text of the two fetches was
byte-identical for both cases.

**Quote verification.** A script re-extracted all twelve statutory
pages and both opinion texts and checked every one of the document's
20 `citations[].quote` values, whitespace- and nbsp-normalized, against
the fetched sources. All 20 matched verbatim; the only normalizations
applied are the two disclosed in the gaps above.

**Corpus validation.** Both data files parse; all clause ids and
document ids are unique; every `clauseOrder` entry resolves to an
existing clause; every `{{placeholder}}` in the new clauses' bodies and
gaps resolves to a declared field. Authority clauses use the exact
key order `id, title, kind, status, checkedDate, body, gap, citations`
with non-empty gaps, drafting clauses use exactly `id, title, kind,
body`, and the document uses `id, title, description, categories,
clauseOrder, fields`. Corpus after merge: 689 documents, 5,858
clauses, 88 Real Estate documents.
