## Genuine findings

- **The applicant-facing wording is statutory, not inferred.** Three
  separate provisions name applicants in their own text: § 12940(m)(1)
  ("an applicant or employee"), § 12940(n) ("the employee or
  applicant," twice), and § 12940(e)(2) ("an applicant's request for
  reasonable accommodation"). § 12926(f) reaches the position the
  individual "holds or desires." Those are the sentences quoted for
  the proposition that these duties operate before hire.
- **Green's conclusion, not its facts, is what reaches hiring.** Green
  was an employee denied return to work, not an applicant; but the
  court's concluding sentence assigns the plaintiff the burden of
  showing qualification "for the position sought or held." The clause
  quotes that sentence and the clause's gap says plainly that Green was
  not a hiring case.
- **Gelfo and Green point in different directions on one point, and
  the sheet says so instead of harmonizing them.** Gelfo's footnote 12
  states that ability to perform "is not part of a prosecution based on
  a perceived disability" and that the burden of showing inability
  belongs on the employer; Green, decided a year later, places the
  burden of proving ability on the plaintiff. The Gelfo clause's gap
  discloses the tension and declines to resolve it. (Footnote 12 is
  described, not quoted as a holding, because the CourtListener layout
  places footnotes after the opinion text, where reporter page
  attribution is unreliable.)
- **A subdivision has been relettered since Scotch was decided.**
  Scotch cites the reasonable-accommodation examples as "§ 12926, subd.
  (n)"; the identical text appears at § 12926(p) in the version
  published as of the checked date. The clause quotes the current
  statutory text rather than Scotch's reproduction of it, and the gap
  discloses the reletter.
- **Scotch resolved a Court of Appeal split, and the clause says what
  it resolved.** Wysinger and Claudio had allowed a § 12940(n) claim
  without proof of an available accommodation; Nadaf-Rahrov required
  one. Scotch reconciled them by requiring the claimant to identify, in
  litigation with full discovery, an accommodation that would have been
  available. The clause quotes both halves of that reconciliation.
- **Green was 4-3.** Chief Justice George and Justices Baxter and
  Corrigan concurred in Justice Chin's opinion; Justices Kennard and
  Moreno concurred in Justice Werdegar's dissent. The dissent is quoted
  once, expressly labeled as a dissent in the citation's `case` field
  and in the gap, to show the question was contested.

## Method

**Statutes — two independent fetches by different URL paths.**
Gov. Code § 12940 and § 12926 were each fetched twice through the
environment proxy with different User-Agent strings AND different
endpoints on leginfo.legislature.ca.gov:

- Fetch A: `codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=GOV&sectionNum=12940.`
  and `...&sectionNum=12926.` (Chrome/Windows UA), HTTP 200.
- Fetch B: `codes_displayText.xhtml?lawCode=GOV&division=3.&title=2.&
  part=2.8.&chapter=6.&article=1.` (the whole article containing
  § 12940) and `...&chapter=4.` (FEHA Chapter 4, Definitions,
  §§ 12925-12928, containing § 12926), Safari/macOS UA,
  `Accept-Language: en-GB`, HTTP 200.

Two earlier attempts at fetch B for § 12926 (chapter 1 and chapter 2)
returned the wrong chapters (General Provisions; Bureau for Descendants
of American Slavery) and were discarded before any quote was accepted;
chapter 4 is the one that contains the definitions. Every statutory
quote was then checked against both fetches after normalizing
non-breaking spaces and the site's line-wrap whitespace (leginfo wraps
long paragraphs with a newline plus tabs; fetch B renders the same text
without spaces after some colons). No quote differed between the two
fetches in anything but that whitespace.

**Case law — no CourtListener MCP calls were made at all.** Cluster ids
were located through the public unauthenticated v4 search JSON
(`/api/rest/v4/search/?type=o&q=...`) reachable via curl through the
proxy, with pauses between queries: Scotch 2245296 (phrase query),
Gelfo 2304510 (phrase query), Green 2518894 (located by
`citation:"42 Cal. 4th 254"` after a case-name query returned unrelated
results). Each opinion's HTML page was then fetched **twice** with two
distinct full browser header sets — A: Chrome/Windows UA,
`Accept-Language: en-US`, and B: Safari/macOS UA,
`Accept-Language: en-GB`, both with `Referer:
https://www.courtlistener.com/` and a browser `Accept` header — for six
fetches in all, every one returning HTTP 200. The extracted opinion
text from the A and B fetches is byte-identical for all three opinions
(Scotch 79,889 chars; Gelfo 76,269; Green 58,487), which is the
expected result for two header-distinguished fetches of the same
static page and is recorded here rather than presented as two
independent transcriptions. No slip-opinion PDF is available for these
1990s-2000s California appellate decisions on
storage.courtlistener.com, and Justia, FindLaw, Google Scholar, and
case.law are egress-blocked from this environment, so CourtListener's
scanned reporter text is the only reachable full text; that limitation
is noted here rather than worked around silently. Canonical opinion
URLs used in the citations were taken from the slug links embedded in
the fetched pages themselves.

**Quote verification.** A script (`verify.py`) re-read all five source
texts (both fetches each), normalized ` ` and collapsed runs of
whitespace, and asserted that every one of the 51 `citations[].quote`
strings appears verbatim in **both** fetches of its source: 51/51
passed, 0 failures. Three candidate quotes were rejected and rewritten
during drafting because they did not match exactly — a Scotch sentence
that begins with the bracketed "[T]he" in the reporter, a phrase about
not being required to create a new position that appears only as the
court's paraphrase, and a § 12926 passage whose subdivision letter had
changed.

**Schema and corpus validation.** Authority clauses use exactly
`{id,title,kind,status,checkedDate,body,gap,citations}` in that order
with a non-empty `gap`; drafting clauses use exactly
`{id,title,kind,body}`; every citation uses exactly
`{case,cite,url,quote}`; the document uses exactly
`{id,title,description,categories,clauseOrder,fields}`. After the
merge, both files parse; clause ids are unique (5,597 total, 11 new);
document ids are unique (669 total); every `clauseOrder` entry across
all 669 documents resolves to an existing clause; and every
`{{placeholder}}` in every clause referenced by a document resolves to
a field that document declares (0 unresolved). A byte-level check
confirmed the merge is append-only: the first 5,586 clauses and first
668 documents are unchanged, and `git diff --stat` shows 465
insertions and 0 deletions.
