# Confidentiality & IP, new document: Copyright Termination Right — Information Sheet

## Why this document

This topic was previously only a brief mention inside an existing
clause, `copyrightassign_termination_right`, in the corpus's existing
Copyright Assignment Agreement document. This document independently
re-verifies 17 U.S.C. § 203 from primary sources and genuinely deepens
coverage with an entire second termination pathway for pre-1978 grants
(17 U.S.C. § 304(c)/(d)), the regulatory notice-content layer (37 C.F.R.
§ 201.10), granular who-may-exercise detail, and effect-of-termination
mechanics — none of which the existing passing-mention clause covers.
Third document of wave 54.

## What this document covers

8 clauses: 2 drafting (declaration, signature block) and 6 authority
clauses, citing 17 U.S.C. §§ 203 and 304, and 37 C.F.R. § 201.10:

- **`copyrightterm_scope_and_override`** (§ 203(a), (a)(5)) — the
  termination right and its override of contrary agreements, including
  waivers written into the original grant.
- **`copyrightterm_work_for_hire_exception`** (§ 203(a), § 304(c)) — the
  work-made-for-hire exclusion, confirmed independently in both
  termination pathways.
- **`copyrightterm_who_may_exercise`** (§ 203(a)(1)-(2)(A), corroborated
  by § 304(c)(2)(A)) — single-author termination and the statutory
  spousal/children termination-interest allocation.
- **`copyrightterm_window_timing_two_pathways`** (§§ 203(a)(3),
  304(c)(3), 304(d)(2)) — the post-1978 35/40-year window and the
  separate pre-1978 56-year and 75-year windows created by the 1998
  Sonny Bono Act amendment.
- **`copyrightterm_notice_requirements`** (§ 203(a)(4)(B), 37 C.F.R.
  § 201.10) — the 2-to-10-year advance notice timing, Copyright Office
  recordation, and the regulation's required notice content.
- **`copyrightterm_effect_of_termination`** (§ 203(b), (b)(1),
  corroborated by § 304(c)(6)(A)) — reversion of rights and the
  derivative-work carve-out.

## Genuine corrections and findings

- **A precise two-pathway structure, not one general rule**: post-1978
  grants use the 35/40-year window under § 203; pre-1978 renewal-term
  grants use a separate 56-year window under § 304(c), plus a later
  75-year window under § 304(d) created by the 1998 Sonny Bono Copyright
  Term Extension Act for rights that had already expired unused.
- **The work-for-hire exception is independently confirmed in both
  pathways**, not assumed to carry over from one section to the other.
- **The spousal-share termination-interest rule is a termination-
  specific allocation formula**, not a general inheritance default —
  independently corroborated by identical operative language in the
  separate § 304(c)(2)(A) pathway.
- **A precise notice mechanism**: written notice served 2-10 years
  before the chosen effective date, falling within the applicable
  window, plus mandatory Copyright Office recordation before the
  effective date — with the regulation's own required-content list
  (37 C.F.R. § 201.10(b)(2)) distinguished from its separate treatment
  of § 304(c)/(d) notices, not conflated.
- **Reversion is not absolute**: a derivative work already prepared
  under the grant continues to be usable after termination, but the
  carve-out does not extend to new derivative works — confirmed by
  identical operative language in § 304(c)(6)(A).

## Honest gap disclosed

The further per-stirpes subdivision among grandchildren, the rule for
when no spouse/children/grandchildren survive, and the joint-work
majority-action rule are not restated in full. The regulation's content
requirements specific to § 304(c)/(d) notices, its permitted service
methods, and harmless-error rules were not independently detailed. The
further-grant restrictions of § 203(b)(3)-(4) are not restated in full.
