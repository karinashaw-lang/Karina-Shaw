# Ending employment, new document: Punitive Damages Against an Employer in a Wrongful-Termination Case (Civ. Code §§ 3294, 3295; Gov. Code § 818; White v. Ultramar; College Hospital; Adams v. Murakami; Roby v. McKesson) — Information Sheet

## Why this document

Processed item of wave 162 (Ending employment slot, continuing the
round-robin with waves 146/148/150/152/154/156/158/160). Read all 84
existing "Ending employment" document titles and ids in
`data/documents.json` before choosing a topic, then grepped both data
files for every candidate the brief named. Results: FEHA
administrative exhaustion and the right-to-sue notice are already
covered (`crd_feha_administrative_complaint_info_sheet` — "12960" and
"12965" both hit there); the McDonnell Douglas framework is described
inside `mixed_motive_same_decision_defense_harris_info_sheet` (wave
160); "Lawson" hits only Lawson v. FMR LLC (the federal SOX decision);
"1102.6" hits only Civil Code § 1102.6 in the real-estate transfer
disclosure sheet.

The chosen topic — when punitive (exemplary) damages may be assessed
against an *employer* on account of a termination — was confirmed
open: "White v. Ultramar", "Ultramar", "College Hospital", "Adams v.
Murakami", "Roby v. McKesson", "same-actor", "Horn v. Cushman" and
"3295" each returned **zero** hits in both data files. The one
pre-existing § 3294 clause in the corpus is
`fraudhire_exemplary_damages_civ_3294`, a single clause of the
*Hiring*-category `fraudulent_inducement_of_employment_info_sheet`; it
quotes § 3294(a), (b) and (c)(3) for a recruiting-fraud claim and its
own gap field says in terms: "The section does not define 'managing
agent'; the case law construing that term is not covered here." This
document is the Ending-employment treatment that fills exactly that
hole, and the relationship is stated in the new document's
description. Nothing else in the corpus addresses § 3294(b)'s employer
limit, § 3295's procedure, the financial-condition requirement, or the
constitutional ratio.

The topic sits alongside the corpus's existing "was the discharge
wrongful" sheets (public policy/Tameny, at-will and Guz, mixed motive
and Harris) and its damages sheet (Parker mitigation): those say when
an employer may be liable and how compensatory damages are measured;
none says when the employer may be *punished*.

## Genuine findings

- **OCR artifacts in the CourtListener text of White.** The opinion as
  served carries scanner artifacts: "ultimatély" at p. 566,
  "purposes'because" at p. 574, and "Califomia" in the amicus passage.
  In addition, the sentence at pp. 566-567 in which the Court first
  states its holding is interrupted mid-word by a star-pagination
  marker ("in their *567corporate decisionmaking"). Every sentence
  carrying an artifact was avoided; the holding is quoted instead from
  its artifact-free formulations at pp. 573 and 577. Disclosed in the
  clause's gap field.
- **OCR artifact in Roby.** Roby's own quotation of White renders the
  bracketed verb as "exercise^] substantial discretionary authority",
  so that sentence is not quoted; the underlying formulation is quoted
  directly from White in the preceding clause. Disclosed in the gap.
- **OCR artifact in College Hospital.** The summary sentence for
  § 3294(c) renders a bracketed word as "injurious]", so that sentence
  is not quoted. Disclosed in the gap.
- **Pin cites audited against the `*NNN` markers** in each
  CourtListener page by a script (`pins.py`) that maps each normalized
  match position back to the raw text and reports the nearest
  preceding marker, rather than by eye. Every quote lies entirely
  within one printed page (no quote straddles a marker). White: 566,
  567, 569, 572, 573, 575, 577; College Hospital: 713, 723, 725, 726;
  Roby: 712, 713, 714, 715, 719; Adams: 109, 110, 112.
- **Statute currency.** Civ. Code § 3294 is shown by leginfo as
  amended by Stats. 1992, Ch. 178, § 5, effective January 1, 1993;
  Civ. Code § 3295 as amended by Stats. 1987, Ch. 1498, § 6; Gov. Code
  § 818 as added by Stats. 1963, Ch. 1681. The text quoted is the
  current text, which is also the text White (1999), College Hospital
  (1994) and Roby (2009) construed for the subdivisions at issue.
- **Nested quotation in Roby.** The three guideposts and the five
  reprehensibility factors are the United States Supreme Court's words
  in State Farm Mut. Automobile Ins. Co. v. Campbell (2003) 538 U.S.
  408, quoted here only as they appear inside the Roby opinion; State
  Farm, BMW v. Gore, and Simon v. San Paolo were not fetched. The
  five-factor quote begins after Roby's bracketed "[1]" and therefore
  starts mid-sentence; it is quoted from "the harm caused was
  physical" onward. Disclosed in the gap.
- **College Hospital's posture.** The decision arose on a Code Civ.
  Proc. § 425.13(a) motion for leave to plead punitive damages against
  a health care provider — a procedure that does not apply to
  employers generally. Its § 3294(b) and (c) constructions are quoted
  for those constructions only, and the posture is disclosed in both
  clauses that rely on it.

## Method

**Statutes.** Civ. Code §§ 3294 and 3295 and Gov. Code § 818 were each
fetched twice through two *different leginfo endpoints* with two
different User-Agent strings, via curl through the environment proxy:
path A `codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=…&sectionNum=…` (Chrome/macOS
UA), path B `codes_displayText.xhtml` for the containing article
(Civ. Code div. 4, pt. 1, tit. 2, ch. 1, art. 3 — "Exemplary Damages";
Gov. Code tit. 1, div. 3.6, pt. 2, ch. 1, art. 2) with a Firefox/Linux
UA. All three returned HTTP 200 on both paths, and the extracted
section text is character-for-character identical between the two
paths after whitespace normalization (§ 3294: 2,440 chars; § 3295:
2,828; § 818: 228).

**Case law, without touching the shared CourtListener MCP quota (zero
MCP calls were made).** Cluster ids were located through the public
unauthenticated `/api/rest/v4/search/?type=o` endpoint (White 1160825,
College Hospital 1296967, Roby 2629580, Adams 1404112). Path A for
each opinion: the CourtListener opinion HTML page fetched with a
Chrome/macOS UA plus `Referer: https://www.courtlistener.com/`; path
B: the same page fetched with a Firefox/Windows UA, a different
`Accept` header and a different Referer. All eight fetches returned
HTTP 200 (the empty-202 bot challenge did not recur) and each pair was
byte-identical after extraction. Path C, a genuinely independent
index: CourtListener's public search feed was queried with
`cluster_id:<id> AND "<exact phrase>"` for one distinctive phrase per
opinion — `1160825`/"exercised substantial discretionary authority
over significant aspects", `1296967`/"requires actual knowledge of the
conduct and its outrageous nature", `2629580`/"formal policies that
affect a substantial portion of the company", `1404112`/"meaningful
evidence of the defendant's financial condition" — each returning
`count: 1` and the expected opinion. The authenticated
`/api/rest/v4/opinions/` endpoint was tried and returned 401
(anonymous access not permitted), which is why the HTML page plus the
phrase-level feed check are the two independent paths rather than the
API. The feed throttled twice mid-run (`429`, "Rate limit exceeded:
125/day"); each time the run waited inside the turn and retried until
it succeeded.

**Quote verification.** All 48 citation-quote instances across the 10
authority clauses were programmatically verified (`build.py`, dry run
before writing, re-run at write time) as exact contiguous substrings
of *both* fetched copies of each source after only these benign
normalizations: nbsp → space, whitespace collapse, and removal of
`*NNN` star-pagination markers from the CourtListener pages. Curly
quotation marks and apostrophes are preserved exactly as the sources
print them — no character substitution was needed for any quote, and
no quote required correction. Result: 48/48 against both copies.

**Corpus validation after merge** (`validate_corpus.py`): both JSON
files parse; 664 documents and 5,533 clauses; all clause ids unique;
all document ids unique; every `clauseOrder` entry resolves to an
existing clause; zero orphan clauses; every `{{placeholder}}` in every
clause body and gap resolves to a field declared by the owning
document; authority-clause key order, non-empty `gap`, and
citation key order checked for the whole corpus, not just the new
clauses. "Ending employment" now holds 85 documents.
