# Real Estate, new document: Anti-Deficiency Protections After Foreclosure or Short Sale (Code Civ. Proc. §§ 726, 580a, 580b, 580d, 580e) — Information Sheet

Wave 166 (Real Estate). Continues the even round-robin of waves 146,
148, 150, 152, 154, 156, 158, 160, 162, and 164.

## Why this document

Before this document, the corpus's 86 Real Estate documents had no
document on California's anti-deficiency scheme. Greps of both data
files found:

- **Zero** hits anywhere in the corpus for `580b`, `580e`, `580a` (as a
  deficiency provision), `Cornelison`, `Coker`, `Roseleaf`,
  `Wozab`, `DeBerard`, `sold-out junior`, `full credit bid`, or
  `one-action rule`.
- `580d` appears in exactly one substantive place:
  `foreclosure_antideficiency_rule`, a single clause inside the
  existing Notice of Default / Nonjudicial Foreclosure Process sheet,
  which quotes § 580d(a) and (b) and expressly says that "California
  law treats deficiency liability differently after a judicial
  foreclosure sale, a topic this document does not address."
- Scattered incidental mentions: `deedoftrust_power_of_sale_statutory_basis`
  and `hbor_intro_scope` name § 580d in passing; two clauses use the
  words "purchase money" in unrelated bulk-sale and successor-liability
  contexts.
- The only pre-existing hits for `2953` are three references to
  **Government** Code § 12953 in an unrelated arbitration clause;
  Civil Code § 2953 was absent from the corpus.

This sheet is the deficiency-side complement to those documents. The
one deliberate overlap is § 580d(a), re-quoted in
`antidef_580d_parity_and_sold_out_juniors` because *Roseleaf*'s
sold-out-junior holding turns on that subdivision's wording; that
clause's `gap` names the existing foreclosure sheet and says why the
subdivision is quoted again. Everything else here — § 726's one-action
and security-first rules and their sanction, § 580b's purchase money
bar, Civil Code § 2953 and the non-waivability holdings, § 580e's short
sale bar, § 580a's fair value measure, § 726(b)'s judicial-foreclosure
deficiency procedure, the sold-out junior rule, and the waste /
full credit bid rules — is new to the corpus.

## What this document covers

13 clauses: 2 drafting (purpose declaration, acknowledgment) and 11
authority clauses, 39 verified quotes in all.

- **`antidef_one_action_and_security_first`** (Code Civ. Proc.
  § 726(a); *Roseleaf Corp. v. Chierighino* (1963) 59 Cal.2d 35;
  *Security Pacific National Bank v. Wozab* (1990) 51 Cal.3d 991) —
  one form of action for a debt secured by real property, the
  security-first rule, and what the judgment may direct.
- **`antidef_section_726_sanction`** (*Wozab*, three quotes) — a
  creditor who sues on the debt before exhausting the security is
  deemed to have waived the security; the debt itself is not
  forfeited; the sanction is reduction to unsecured status.
- **`antidef_purchase_money_bar_580b`** (§ 580b(a), (b), (c), (d)) —
  the three purchase money situations, the extension to refinances
  with the new-advance exception, the guarantor/pledgor/surety
  carve-out, and the combined chattel-and-realty rule.
- **`antidef_purposes_of_580b`** (*Roseleaf*, two quotes; *DeBerard
  Properties, Ltd. v. Lim* (1999) 20 Cal.4th 659) — risk placed on the
  purchase money mortgagee, discouragement of overvaluation, and the
  stabilizing function in a downturn.
- **`antidef_waiver_of_antideficiency_protection`** (Civ. Code § 2953;
  *DeBerard*, two quotes; *Coker v. JPMorgan Chase Bank, N.A.* (2016)
  62 Cal.4th 667) — waivers made in connection with making or renewing
  a loan are void; § 580b may not be waived post-sale; a waiver given
  for short sale approval is invalid.
- **`antidef_short_sale_bar_580e`** (§ 580e(a)(1), (b), (c), (d)(1),
  (e)) — the consented short sale of a dwelling of not more than four
  units, the bar on extra compensation for consent, the fraud/waste
  carve-out, the exclusion of entity borrowers, and void waivers.
- **`antidef_coker_short_sale_of_purchase_money_loan`** (*Coker*, two
  quotes) — § 580b applies to short sales as it does to foreclosure
  sales, and survives a borrower's waiver of § 726.
- **`antidef_fair_value_limit_580a`** (§ 580a, three quotes) — the
  complaint's required contents, the probate-referee appraisal, the
  fair value finding, the double cap on the judgment, the three-month
  deadline, and the sale-first requirement.
- **`antidef_judicial_foreclosure_deficiency_726b`** (§ 726(b) two
  quotes, § 726(e)) — what the foreclosure decree must declare, the
  three-month application and fair value hearing, the 15-day notice,
  and the link between deficiency availability and the statutory right
  of redemption.
- **`antidef_580d_parity_and_sold_out_juniors`** (§ 580d(a), (c);
  *Roseleaf*, three quotes) — the power-of-sale bar, the parity
  rationale, and the holding that a junior lienholder whose security is
  sold out by a senior sale may still sue on its note.
- **`antidef_waste_claims_and_the_full_credit_bid`** (*Cornelison v.
  Kornbluth* (1975) 15 Cal.3d 590, four quotes) — waste recovery after
  a nonjudicial sale barred except for bad faith waste, the lender's
  freedom to bid less than the debt, the measure of waste damages after
  an underbid, and the full credit bid's effect.

## Genuine corrections and findings

- **One quote is deliberately trimmed of terminal punctuation because
  the source's period was OCR'd as a comma.** In the fetched
  CourtListener text of *Wozab*, the sentence "When a secured creditor
  violates section 726(a) by obtaining judgment on the debt before
  foreclosing upon the security, he is deemed to have waived the
  security" is followed by `, (pp. 997-998, ante; Walker v. Community
  Bank, supra ...)` — a comma where the reporter has a period. Rather
  than reproduce the defect or silently repair it, the citation stops
  at the last word of the sentence. Disclosed in the
  `antidef_section_726_sanction` `gap`.
- **A *Cornelison* sentence carrying an OCR comma was left unquoted
  entirely.** The court's statement that a full credit bidder "cannot
  establish any impairment, of security" contains a stray comma in the
  fetched text. The clause instead quotes the immediately following
  sentence (the underbid rule, clean in both fetches) and the
  recapitulation paragraph on page 608 (also clean), and states the
  full credit bid consequence in the body. Disclosed in the
  `antidef_waste_claims_and_the_full_credit_bid` `gap`.
- **Other OCR artifacts in the fetched texts were avoided, not
  quoted.** *Roseleaf* contains `sueh mortgage`, `the deficiency may he
  limited`, `In either case the debtor is pro-' teeted`, and `Stats.
  1939, eh. 586`; *Cornelison* contains `despóilers`, `tó permit`, and
  a trailing hyphen after the bad-faith holding; *Coker* contains
  `Sechon 726` twice; *DeBerard* contains `Gh irardo`, `pinchase money
  lien`, and `the Liras'` for "the Lims'". None of those passages is
  quoted. The bad-faith holding quoted from *Cornelison* ends at
  "mortgagor." and stops before the stray hyphen.
- **Superseded statutory text is flagged, never passed off as
  current.** *Roseleaf* (1963) quotes the 1939 version of § 580d, and
  *DeBerard* (1999) and *Coker* (2016) construe versions of § 580b
  whose operative words were "[n]o deficiency judgment shall lie in any
  event." The current §§ 580b and 580d, quoted here as amended in 2014
  and 2022, instead bar a deficiency from being "owed or collected" as
  well as reduced to judgment. No superseded statutory quotation
  appears in any citation; the divergence is stated in the `gap` of
  every clause that relies on one of those opinions.
- **§ 580b is not on Civil Code § 2953's list.** The statute voids
  waivers of Civ. Code §§ 2924, 2924b, 2924c and Code Civ. Proc.
  §§ 580a and 726 — not § 580b. The sheet states the list as written
  and then states *DeBerard*'s holding that the omission does not
  license a post-sale waiver, rather than conflating the two.
- **§ 580a is a measure, not an authorization.** The clause says so
  explicitly: § 580a governs how a post-power-of-sale deficiency is
  pleaded and computed, while § 580d bars such a deficiency in the
  ordinary case; *Roseleaf* held § 580a's limits inapplicable to a
  sold-out junior's action on its own note.
- **Coker's § 726 point is stated as what it is.** The court agreed
  with the lender that the borrower had waived § 726 by requesting the
  short sale; the quoted sentence therefore states the consequence of
  such a waiver, not a holding that § 726 always survives a short sale.
  Said in that clause's `gap`.

## Method

**Statutes.** Code Civ. Proc. §§ 580a, 580b, 580d, 580e, and 726, and
Civ. Code § 2953, were each fetched **twice** from
`leginfo.legislature.ca.gov` via curl through the environment proxy, by
two independent paths: (a) `codes_displaySection.xhtml` under a
Chrome/Windows User-Agent, and (b) the separate
`printCodeSectionWindow.xhtml` endpoint under a
`curl-verify/1.0 (independent second fetch)` User-Agent. All six
section pairs extracted to byte-identical section text (the print
endpoint returns a ~6 KB page against the ~165 KB full page, so the two
paths are genuinely different renderings of the same section). One
`curl (35) Recv failure: Connection reset by peer` on the Civ. Code
§ 2953 fetch was retried inside the turn until it returned 200.

**Case law — no CourtListener MCP calls were made.** Cluster ids were
located through the public, unauthenticated v4 search API
(`/api/rest/v4/search/?type=o&q=...`) via curl through the proxy; the
opinions themselves came from the public opinion HTML.

- *Roseleaf Corp. v. Chierighino* (1963) 59 Cal.2d 35 — cluster
  **1126148**.
- *Cornelison v. Kornbluth* (1975) 15 Cal.3d 590 — cluster
  **1161136**.
- *Security Pacific National Bank v. Wozab* (1990) 51 Cal.3d 991 —
  cluster **2612486**.
- *DeBerard Properties, Ltd. v. Lim* (1999) 20 Cal.4th 659 — cluster
  **1277425**.
- *Coker v. JPMorgan Chase Bank, N.A.* (2016) 62 Cal.4th 667 — cluster
  **3171241**.

Each opinion was fetched **twice** with two distinct full header sets
(Chrome/Windows and Safari/macOS, each with
`Referer: https://www.courtlistener.com/`, distinct `Accept` and
`Accept-Language`) and at two distinct URL paths (`/opinion/<id>/x/`
and `/opinion/<id>/y/`). All pairs extracted to identical opinion text.
The *Coker* second fetch first returned the empty 202 the task note
describes; it was retried in-turn after a 30-second wait under a
Firefox/Windows header set at the canonical slug
`/opinion/3171241/coker-v-jpmorgan-chase-bank-na/` and returned 200
with text identical to the first fetch.

Justia, FindLaw, Google Scholar, and case.law are egress-blocked and
were not used.
