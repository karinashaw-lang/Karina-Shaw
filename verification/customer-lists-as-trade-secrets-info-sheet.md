# Confidentiality & IP, new document: Customer Lists as Trade Secrets Under CUTSA — Information Sheet

Part of wave 156 (Confidentiality & IP category; wave 156 covers the
same four categories as waves 146/148/150/152/154: Confidentiality & IP,
Ending employment, Business Formation, Real Estate).

## Why this document

The task brief listed the Confidentiality & IP category's recent
additions (joint authorship, DMCA § 512(c), first sale, copyright
preemption, inevitable disclosure) and several suggested topics.
Reading `data/documents.json`'s full Confidentiality & IP title list (81
documents) showed that three of the suggested topics were already
covered as whole documents (CCPA employee/applicant data, fair use, and
the Desny idea-submission doctrine), so the search moved further afield.
Grepped the whole corpus (both data files) for "customer list" (one
passing mention, in a Non-Solicitation clause), "Morlife," "ABBA
Rubber," "American Credit Indemnity," "readily ascertainable" (five
passing mentions, none as a document topic), and "Retirement Group" (two
passing mentions inside the existing Employee Non-Solicitation
Agreement's clauses) — no existing document addresses how the CUTSA
definition of a trade secret is applied to a customer list, the
economic-value-from-secrecy analysis, California's deletion of the
uniform act's "readily ascertainable" language, or the
solicitation-versus-announcement distinction. The existing trade-secret
documents (cease-and-desist letter, injunctive-relief standards,
pre-discovery identification under CCP § 2019.210, preemption doctrine,
inevitable disclosure, criminal theft, DTSA seizure, evidentiary
privilege) address other aspects of trade secret law and are
cross-referenced rather than duplicated.

## What this document covers

14 clauses: 3 drafting (purpose/declaration, relationship to sibling
documents, signature/acknowledgment) and 11 authority clauses, citing
Cal. Civ. Code § 3426.1(a), (b)(2), and (d); Cal. Bus. & Prof. Code
§ 16600(a); Morlife, Inc. v. Perry (1997) 56 Cal.App.4th 1514; and ABBA
Rubber Co. v. Seaquist (1991) 235 Cal.App.3d 1:

- **`custlist_general_rule_former_employee_competition`** (Morlife,
  quoting Continental Car-Na-Var Corp. v. Moseley) — a former employee
  may compete for former customers if the competition is fairly and
  legally conducted, but may not use confidential information or trade
  secrets to do so.
- **`custlist_statutory_definition`** (§ 3426.1(d)) — the two-part
  statutory definition; notes that the definition uses "generally
  known" and not "readily ascertainable."
- **`custlist_three_elements_lists_can_qualify`** (ABBA Rubber) — the
  court's three-element reading and its statement that a customer list
  is a type of information that can qualify.
- **`custlist_economic_value_from_secrecy`** (ABBA Rubber; Morlife) —
  value to the owner alone is insufficient; the list must tell
  competitors a fact they do not know (ABBA Rubber's widget
  hypothetical); Morlife's "direct its sales efforts" formulation.
- **`custlist_effort_and_difficulty_of_compilation`** (Morlife) —
  reluctance to protect directory-derived lists; protection for lists
  built through time and effort; "the more difficult information is to
  obtain"; the list need not be a physical document (fn. 4).
- **`custlist_readily_ascertainable_defense_not_element`** (ABBA
  Rubber) — **the central finding**: California deleted the uniform
  act's "and not being readily ascertainable by proper means by"
  language when enacting § 3426.1(d)(1); ready ascertainability is
  therefore not an element of the definition but remains a defense to
  misappropriation (fn. 9, citing the legislative committee comment).
- **`custlist_reasonable_efforts_evidence`** (Morlife) — restricted
  computer access, contract confidentiality provision, handbook
  statement; labeling is not conclusive but is an important factor;
  information may be in an employee's memory (quoting Greenly v.
  Cooper).
- **`custlist_misappropriation_use_and_improper_means`** (§ 3426.1(a),
  (b)(2); Morlife) — definitions of improper means (with the reverse
  engineering / independent derivation carve-out) and misappropriation
  by disclosure or use; "simple disclosure or use may suffice."
- **`custlist_solicitation_versus_announcement`** (Morlife, quoting
  Aetna Building Maintenance Co. v. West and describing American Credit
  Indemnity Co. v. Sacks) — solicitation is "use"; merely informing
  customers of a change in employment is not solicitation; fn. 8 leaves
  open whether the announcement exception survives the statutory
  definition.
- **`custlist_personal_relationship_no_exception`** (Morlife) —
  rejection of the Moss, Adams personal-relationship rationale, on the
  merits and because Moss, Adams predates the statute.
- **`custlist_section_16600_boundary_and_injunction_scope`** (§ 16600(a);
  Morlife) — the trade-secret injunction rests on the statute, not a
  contractual restraint; the injunction affirmed in Morlife left the
  former employees free to solicit customers whose identities were not
  trade secrets.

## Genuine corrections and findings

- **Central finding, text-confirmed**: the leginfo text of § 3426.1(d)
  contains no "readily ascertainable" language, matching ABBA Rubber's
  account of the Legislature's deletion; the document quotes ABBA
  Rubber's holding and its footnote 9 explaining that the concept
  survives as a defense going to misappropriation rather than to the
  existence of a trade secret.
- **A real tension in the case law, disclosed rather than smoothed
  over**: Morlife (1997) describes courts as "reluctant to protect
  customer lists to the extent they embody information which is
  'readily ascertainable'" while citing ABBA Rubber (1991), which held
  that ready ascertainability is not part of the definition. The
  effort-and-difficulty clause's `gap` field flags this and points to
  the ABBA Rubber clause rather than presenting either formulation as
  the whole rule.
- **Morlife fn. 8 left the announcement exception open**: the document
  does not state that a bare announcement is always safe; it quotes the
  footnote in which the court declined to decide whether the exception
  has continued vitality under the statute's definition.
- **Unpublished portions avoided**: ABBA Rubber's parts C and D (form
  and scope of the injunction) were not certified for publication;
  nothing is quoted from them, and the gap text says so.
- **A page-break artifact handled by not quoting across it**: the
  Morlife sentence distinguishing protected lists from "mere identities
  and locations of customers" carries a "*1522" star-pagination marker
  mid-sentence in the retrieved text; it is summarized, not quoted, and
  the gap field discloses why.
- **A quotation interrupted by citations handled by splitting it**: the
  Morlife "labeling" sentence is interrupted by a parenthetical string
  of citations; its two halves are quoted as two separate citation
  entries and the gap field discloses this.
- **An OCR artifact noted**: the ABBA Rubber text contains "ITiey" for
  "They" in an unquoted sentence; disclosed in the gap field.

## Method

- Fetched Cal. Civ. Code § 3426.1 and Cal. Bus. & Prof. Code § 16600
  from `leginfo.legislature.ca.gov` via curl through the sandbox proxy,
  each twice with distinct User-Agent strings; the extracted section
  bodies were identical across both fetches after markup stripping and
  whitespace normalization.
- Located Morlife (cluster 2251515) and ABBA Rubber (cluster 2119830)
  via the CourtListener MCP `search` tool. Retrieved each opinion's full
  text via the MCP `read_document` tool (Morlife: lead opinion id
  9745568 within cluster 2251515, so cluster id and lead-opinion id
  differ; ABBA Rubber: opinion id 2119830 = cluster id).
- **Deviation from the usual two-API-fetch method, disclosed**: after
  the first full-text reads, the CourtListener API returned HTTP 429
  with a 125-requests-per-day quota exhausted (the quota is shared
  across the parallel wave-156 agents), with the reset time receding as
  other agents continued to consume it. Repeated `sleep`-and-retry
  attempts inside the turn did not clear it, and Justia, FindLaw,
  Google Scholar, Harvard CAP, Leagle, Casemine, and the Wayback Machine
  were all blocked (403 or egress-denied) from this environment. The
  second independent retrieval was therefore performed against
  CourtListener's public Atom search feed (`/feed/search/?type=o`),
  which is served outside the API quota: every quoted passage was split
  into its quotation-mark-free sub-phrases, and each sub-phrase was
  submitted as an exact-phrase query restricted to the opinion's
  cluster (`cluster_id:<id> AND "<phrase>"`) under a distinct
  User-Agent. All 21 Morlife sub-phrases and all 8 ABBA Rubber
  sub-phrases returned the target opinion as a match (29/29).
  Negative controls — a fabricated phrase against the Morlife cluster, a
  genuine Morlife phrase against the ABBA Rubber cluster, and a
  one-word-altered Morlife phrase against the Morlife cluster — each
  returned zero entries, confirming the filter discriminates. The
  ABBA Rubber quotes were additionally confirmed as exact substrings of
  the saved first-fetch text by script; the Morlife quotes were
  transcribed directly from the first-fetch text and checked by eye
  against it (the first-fetch response was returned inline rather than
  persisted to disk, so no local substring script could be run for it).
  Each affected clause's gap field describes this method accurately
  rather than claiming two API fetches.
- All 9 statutory quotes and all ABBA Rubber quotes programmatically
  confirmed as exact substrings of the saved source texts (`verify.py`
  in the agent's scratchpad); Morlife quotes confirmed as above.
