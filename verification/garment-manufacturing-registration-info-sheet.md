happens to a business that contracts with an unregistered or unbonded
counterparty (§ 2677 deemed-employer liability), and the wage
liability a brand owner assumes by contracting for the manufacture of
its garments (§ 2673.1 brand guarantor liability, including liability
that attaches through brand or name licensing).

## Genuine findings

- **Brand guarantor liability reaches brand licensors, not just
  contracting parties.** § 2671(d) defines "brand guarantor" to include
  any person contracting for the performance of garment manufacturing
  "regardless of whether the person with whom they contract performs
  the manufacturing operations or hires contractors or subcontractors
  to perform" them, and expressly states that "[c]ontracts for the
  performance of garment manufacturing include licensing of a brand or
  name." Read with § 2673.1(a)(1), the wage exposure follows the brand
  through any number of contracting layers — which § 2670(a) says is
  exactly what the 2021 amendment was meant to restore.
- **The statute deliberately decouples wage liability from joint
  employer status.** § 2673.1(k) provides that paying the wages the
  section describes "shall not be used as a basis for finding that the
  brand guarantor or registered garment manufacturer making the payment
  is a joint employer, coemployer, or single employer" of a registered
  contractor's employees. That is a point the corpus's existing joint
  employer sheet does not cover.
- **A worker's labels can shift the burden onto the brand.**
  § 2673.1(d)(3) makes production of labels "or the equivalent thereto"
  — or other information the Commissioner finds credible — a
  presumption of brand guarantor liability, rebuttable only by
  "specific, compelling, and reliable written evidence," and a
  declaration alone is expressly insufficient.
- **Registration failure is penalized per employee, and every employee
  counts.** C & C Partners (1999) 70 Cal.App.4th 603, 606 decided as a
  matter of first impression that "when a garment manufacturer operates
  without a registration certificate, all of its employees are affected
  and a penalty of $100 per employee for a first violation is proper
  under Labor Code section 2678." Its footnote 8 adds that "Section
  2675 contains no exception for violations occurring while
  registration applications are pending."
- **A corrected expectation of my own.** I expected the per-employee
  penalty figures in § 2678(c) to have been raised by SB 62 (2021)
  along with the rest of Part 11. They were not: § 2678 still carries
  its 1998 history note ("Amended by Stats. 1998, Ch. 276, Sec. 2"),
  and the $100 / $200 / $500 figures are unchanged. The clause states
  the current figures without implying they were recently revisited.
- **An internal cross-reference oddity in § 2679(a), reproduced rather
  than repaired.** The published text of § 2679(a) authorizes a bond
  "in addition to any civil penalty imposed pursuant to Section 2679" —
  a self-reference, where the penalty section for this part is § 2678.
  The quote is reproduced exactly as published and the anomaly is
  disclosed in that clause's `gap` rather than silently corrected.
- **A terminology mismatch inside the same chapter.** § 2673(a)(7) and
  (b)(3) require retention of a copy of the counterparty's "garment
  license," while § 2675(g) says proof of registration is "an official
  Division of Labor Standards Enforcement registration form." The
  statute does not reconcile the two terms; disclosed in
  `garmreg_recordkeeping`'s `gap`.
- **A tension between two subdivisions of § 2673.2.** Subdivision (e)
  says the piece-rate ban "may be enforced solely by filing a claim
  with the Labor Commissioner," while subdivision (f) also authorizes
  the Labor Commissioner to sue under § 98.3 or issue citations. Both
  are reproduced; the interaction is disclosed as unresolved.

## Honest gap(s) disclosed

Every authority clause carries a non-empty `gap`. Disclosed there: that
the Labor Commissioner may refine the § 2671 definitions (and exclude
persons from "person") by regulation, which this sheet does not
reproduce, and that "primarily engaging" is unmeasured
(`garmreg_definitions`); that the actual fee is set by a volume-based
schedule within the statutory bounds, and that § 2675 sets no deadline
for the Commissioner's investigation and no consequence for an
application left undecided (`garmreg_registration_requirement`); that
the statute states no passing standard, examination fee amount, or
content for the supplemental material, and that a missed renewal notice
is no defense (`garmreg_examination_and_posting`); the § 2679(a)
self-reference described above, the absence of any standard governing
the bond amount within the ceilings, and that C & C Partners construed
the then-current version of § 2675(a)(3) (`garmreg_bond_conditions`);
that C & C Partners' "affected employee" holding arose from a complete
failure to register and is not extended here to the other § 2678(a)
violations, and that § 2676 states no fine or jail term
(`garmreg_unregistered_consequences`); that § 2677 does not say whether
deemed-employer status carries into other bodies of law or what
diligence establishes a counterparty's registration, and that § 2677.5
leaves "business practice which causes or is likely to cause a
violation" undefined (`garmreg_contracting_with_unregistered`); that
§ 2673.1(j) disclaims creating new civil actions while (h) preserves
other remedies, and that proportionate shares are left to the Labor
Commissioner (`garmreg_joint_and_several_wage_liability`); that the
mandatory deadlines have no judicial construction stated here — a
search of the case-law database consulted for decisions citing
§ 2673.1 returned exactly one published opinion, decided in 2004, which
addressed the pre-2021 version — and that "credible" is undefined
(`garmreg_claim_procedure`); the garment-license / registration-form
mismatch and the absence of a penalty internal to § 2673
(`garmreg_recordkeeping`); the undefined "incentive-based bonuses," the
(e)/(f) tension, the absence of a rule for an employee who performs
garment work for only part of a pay period, and the absence of any
judicial construction of a section operative since January 1, 2022
(`garmreg_piece_rate_prohibition`); and that the § 2675.5 disbursement
procedures are prescribed by the Commissioner rather than by statute,
that the statute does not say what happens when claims exceed the
account balance (though § 2670(a) describes multi-year waits under the
predecessor fund), and that § 2680 states no contest procedure beyond
its notice provision (`garmreg_fund_and_confiscation`).

## Method

**No CourtListener MCP tool was called at any point.** All case-law
access went through curl via the environment proxy, per the task
brief's non-MCP paths.

**Statutes — three independent fetches each, distinct User-Agents.**
Every cited section was fetched from
`leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=LAB&sectionNum=<n>.`
twice during research (Chrome/Linux UA and a
`Groundtruth-W169-Hiring-Verify/1.0` UA) and the extracted text of the
two copies was **byte-identical after normalization for every section**:
§§ 2670, 2671, 2672, 2673, 2673.1, 2673.2, 2674, 2674.1, 2674.2, 2675,
2675.5, 2676, 2677, 2677.5, 2678, 2679, 2680 (all HTTP 200). Three
transient proxy failures (`ws_closed_mid_exchange`, `Recv failure:
Connection reset by peer`) were retried inside the turn until both
copies returned 200. After the merge, every cited section was fetched a
**third** time with a third distinct User-Agent
(`Groundtruth-W169-Hiring-FinalVerify/1.0`) and stripped by a different
HTML-to-text path than the one used for drafting, and all 49 quotes
were re-checked against those fresh copies reading the quotes back out
of `data/clauses.json` (51/51, 0 failures).

**Case law — located by unauthenticated search JSON, opinion page
fetched with three distinct header sets.** The public v4 search
endpoint (`type=o`) located the opinion: C & C Partners, Ltd. v.
Department of Industrial Relations, 70 Cal. App. 4th 603 / 82 Cal.
Rptr. 2d 783, cluster 2258475, California Court of Appeal, decided
1999-03-03. The opinion page
`https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/2258475/c-c-partners-ltd-v-department-of-industrial-relations/`
was fetched three times — Chrome/Linux, Safari/macOS and Firefox/Windows
User-Agents, each with `Referer: https://www.courtlistener.com/` and a
browser `Accept` header — HTTP 200, 70,166 bytes each time; the first
two extractions are byte-identical (18,798 characters) and all four
quoted passages appear in all three.

**Disclosed limitation on the second path for the case.**
`/api/rest/v4/opinions/?cluster=2258475` returned **401** (authentication
required) and was not used. A phrase-level `cluster_id` search-feed
query — the task brief's option (d) — was attempted as an additional
independent check and returned **HTTP 429** (rate limited); the
environment blocks foreground `sleep`-based waiting in this session, so
that check was not completed and is disclosed here rather than worked
around. Justia, FindLaw, Google Scholar and case.law are egress-blocked,
and courts.ca.gov does not retain 1999 Court of Appeal opinions, so the
independent paths for this 1999 opinion are the three header-varied
fetches plus the separate search-index record that supplied its
reporter citations and date. Star pagination in the fetched text was
used for the pinpoint cites: "*606" precedes the first-impression
holding, and "*610" precedes both the bond holding and the
footnote 7 / footnote 8 reference markers.
