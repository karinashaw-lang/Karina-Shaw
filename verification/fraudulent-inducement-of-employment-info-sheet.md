## What this document covers

9 clauses: 2 drafting (intro, certification) and 7 authority clauses,
citing Civ. Code §§1709, 1710, 1572 (intro clause and subd. 4); Civ.
Code §3294(a), (b), (c)(3); and Lazar v. Superior Court (Rykoff-Sexton,
Inc.), 12 Cal.4th 631 (1996) (26 passages across five clauses):

- **`fraudhire_deceit_statutes_civ_1709_1710_1572`** — the 1872
  codification of deceit: §1709 (liability for willful deception
  intended to induce a change of position), §1710 (the four categories
  of deceit, the fourth being a promise made without intent to perform),
  and §1572 (actual fraud in inducing a contract, with the same
  promise-without-intent category).
- **`fraudhire_lazar_elements_and_promissory_fraud`** — Lazar: the
  question presented; the five elements of fraud (quoting Witkin);
  promissory fraud as a subspecies of deceit; the claim lies where a
  defendant fraudulently induces a contract and does not depend on
  whether the promise is enforceable as a contract; the double-recovery
  limit.
- **`fraudhire_lazar_recruiting_representations_stated_claim`** — the
  recruiting representations alleged in Lazar (job security so long as
  he performed, long-term relationship, financial strength, rapid raises,
  "our word is our bond"), their alleged falsity when made, the reliance
  alleged (resigning, relocating, buying a home), and the holding that
  these allegations state promissory fraud "as traditionally
  understood."
- **`fraudhire_lazar_hunter_termination_distinguished`** — Lazar's
  explanation of Hunter v. Up-Right: a misrepresentation used to effect a
  termination is not independently actionable because the employer could
  have terminated directly (no detrimental reliance); Hunter's express
  reservation of misrepresentations designed to induce some other
  detrimental change of position; the unanimity in Hunter that
  promissory fraud remains viable; and the line Lazar draws (pre-hire
  representations made when the employer had no coercive power and the
  applicant was free to decline).
- **`fraudhire_lazar_no_employer_exemption_and_pleading_specificity`** —
  Lazar: the Civil Code fraud statutes contain no express exception for
  employers or employees; Foley provides no authority for exempting
  employers from ordinary fraud rules; election to affirm the contract
  and sue for the fraud; fraud must be pled specifically, with the
  heightened Tarmann burden for claims against a corporate employer.
- **`fraudhire_lazar_damages_scope`** — Lazar: out-of-pocket plus
  benefit-of-the-bargain damages; damages "separate from the
  termination" (moving expense and disruption, loss of security and
  income from former employment); lost income from the new job must be
  sought in contract on the facts as pled; double-recovery limit; §3333
  and §3294 exemplary damages.
- **`fraudhire_exemplary_damages_civ_3294`** — Civ. Code §3294(a)
  standard, (c)(3) definition of fraud, and (b) the employer's liability
  for employees' acts only with advance knowledge/conscious disregard,
  authorization or ratification, or personal culpability — and, for a
  corporation, only through an officer, director, or managing agent.

## Genuine findings

- **Lazar's line is about coercive power, not timing labels.** The
  opinion does not say "pre-hire statements are actionable, post-hire
  statements are not." Its rationale is that Hunter's employer could
  have achieved the same result (termination) truthfully, so the lie
  caused no detriment, whereas Rykoff could not have moved Lazar across
  the country truthfully. The Hunter clause is written around that
  mechanism, and its gap field says the opinion does not hold every
  pre-hire statement actionable.
- **The damages line runs between the two jobs, not between tort and
  contract in the abstract.** Lazar allows tort recovery for what the
  plaintiff lost by leaving the old job (security, income, relocation
  cost) and channels loss of income from the new job into the contract
  claim, "on the facts as pled." The clause quotes that qualifier and
  the gap field flags that it is not stated as a universal rule.
- **§3294(b) matters more here than in most fraud cases.** Recruiting
  statements are almost always made by individual managers. The
  corporate-employer rule (officer, director, or managing agent) is the
  gate to exemplary damages that Lazar says are "appropriate" in
  principle, so it is quoted in full rather than summarized.
- **Lazar and the §970 document are siblings, not duplicates.** Lazar
  himself pleaded §970 alongside common-law fraud, and the §970 claim
  survived demurrer independently. The intro and facts clauses say so
  and defer to the existing document for the statute.

## Honest gap(s) disclosed

(`fraudhire_lazar_elements_and_promissory_fraud`); that every fact
recited is an allegation, that the outcome on remand is not reported,
and that the opinion does not classify categories of recruiting
statements (`fraudhire_lazar_recruiting_representations_stated_claim`);
that **Hunter and Foley are quoted only as Lazar reproduces them because
the CourtListener daily cap prevented an independent second fetch**, and
that the quoted footnote is Hunter's language
(`fraudhire_lazar_hunter_termination_distinguished`); that Stansfield,

(`fraudhire_lazar_no_employer_exemption_and_pleading_specificity`); that
the damages discussion is fact-bound, that §3333 was not fetched, and

(`fraudhire_exemplary_damages_civ_3294`).

## Method

**Statutes** — fetched directly from leginfo.legislature.ca.gov via curl
through the environment's proxy (raw HTML fetched and the
`codeLawSectionNoHead` statutory-text div parsed directly, following
the approach used in prior waves):

- Civ. Code §§1709, 1710, 1572, 3294: each fetched twice, User-Agent
  "Mozilla/5.0 (X11; Linux x86_64) groundtruth-verifier/1.0" and
  "Mozilla/5.0 (Macintosh; Intel Mac OS X 10_15_7) ... Version/17.2
  Safari/605.1.15". All eight fetches 200 OK. Raw page sizes varied
  between the two fetches of a section (161,898–165,204 bytes) because
  of session-specific JSF ViewState tokens; the extracted statutory-text
  div was byte-identical between the two fetches for all four sections
  (401 / 763 / 1,045 / 2,935 characters including the heading breadcrumb
  and enactment note).

**Case law** — the CourtListener API's shared daily cap (125/day across
the four parallel agents) was already exhausted when this task began:
the first `read_document` call returned 429 with "expected available in
4343 seconds". The wait was taken inside the turn (foreground
`until [ $(date +%s) -ge ... ]` loops, about 72 minutes). After the
window reopened, exactly one API call succeeded — `read_document`
(cluster_id 1250721) returned the full Lazar opinion
(`html_with_citations`, 70,650 characters; lead opinion id 9583405,
concurrences by Mosk, J. and Kennard, J. listed but not used) — and the
next three calls (Hunter 1199869, Agosta 2257651, Helmer 2255483) were
throttled again with "expected available in 7950 seconds", the cap
being a rolling window shared with the other agents. Rather than wait a
further 2+ hours per call with no guarantee of a slot, the document was
scoped to the Supreme Court opinion that was fully in hand plus the
statutes, and the un-fetched cases are disclosed in the gap fields above.

Cluster ids were located without API calls through CourtListener's
public Atom search feed (`/feed/search/?type=o&case_name=...`), which is
reachable via curl through the proxy: Lazar 1250721, Hunter 1199869,
Agosta 2257651, Helmer 2255483, Toscano 2274753. The CourtListener HTML
opinion page returned a 202 with an empty body (WAF challenge), the v3
and v4 REST endpoints returned 401 without credentials, and Justia,
FindLaw, casetext, leagle, anylaw, casemine, Google Scholar, and both
case.law hosts were blocked or gone (403/410/connection refused), so no
full-text mirror was available as a second fetch.

- Lazar (1250721): first fetch via MCP `read_document` as above (saved
  from the tool-results file; tags stripped, `<page-number>` markers
  removed, entities unescaped, whitespace collapsed). **Second,
  independent path:** each of the 26 quoted passages was submitted to
  the public Atom search feed as an exact-phrase query restricted to the
  cluster (`q=cluster_id:1250721 AND "<phrase>"`, User-Agent
  "curl/8 groundtruth-verifier (wave157)"), which runs against
  CourtListener's separate search index rather than the REST API and was
  not subject to the cap. 19 of the 26 passages matched as a single
  phrase (the feed returned exactly one entry, titled "Lazar v. Superior
  Court", with a highlighted snippet). The remaining 7 initially
  returned no entry; investigation showed the search index stores the
  reporter page markers inline (the snippet for one query reads
  "employed by Rykoff so long *636 as he performed"), so phrases that
  cross a page break cannot match as a unit, and the first-pass fallback
  (truncating to 110 characters) sometimes cut mid-word. Each of those 7
  passages was then re-queried as two or three sub-phrases split at the
  page break or at a sentence boundary, and every sub-phrase returned
  exactly one entry for Lazar (logs: `feed_check.log`, `feed_retry.log`,
  `feed_halves.log` in the scratch directory). This confirms every
  quoted string exists in a second, independently indexed copy of the
  opinion, with the caveat that a phrase search confirms word sequence
  rather than punctuation characters; punctuation was checked against
  the API text.
