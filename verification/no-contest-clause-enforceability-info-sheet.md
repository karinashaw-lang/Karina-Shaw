# Estate Planning, new document: No-Contest Clause Enforceability — Information Sheet

## Why this document

The corpus's existing Last Will and Testament document has a single,
narrow clause (`will_no_contest_clause`) stating the general limit.
This document adds real depth on Prob. Code §§ 21310-21315: the
enumerated protected-contest categories, the probable-cause standard's
definition, the statutory definition of a "direct contest," and
application to both wills and trusts.

## What this document covers

7 clauses: 2 drafting (declaration, certification) and 5 authority
clauses:

- **`nocontest_narrow_default_and_categories`** (Prob. Code § 21311(a))
  — the default rule and the three enumerated categories where a
  no-contest clause IS enforceable.
- **`nocontest_probable_cause_standard`** (§ 21311(b)) — the
  probable-cause definition.
- **`nocontest_direct_contest_defined`** (§ 21310(b)) — the six
  enumerated grounds that make a challenge a "direct contest."
- **`nocontest_applies_to_wills_and_trusts`** (§ 21310(e); § 21315(a)
  -(b)) — the instrument-neutral scope.
- **`nocontest_gap_filler_and_mandatory`** (§ 21312; § 21313; § 21314)
  — strict construction against the transferor's intent, the scheme's
  non-exhaustive character, and its non-waivable application.

## Genuine corrections and findings

- **A correction to the original framing**: California's No Contest
  Clause Part is exactly §§ 21310-21315 — confirmed directly from
  leginfo's own Part heading ("PART 3. NO CONTEST CLAUSE
  [21310-21315]"), not "21310-21315ish." That is the complete range;
  nothing exists beyond § 21315 in this Part.
- Confirmed § 21311(a)'s three enumerated categories, § 21311(b)'s
  probable-cause definition, and § 21310(b)'s six direct-contest
  grounds exactly as originally framed.
- Confirmed the scheme is instrument-neutral: "protected instrument"
  and coverage of "any instrument... that became irrevocable on or
  after January 1, 2001" apply to wills and trusts alike, corroborated
  by § 21310(b)(5)'s separate will/trust revocation cross-references.
- A genuine additional finding beyond the original framing: § 21312
  requires strict construction of a no-contest clause against the
  transferor's intent — real added depth. Section 21313 confirms this
  Part is not a complete codification (common law fills gaps), and
  § 21314 confirms the Part applies notwithstanding contrary
  instrument language — it cannot be waived by the drafting
  instrument.

## Honest gap disclosed

The declaratory-relief safe-harbor mechanism (originally asked about
as task item 6) could not be verified. Section 21320, fetched
directly, returns no code-section content on leginfo, confirmed
against the Part 3 table of contents, which bounds the Part at
§ 21315. This matches known legal history: a safe-harbor "application"
procedure existed under the former pre-2010 no-contest statute (old
§ 21320), but was repealed and not carried forward in the 2008/2010
recodification that produced the current §§ 21310-21315 scheme.
Omitted entirely rather than fabricated or placeholder-cited.
