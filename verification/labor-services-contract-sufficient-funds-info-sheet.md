## Genuine findings

- **The statute regulates the contract price, not the employment
  relationship.** Subdivision (a) forbids *entering into* the
  agreement; liability does not depend on the hiring party being an
  employer or joint employer of the contractor's workers. That is what
  makes it a hiring-side rule distinct from § 2810.3 and from the
  wage-order joint-employer analysis.
- **The presumption is a burden-of-proof presumption, not a safe
  harbor.** Subdivision (b) says a contract meeting all of subdivision
  (d) creates a *rebuttable* presumption "affecting the burden of
  proof" that there has been no violation. The statute does not say
  what rebuts it, and the sheet does not suggest otherwise.
- **A defective contract alone is not a damages case.** Subdivision
  (g)(1) bars an action "unless it is pleaded and proved that an
  employee was injured as a result of a violation of a labor law or
  regulation in connection with the performance of the contract or
  agreement" — the underfunding must have produced an actual labor-law
  violation injuring an employee.
- **Castillo supplies the operative two-part showing.** Liability
  requires both that the hiring party "knew or should have known the
  contract price was reasonably likely to be insufficient at the time
  the contract was executed" *and* that "the contract price proved
  insufficient in practice." The first question is prospective, the
  second retrospective.
- **Sufficiency is measured against the legal minimum, not the market.**
  Castillo rejected the plaintiffs' prevailing-wage theory: "there is
  no general law requiring an employer to pay its workers the average
  local wage for a particular skill or trade, if that average wage is
  higher than the legal minimum." Its own footnote preserves the point
  that where a prevailing wage law *does* apply, the prevailing wage is
  the minimum permissible wage — so the holding is about the absence of
  a free-floating market-wage requirement, not about public works.
- **Deliberate ignorance is not a defense.** Subdivision (i)(3) makes a
  failure to request or obtain information the contractor was required
  to supply *itself* constitute knowledge of that information, and
  Castillo rejected an actual-knowledge-only reading as "unworkable."
- **The 2024 amendment matters to the case law.** The section's history
  note reads "(Amended by Stats. 2024, Ch. 739, Sec. 1. (AB 2754)
  Effective January 1, 2025.)", and that bill's Legislative Counsel's
  Digest (fetched separately, HTTP 200, 110,308 bytes) says the measure
  "would apply these provisions to port drayage motor carriers, except
  as specified" and "would define port drayage motor carriers for these
  purposes." Castillo (2011) quotes subdivision (a) as then reaching a
  "construction, farm labor, garment, janitorial, or security guard
  contractor" — five categories, no port drayage and no warehouse. The
  clauses therefore say only that Castillo construed an earlier,
  narrower version and quote that earlier list, rather than attributing
  the "warehouse" addition to AB 2754: the digest does not claim it,
  and I did not identify which earlier measure added it. (Three gap
  sentences were rewritten after the first merge for exactly this
  reason; the correction is recorded here.)

## Honest gap(s) disclosed

Every authority clause carries a non-empty `gap`. Disclosed there:
that the statute neither lists the "applicable" laws nor states a
method for computing sufficiency, that the covered industries in
subdivision (a) are the only ones reached, and that the section was
last amended effective January 1, 2025 so that decisions predating the
current text construe a narrower list — with the earlier list quoted
(`lc2810_core_prohibition`); that coverage
turns on acting "in the capacity of" a listed contractor rather than
on licensure, that the five non-drayage contractor categories are
undefined in this section, and that § 2810.4(a)(5)(B)–(C) add
owner/lessee/licensee/bailee and successor coverage summarized rather
than quoted (`lc2810_covered_contractors`); that "family member" and
the part-year residency test are undefined and the statute is silent
on who bears the burden of proving an exception (`lc2810_exceptions`);
that the (b) presumption is rebuttable with no stated rebuttal
standard, that (d)(1)–(6) and (d)(9) are summarized rather than
quoted, and that possible Labor Commissioner regulations adding
required provisions were not surveyed
(`lc2810_written_contract_presumption`); that "material change" is
undefined and no deadline is fixed for updating estimates
(`lc2810_material_changes_and_estimates`); that § 2810 states no
internal penalty for failing to retain or produce the contract and
that the Public Records Act exemption is a bare cross-reference
(`lc2810_recordkeeping_and_labor_commissioner`); that the section
states no limitations period, no class-treatment rule and no Labor
Commissioner citation procedure, and that the injury requirement means
a defective contract alone is not described as supporting damages
(`lc2810_employee_remedies`); that the (i) definitions are phrased as
what the terms "include" and so are not exhaustive, that Castillo is a
First District Court of Appeal decision construing the pre-2024
statute, and that later decisions agreeing or disagreeing with it were
not surveyed (`lc2810_knowledge_standard`); that Castillo is not a
Supreme Court decision and arose on summary judgment/adjudication
review, that its two-question framing is addressed to a "complex
contract," that the two-separate-questions quotation ends before a
footnote-reference marker, and that the court's "cultivating
ignorance" sentence is interrupted mid-sentence by a star-pagination
marker (*1198) in the available text so the adjacent unbroken
sentences are quoted instead (`lc2810_castillo_two_part_test`); and
that the statutory text names no wage standard at all, that the last
two quotations come from the opinion's footnote rather than its body,
that the first quotation begins mid-sentence after "Based on these

enumerated in the clause (`lc2810_measure_of_sufficiency`).

## Method

access went through curl via the environment proxy.

**Statute — two independent leginfo endpoints, different User-Agents.**
Lab. Code § 2810 was fetched twice by two different URL paths on
`leginfo.legislature.ca.gov`:

- `codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=LAB&sectionNum=2810.` — HTTP 200,
  172,219 bytes (Chrome/Windows UA).
- `codes_displayText.xhtml?lawCode=LAB&division=3.&chapter=2.&article=2.`
  — HTTP 200, 234,358 bytes (Safari/macOS UA), the whole of Article 2
  (§§ 2800–2810.8), from which § 2810 was extracted.

(8,433 characters after whitespace normalization, `A == B` True),

Sec. 1. (AB 2754) Effective January 1, 2025.)". Lab. Code § 2810.4 was
fetched by `codes_displaySection.xhtml` (HTTP 200, 183,226 bytes,
Chrome/Windows UA) and its (a)(5)(A) definition independently
confirmed in the Article 2 `codes_displayText` copy above.

then phrase-verified against CourtListener's search index.** The
unauthenticated v4 search JSON located the opinion (Castillo v. Toll
Bros., Inc., 197 Cal. App. 4th 1172 / 130 Cal. Rptr. 3d 150, cluster
