## Why this document

Read the full Confidentiality & IP title list in `data/documents.json`
(87 documents) first. Nearly every candidate the task brief suggested
already exists as its own document or inside one: Code Civ. Proc.
§ 2019.210 (`trade_secret_pre_discovery_identification_info_sheet`;
"2019.210" returns 31 hits), the § 16601/§ 16602.5 exceptions
(`sale_of_business_dissolution_noncompete_exceptions_info_sheet`;
"16601" 27 hits), CIPA (`california_invasion_of_privacy_act_info_sheet`;
"637.2" 30 hits), the CUTSA injunction and reasonable-royalty remedies
(`trade_secret_injunctive_relief_standards_info_sheet`; "3426.2" 27
hits, "reasonable royalty" 18), employee non-solicitation after AMN
Healthcare (inside `employee_nonsolicit`; "AMN Healthcare" 12 hits,
"Loral" 5), reverse engineering as a trade-secret defense
(`dmca_anticircumvention_reverse_engineering_info_sheet` and
`electronics_right_to_repair_ip_disclosure_limits_info_sheet`), and
17 U.S.C. § 411(a)/Fourth Estate (inside
`copyright_registration_info_sheet`; "411(a)" 13 hits, "Fourth Estate"
4).

The search therefore moved to the one federal copyright forum the
corpus does not mention at all. Grepped both data files at `HEAD`
before drafting: `Copyright Claims Board` (0 hits in either file),
`Claims Board` (0), `CASE Act` (0 as that phrase), `Copyright
Alternative in Small-Claims Enforcement` (0), `1501`/`1502`/`1504`/
`1506`/`1509` (hits only for Labor Code §§ 1501-1509, the California
family-leave provisions in the During-employment category, and for
Civil Code cross-references — no Title 17 chapter 15 citation anywhere),
`chapter 15` (6 hits, none about Title 17), `small claims`/`small-claims`
(15 hits, all California small claims court in the Real Estate and
employment categories). The sibling copyright documents were then read
rather than grepped: `copyright_registration_info_sheet` covers § 411(a)
and § 412 timing for a district court suit and says nothing about
chapter 15; `dmca_takedown_notice`, `dmca_counter_notification_info_sheet`
and `dmca_512c_safe_harbor_info_sheet` cover § 512 only. Two existing
clauses do describe § 512(f) misrepresentation liability
(`dmca_misrepresentation_liability` and
`dmcacounter_misrepresentation_liability`), but neither names a forum in
which such a claim may be brought, and neither mentions the Board; this
document adds § 1504(c)(3), which makes a § 512(f) claim one of the three
claim types the Board may decide.

17 clauses — 3 drafting (purpose/scope, relationship to the project's

clauses citing 17 U.S.C. §§ 1502(a), 1502(b)(1), 1503(g), 1504(a),

1504(e)(1)(D), 1504(e)(2)(A)(i), 1504(e)(3), 1504(f), 1505(a)-(c),
1506(c)(1)-(2), 1506(d), 1506(e)(1), 1506(e)(3), 1506(f)(1)(A)-(B),
1506(f)(1)(C)(i), 1506(f)(3), 1506(g), 1506(h)(1), 1506(i), 1506(n),
1506(t)(1)(B), 1506(t)(3), 1506(u)(2), 1506(u)(4), 1506(w), 1506(x),
1506(y)(2)(A), 1506(y)(3), 1506(z), 1506(aa)(1), 1506(aa)(3)(B),
1507(a)(2)-(3), 1507(c)-(f), 1508(a), 1508(c)(1)(A)-(C), 1509(a)-(b);
and two federal decisions, In re Bronner v. Essayzoo (D.D.C. July 2,
2026) and Santora v. Copyright Claims Board (D.D.C. Mar. 26, 2026):

- **`ccb_establishment_and_voluntary_forum`** (§§ 1502, 1504(a), 1506(c),
  1506(d)) — the Board as an alternative forum inside the Copyright
  Office, three Copyright Claims Officers, voluntary participation with
  the right to sue in district court and to a jury trial preserved,
  internet-based proceedings with no in-person appearance requirement,
  and representation by an attorney or a pro bono law student permitted
  but not required.
- **`ccb_permissible_claims`** (§ 1504(c), (f)) — infringement,
  declaration of noninfringement, § 512(f) misrepresentation, the
  same-transaction counterclaim, defenses, multi-party claims, and joint
  and several liability.
- **`ccb_excluded_claims`** (§ 1504(d); § 1506(f)(3)) — claims already
  adjudicated or pending in court, claims by or against Federal or State
  governmental entities, claims against respondents residing outside the
  United States, and dismissal for unsuitability.
- **`ccb_registration_requirement`** (§ 1505) — the application-plus-
  deposit-plus-fee threshold, the certificate condition on rendering a
  determination, abeyance and one-year dismissal, dismissal on refusal,
  and the § 410(c) presumption.
- **`ccb_limitations_period_and_tolling`** (§ 1504(b)) — 3 years from
  accrual, and tolling of the § 507(b) court deadline while the Board
  proceeding is pending.
- **`ccb_commencement_and_service`** (§ 1506(e)-(g)) — filing and fee,
  Copyright Claims Attorney compliance review with two 30-day cures, the
  prior-§ 512-notice condition for claims against certain online service
  providers, and the 90-day proof-of-service deadline.
- **`ccb_opt_out_procedure`** (§ 1506(g)(1), (h)(1), (i), (aa)) — the
  60-day opt-out running from service, dismissal without prejudice on a
  timely opt-out, the statutory warning that not opting out forfeits an
  Article III forum and a jury trial, and the preemptive opt-out for
  libraries and archives with no fee and no renewal.
- **`ccb_monetary_limits_and_relief`** (§ 1504(e)) — the $30,000
  per-proceeding ceiling, $15,000 per work timely registered, $7,500 per
  work and $15,000 per proceeding otherwise, the bar on any willfulness
  finding, the election of damages, agreed cessation as the only
  cessation relief, and each side bearing its own fees absent bad faith.
- **`ccb_discovery_smaller_claims_and_default`** (§ 1506(n), (t), (u),
  (z)) — discovery limited to documents, interrogatories and requests for
  admission; the one-officer track for claims at or under $5,000; written,
  published determinations; and the proposed-default/30-day-opposition
  sequence.
- **`ccb_reconsideration_and_register_review`** (§ 1506(w)-(x)) — 30 days
  for clear error or technical mistake, then 30 days for Register review
  limited to abuse of discretion.
- **`ccb_effect_of_determination`** (§§ 1507, 1509) — party-only
  preclusion, no preclusive effect for an ownership determination, no
  precedential value, party submissions unusable elsewhere, unasserted
  counterclaims preserved, no binding effect on an opt-out party, the
  § 512(g)(2)(C) bridge, the mandatory district court stay, and
  qualification as ADR under 28 U.S.C. § 651.
- **`ccb_district_court_confirmation`** (§ 1508(a)-(b); In re Bronner) —
  the one-year application to confirm an unpaid award and reduce it to
  judgment, the mandatory grant absent vacatur, the fee-and-expense
  shift, and a worked example: a $1,200 default determination confirmed
  and $52 in filing costs imposed on the non-paying respondent.
- **`ccb_limited_judicial_review`** (§ 1503(g), § 1508(c); Santora) — the
  three exclusive grounds for vacating, modifying or correcting a
  determination and the express exclusion of APA review.
- **`ccb_bad_faith_conduct`** (§ 1506(y)) — the $5,000 fee award, the
  $2,500 costs-only award to a pro se party, the extraordinary-
  circumstances exception, and the 12-month filing bar.

## Verification and findings

- **Every statute was fetched twice, from two publishers.** The Office
  of the Law Revision Counsel (`uscode.house.gov`, prelim edition, whose
  retrieved pages state "Text contains those laws in effect on
  September 12, 2026") and, independently, Cornell LII
  (`law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/17/1502` … `/1509`), each under a
  distinct User-Agent. All 11 sections of chapter 15 (§§ 1501-1511) were
  retrieved from the Law Revision Counsel; the 8 sections actually
  quoted were retrieved from both hosts.
- **All 72 `citations[].quote` values in the merged document were
  verified by substring match against both hosts' texts; 0 failures.**
  The check was re-run against the merged `data/clauses.json`. Quotes
  reproduce the Law Revision Counsel characters (straight apostrophes,
  e.g. `attorneys' fees`, `respondent's right`); each authority clause's
  `gap` records that the Cornell rendering carries the identical
  statutory words and differs only in typography.
- **One quote deliberately uses the Cornell character.** § 1505(a) ends
  in an em dash in Cornell's rendering and a plain hyphen in the Law
  Revision Counsel's HTML. The quote reproduces the em dash, and the
  `ccb_registration_requirement` gap and the citation's `case` field
  both say so.
- **Extraction artifacts were identified and normalized symmetrically,
  not written into quotes.** Cornell's markup carries cross-reference
  links inside sentences, so a tag-stripping pass yields `title 28 .`
  and `respondent ’s`; CourtListener's HTML links citations, yielding
  `17 U.S.C. § 1508 (a)`. The comparison collapses whitespace and
  removes spaces adjacent to punctuation and parentheses on **both**
  sides before matching. No quote text was altered to force a match.
- **A candidate quote was dropped rather than normalized.** The Santora
  PDF's text layer renders "Small -Claims" with a stray space inside the
  hyphenated phrase, so the sentence describing the CASE Act's name was
  removed from the citation set instead of being "fixed"; the three
  Santora quotes that remain match both sources exactly.
- **The two decisions were located and retrieved without spending any
  CourtListener MCP quota.** The public, unauthenticated
  `api/rest/v4/search/?type=o` endpoint returned cluster ids 10885130
  (In re Bronner v. Essayzoo) and 10817052 (Santora v. Copyright Claims
  Board). No CourtListener MCP tool was called at any point in this task.
- **Each opinion was read in two independent renderings**: the slip
  PDF on `storage.courtlistener.com` (text extracted with pypdf) and the
  CourtListener HTML opinion page fetched with a browser User-Agent and
  `Referer: https://www.courtlistener.com/` (HTTP 200 on the first try
  for both).
- **Each case was additionally phrase-confirmed** through
  CourtListener's public search index with
  `cluster_id:<id> AND "<exact phrase>"`; both queries returned
  `count=1` with the expected case as the only result (no 429s; two
  queries total).

## Honest gaps disclosed

- **No retrieval path for the two opinions that is independent of
  CourtListener exists in this environment.** The court's own ECF copies
  (`ecf.dcd.uscourts.gov/cgi-bin/show_public_doc?2024mc0117-10` and
  `?2025cv1055-61`, the `download_url` values in the search results) are
  refused by the egress proxy (403 on CONNECT). The double-fetch was
  therefore PDF-versus-HTML from one publisher plus the phrase-level
  index confirmation described above, and both case clauses' gaps say
  that no reporter citation was located this session and identify the
  decisions by docket number and date.
- **Both decisions are unpublished district court memorandum opinions**
  that bind no other court — Bronner resolved an unopposed motion, and
  Santora rested in substantial part on the plaintiff's failure to
  respond to the moving party's arguments. Both clause bodies and gaps
  say so; neither is presented as settled law.
- **A textual irregularity in the enacted statute is disclosed rather
  than silently repaired.** 17 U.S.C. § 1506(y)(2) reads "award
  reasonable costs and attorneys' fees to any adversely affected party
  **of in** an amount of not more than $5,000" — the words "of in"
  appear identically on both hosts, so this is the enacted text and not
  a transcription error. `ccb_bad_faith_conduct` paraphrases that phrase
  in its body, quotes the adjacent § 1506(y)(2)(A) instead, and states
  the irregularity and the reason for the substitution in its `gap`.
- **The Register of Copyrights' regulations were not retrieved.**
  37 C.F.R. parts 220-234 and the Copyright Office fee schedule fill in
  the filing fee amount, the opt-out form, the service-agent directory,
  the smaller-claims procedure and the library/archives opt-out list.
  None of those were independently fetched and verified in this session,
  so the document states no fee amount and no regulatory detail; six
  gaps say expressly that the regulations were not retrieved.
- **Open questions the document does not resolve, each named in the
  relevant gap**: when a copyright claim "accrue[s]" for § 1504(b)(1);
  whether a business entity may appear through a non-attorney officer;
  what "exceptional circumstances" extend the 60-day opt-out; what
  counts as one "work infringed"; what "excusable neglect," "exceeded
  its authority," and "harassing or other improper purpose" mean; and
  how tolling under § 1504(b)(2) interacts with preclusion under
  § 1507(a).
- **Scope.** The document addresses 17 U.S.C. §§ 1501-1511 only. It does
  not restate infringement elements under § 106, fair use, first sale,
  joint authorship, the § 512 notice-and-takedown regime, § 411(a) and
  Fourth Estate, or the § 412 statutory-damages timing rule; a dedicated
  drafting clause places each of those with the sibling document that
  covers it. No constitutional question about adjudication of copyright
  claims outside an Article III court is addressed, and
  `ccb_establishment_and_voluntary_forum`'s gap says so expressly.

## Method

- Grepped `data/clauses.json` and `data/documents.json` for every
  element of the topic before drafting (results above), and read the
  adjacent copyright and DMCA documents rather than relying on titles.
- Verified every `citations[].quote` by substring match against the
  retrieved texts after Unicode normalization (nbsp, curly→straight
  quotation marks and apostrophes, em/en dash→hyphen), whitespace
  collapsing, and removal of extraction-induced spaces adjacent to
  punctuation and parentheses — applied identically to quote and
  source: 72 quotes, 0 failures.
- Fields: `partyName`, `workDescription`, `preparerName` and
  `dateSigned` all reuse existing corpus field ids, and `partyName`'s
  label is reused verbatim from an existing document; no new field id
  was introduced. All four are required, because the app leaves a blank
  field's `{{placeholder}}` visible in the assembled output.
- Authority clauses carry exactly
  `{id,title,kind,status,checkedDate,body,gap,citations}` in that order,
  with a non-empty `gap`; drafting clauses exactly
  `{id,title,kind,body}`; citations exactly `{case,cite,url,quote}`;
  the document exactly
  `{id,title,description,categories,clauseOrder,fields}`.
- Corpus-wide validation after merge: both JSON files parse in the
  repo's two-space, non-ASCII-preserving format; no duplicate clause or
  document ids; every id in every document's `clauseOrder` resolves to
  an existing clause; every `{{placeholder}}` in every document's clause
  bodies and gap notes resolves to a field declared on that document.
- Every clause states what the statute or the opinion says. No clause
  tells a reader what to do, what to file, or whether the Board is the
  right forum for any dispute.
