# Business Formation, new document: Contracts Signed Before the Corporation or LLC Exists — Preorganization Contracts, Adoption, and Ratification — Information Sheet

Part of wave 166 (Business Formation category; wave 166 covers the same
four categories as waves 146/148/150/152/154/156/158/160/162/164:
Confidentiality & IP, Ending employment, Business Formation, Real
Estate).

## Why this document

The brief listed the Business Formation category's recent additions and
several candidate topics. Reading the full list of the 86 existing
Business Formation documents in `data/documents.json` showed that most
of the suggested topics were already whole documents: distributions to
shareholders (`distribution_restrictions_info_sheet`), derivative
actions (`shareholder_derivative_lawsuit_info_sheet`), interested-
director transactions
(`self_dealing_interested_director_officer_transactions_info_sheet`),
§ 2115 (`pseudo_foreign_corporation_2115_info_sheet`), close
corporations (`close_corporation_info_sheet`), the § 25102(f) limited
offering exemption
(`california_limited_offering_exemption_info_sheet`), and suspension/
revivor (`certificate_of_revivor_reinstatement_info_sheet`).

Grepped the whole corpus (both data files) for "promoter" (0 hits),
"pre-incorporation" / "preincorporation" (1 hit — a clause in the
Founders' Agreement about pre-incorporation *IP ownership*, a different
question), "de facto corporation" (0), "corporation by estoppel" (0),
"Timberline" (0), "El Rio Oils" (0), "Cleveland v. Johnson" (0),
"02 Development" (0), "novation" (in other contexts only), and Civ. Code
§§ 2342/2343 (0). Corporations Code §§ 200 and 17702.01 appear in the
corpus only incidentally; no document addresses what happens to an
agreement signed in an entity's name before the entity exists — a
recurring situation for people who sign a lease, a purchase agreement,
or an investor letter "as" a company whose articles are filed weeks
later. Also confirmed as already covered and therefore cross-referenced
rather than duplicated: alter ego liability, personal tort liability of
officers/members, corporate signing authority (§ 313 / Snukal), and
partnership formation by conduct (§ 16202, inside the joint venture and
partnership documents).

## What this document covers

12 clauses: 2 drafting (purpose/scope declaration, preparation and
signature block) and 10 authority clauses.

- **`preorgcontract_when_entity_begins_to_exist`** — Corp. Code § 200(a)
  and (c) (a corporation is formed by executing and filing articles; the
  corporate existence begins upon the filing) and § 17702.01(a), (d),
  (e) (an LLC is formed when the Secretary of State has filed the
  articles of organization; the filing is conclusive proof the organizer
  satisfied all conditions).
- **`preorgcontract_entity_not_a_party_before_formation`** — 02
  Development, LLC v. 607 South Park, LLC (2008) 159 Cal.App.4th 609:
  the entity did not exist when the assignment agreement was executed;
  "a nonexistent business entity cannot be a party to a contract" is
  "true but irrelevant"; once the entity came into existence it could
  enforce a preorganization contract made on its behalf if it adopted or
  ratified it.
- **`preorgcontract_entity_may_adopt_after_formation`** — El Rio Oils,
  Ltd. v. Pacific Coast Asphalt Co. (1949) 95 Cal.App.2d 186, 192-193
  (preorganization agreement made for the corporation's benefit,
  enforceable after organization; the corporation adopted and ratified
  it and performed) plus 02 Development's "hornbook law" statement,
  "California law does not deviate from that well-established norm," and
  its statement that no authority supports treating LLCs differently.
- **`preorgcontract_civil_code_ratification_rules`** — Civ. Code § 2307
  (agency by subsequent ratification), § 2310 (manner of ratification,
  including by accepting or retaining the benefit with notice), § 2312
  (the principal must have power to confer authority at the time of
  ratifying), § 2313 (no retroactive validation to the prejudice of
  third persons), and § 1589 (voluntary acceptance of a benefit is
  consent to the obligations).
- **`preorgcontract_ratification_knowledge_and_self_dealing`** —
  Cleveland v. Johnson (2012) 209 Cal.App.4th 1315, 1335-1337: the jury's
  ratification finding; the actual-knowledge language of Commercial
  Lumber Co. v. Ukiah Lumber Mills (1949) 94 Cal.App.2d 215 and its
  interested-director / promoter-knowledge rule as quoted in Cleveland;
  the court's refusal to apply that principle absent self-dealing
  ("would eviscerate the principle of ratification"); and its reliance on
  benefit plus notice.
- **`preorgcontract_position_of_the_person_who_signed`** — Civ. Code
  §§ 2342, 2343, and 2313, quoted as the Code states them, with an
  express disclosure that no California appellate holding stating the
  treatise promoter-liability rule was located.
- **`preorgcontract_novation`** — Civ. Code §§ 1530, 1531, 1532.
- **`preorgcontract_promoter_fiduciary_position`** — Cleveland quoting
  California-Calaveras Mining Co. v. Walls (1915) 170 Cal. 285, 296, and
  Cleveland's own holding that on those facts the promoter and the
  corporation owed a fiduciary duty to an investor who never received
  stock.
- **`preorgcontract_preorganization_subscription_is_a_security`** — Corp.
  Code § 25019: "preorganization certificate or subscription" is inside
  the statutory definition of "security," and the listed items are
  securities "whether or not evidenced by a written document."
- **`preorgcontract_what_these_authorities_do_not_decide`** — the limits
  both opinions placed on themselves: 02 Development's note that the
  burden of production never shifted on ratification, and Cleveland's
  statement that it was not saying all corporations or promoters owe
  fiduciary duties to all "investors."

## Genuine findings and corrections

- **A remembered case turned out to be about something else.** The
  research began from a recollection that Timberline, Inc. v. Jaisinghani
  (1997) 54 Cal.App.4th 1361 was a promoter-liability decision. Fetching
  it showed it is about whether a corporation suspended for failure to
  pay franchise taxes may renew a judgment — a topic this corpus already
  covers in the revivor sheet. It is not cited. Spencer v. Hibernia Bank
  (1960), which surfaced in a "preincorporation contract" phrase search,
  likewise turned out to concern 19th-century savings-society membership
  and is not cited. Claremont Press Publishing Co. v. Barksdale (1960),
  which matched a "before incorporation" + "personally liable" search, is
  an alter ego case (already covered elsewhere in the corpus) and is not
  cited.
- **An honest negative result, disclosed in the clause rather than
  papered over.** The familiar treatise rule — that a promoter is
  personally liable on a contract made for a corporation not yet formed
  and stays liable after the corporation adopts it unless there is a
  novation — could not be tied to any published California appellate
  holding using the sources available here. CourtListener's free
  full-text search of `cal` and `calctapp` was queried for
  "promoter is personally liable" (0), "promoters are personally liable"
  (0), "nonexistent corporation" + "personally liable" (0), "promoter" +
  "look to the corporation" (0), "promoters" + "liable on the contract"
  (0), and "promoter" + "personally liable" + "novation" (1, a 1931
  decision). Rather than state the treatise rule as California law, the
  signer clause quotes only Civ. Code §§ 2342, 2343, and 2313 and its
  `gap` field says plainly that the broader rule is not stated here and
  why, and the novation clause says the same.
- **Commercial Lumber is quoted only as it appears inside Cleveland.**
  The 1949 opinion was not independently fetched; both affected citation
  entries name it in the `case` field as quoted within Cleveland, and the
  gap says so. The same disclosure is made for California-Calaveras
  Mining Co. v. Walls.
- **OCR defects in the scanned CourtListener texts were avoided, not
  patched.** Cleveland's text renders "[E]ven" as "[Ejven" and "cases" as
  "eases" in places; El Rio Oils and other scanned opinions have similar
  artifacts. Quotations were selected to fall outside the damaged
  stretches (for example, the Commercial Lumber knowledge sentence is
  quoted from "ratification will not be presumed…" onward, after the
  damaged bracketed "[E]ven"), and each affected clause's gap discloses
  the defect and the avoidance. No quote was silently "corrected."
- **Star-pagination markers inside sentences were avoided.** Cleveland's
  sentence quoting Civ. Code § 2310 carries a "*1337" marker in
  mid-citation; that sentence is described rather than quoted, and the
  statute itself is quoted from leginfo in the neighboring clause.
- **§ 25019 is quoted as a truncation, and the truncation is disclosed.**
  The definition is one very long sentence; the quote ends at "investment
  contract" and the clause's gap says that the remainder of the list and
  the four exclusions appear at the linked source.

## Method

- Statutes: Corp. Code §§ 200, 17702.01, 25019 and Civ. Code §§ 1530,
  1531, 1532, 1589, 2307, 2310, 2312, 2313, 2342, 2343 were each fetched
  **twice by two genuinely different routes** on
  `leginfo.legislature.ca.gov` through the environment proxy — the
  single-section view (`codes_displaySection.xhtml`) under a Chrome/Linux
  User-Agent, and the whole-chapter or whole-article text view
  (`codes_displayText.xhtml`) under a Safari/macOS User-Agent. Every
  quoted sentence matched word for word in both.
- Case law: located via the public, unauthenticated CourtListener v4
  search JSON (no MCP quota consumed at any point in this task). Opinion
  text for 02 Development (cluster 2303313), El Rio Oils (cluster
  1162862), and Cleveland v. Johnson (cluster 5811374) was fetched twice
  from CourtListener's opinion HTML pages with two distinct header sets
  (different User-Agent, Referer, and Accept/Accept-Language), and the
  stripped texts were compared by script.
- Third, phrase-level check: `cluster_id:<id> AND "<exact phrase>"`
  queries against the public search endpoint confirmed one phrase from
  02 Development ("it could enforce any preorganization contract made in
  its behalf"), one from El Rio Oils ("it was a preorganization agreement
  made for the benefit of the corporation"), and two from Cleveland
  ("eviscerate the principle of ratification"; "do occupy a position of
  trust and confidence, and it devolves upon them to make full
  disclosure"). The Cleveland queries returned HTTP 429 twice before
  succeeding on retry after a wait; the clause gaps describe this
  accurately.
- All 36 citation quotes in the merged `data/clauses.json` were then
  re-extracted from the committed file and programmatically confirmed as
  exact substrings (after whitespace normalization only) of **both**
  saved fetches of their source — 36/36, 0 failures.
