# Confidentiality & IP, new document: CUTSA Bad-Faith Attorney's Fee Awards (Civil Code § 3426.4) — Information Sheet

Part of wave 160 (Confidentiality & IP category; wave 160 covers the
same four categories as waves 146/148/.../158: Confidentiality & IP,
Ending employment, Business Formation, Real Estate).

## Why this document

The task brief listed the Confidentiality & IP category's recent
additions (CUTSA statute of limitations, customer lists, joint
authorship, DMCA § 512(c), first sale, copyright preemption, inevitable
disclosure) and several suggested topics. Reading `data/documents.json`'s
full Confidentiality & IP title list (83 documents) showed that several
suggested topics were already covered as whole documents (the Code Civ.
Proc. § 2019.210 identification requirement, the Economic Espionage Act,
trade secret injunctive relief standards) and that employee
non-solicitation clauses after AMN Healthcare are addressed inside the
existing Employee Non-Solicitation Agreement. Grepped the whole corpus
(both data files) for "3426.4" (three hits: one sentence inside the
Trade Secret Misappropriation Cease and Desist Letter's remedies
overview clause, whose gap field records that no case law on the fee
provision could be retrieved because of a CourtListener rate limit, and
two out-of-scope mentions in relationship clauses), "SASCO" (none),
"Gemini Aluminum" (none), "FLIR" (none), "CytoDyn" (none), "Cypress
Semiconductor" (hits only for the unrelated 2008 Cypress v. Superior
Court limitations decision), "specious" (none), and "bad faith" (35
hits, none about § 3426.4) — no existing document addresses the
fee-shifting provision, the objective-speciousness / subjective-bad-faith
test, or prevailing-party status after a voluntary dismissal. The
existing trade-secret documents are cross-referenced rather than
duplicated.

## What this document covers

18 clauses: 3 drafting (purpose/declaration, relationship to sibling
documents, signature/acknowledgment) and 15 authority clauses, citing
Cal. Civ. Code § 3426.4; 18 U.S.C. § 1836(b)(3)(D); Gemini Aluminum
Corp. v. California Custom Shapes, Inc. (2002) 95 Cal.App.4th 1249;
FLIR Systems, Inc. v. Parrish (2009) 174 Cal.App.4th 1270; SASCO v.
Rosendin Electric, Inc. (2012) 207 Cal.App.4th 837; CytoDyn of New
Mexico, Inc. v. Amerimmune Pharmaceuticals, Inc. (2008) 160 Cal.App.4th
288; and Cypress Semiconductor Corp. v. Maxim Integrated Products, Inc.
(2015) 236 Cal.App.4th 243:

- **`cutsafee_statutory_text_three_triggers`** (§ 3426.4; CytoDyn) —
  the three triggers, the discretionary award to the prevailing party,
  the 2006 expert-witness-cost sentence, and CytoDyn's overview of the
  act's remedies and its description of Vacco Industries as a fee award
  to a plaintiff after a malice finding.
- **`cutsafee_deterrent_purpose_and_sanction_character`** (Gemini;
  FLIR) — legislative history (via Stilwell): a deterrent to specious
  claims; an award is a sanction within broad discretion; the Gemini
  facts and $160,200 award.
- **`cutsafee_two_prong_standard_speciousness_not_frivolousness`**
  (Gemini; FLIR; SASCO) — objective speciousness (not CCP § 128.5
  frivolousness) plus subjective bad faith; both elements required.
- **`cutsafee_objective_speciousness_complete_lack_of_evidence`**
  (FLIR; SASCO; Gemini; Cypress v. Maxim) — complete lack of evidence;
  defendant need not prove a negative; speculation from a change of
  employers is not evidence; no economic value means no merit; the
  "superficially appears to have merit" clause is superfluous.
- **`cutsafee_sasco_no_import_of_pleading_sanction_standards`** (SASCO)
  — CCP § 128.7 not imported; the court does not ask why evidence is
  missing; discovery-intransigence arguments go to the subjective
  prong; predictive likelihood of discovery is not the test.
- **`cutsafee_subjective_bad_faith_improper_purpose`** (SASCO; Gemini;
  Cypress v. Maxim) — improper purpose (harassment, delay, thwarting
  competition); usually inferred circumstantially; purpose, not belief
  in the merits, is the test.
- **`cutsafee_inferences_timing_ignored_warnings_maintenance`** (Gemini;
  FLIR) — timing; pressing on after opposing counsel identifies fatal
  shortcomings; filing or maintenance; dilatory tactics and settlement
  demands.
- **`cutsafee_flir_anticompetitive_motive_inevitable_disclosure`**
  (FLIR) — the $1,641,216.78 award; CEO's "can't tolerate a direct
  competitive threat" testimony; inevitable disclosure is not the law;
  overbroad injunction and § 16600-violating settlement demands as
  evidence of bad faith.
- **`cutsafee_denial_of_summary_judgment_no_bar`** (FLIR; Gemini) —
  denial of summary judgment, nonsuit, or a motion in limine does not
  preclude a bad-faith finding; sham-declaration rationale; unclean
  hands distinguished.
- **`cutsafee_prevailing_party_after_voluntary_dismissal`** (Cypress v.
  Maxim; SASCO) — three questions; liberal construction of "prevailing
  party"; dismissal to avoid an adverse ruling; the $484,943.46 and
  $180,817.50 awards.
- **`cutsafee_cypress_maxim_recruiting_suit_improper_purpose`** (Cypress
  v. Maxim) — soliciting a competitor's employees is lawful absent
  concomitant wrongdoing; de facto inevitable disclosure; presuit
  threats and press release; evasive § 2019.210 response.
- **`cutsafee_cytodyn_only_trade_secret_claims`** (CytoDyn; SASCO) —
  fees only for trade secret claims, not patent or trademark claims;
  the court is constrained by the statute; § 128.7 and § 3426.4 are
  separate.
- **`cutsafee_burden_findings_hearing_and_review`** (Cypress v. Maxim;
  FLIR; SASCO) — burden on the moving defendant; no express findings
  required; posttrial evidence on bad faith (Yield Dynamics as
  described); abuse-of-discretion review; independent review of legal
  questions.
- **`cutsafee_apportionment_and_fees_on_appeal`** (SASCO; Gemini; FLIR)
  — awards discounted for non-trade-secret claims; fees on appeal,
  fixed by the trial court on remand.
- **`cutsafee_dtsa_parallel_fee_provision`** (18 U.S.C.
  § 1836(b)(3)(D)) — the federal provision; bad faith "may be
  established by circumstantial evidence"; three textual differences
  from § 3426.4 noted as the researcher's own comparison.

## Genuine corrections and findings

- **Italicized words split across lines in the retrieved text**:
  CourtListener's page renders italicized words ("deterrent to
  specious," "Stilwell," "or," "purpose," "express," "they have to
  prove") on their own lines. The quotes spanning them are matched after
  whitespace normalization only; no words were altered. Each affected
  clause's gap field names the italicized word so a reader checking the
  official reporter knows why the line breaks exist.
- **A page marker steered around**: SASCO's sentence "Speculation that
  the individual employees must have taken trade secrets from SASCO
  based on their decision to change employers does not [*849]
  constitute evidence of misappropriation" carries an in-sentence page
  marker in the retrieved text; the clause paraphrases it and the gap
  field says so.
- **An OCR spacing artifact avoided**: CytoDyn's "erroneously—or in
  bad faith— requested" has a stray space after the second em dash in
  the retrieved text; that sentence is paraphrased rather than quoted.
- **Gemini quotes the pre-2006 statute**: Gemini (2002) quotes
  § 3426.4 without the expert-witness sentence added by Stats. 2006,
  ch. 62; the statute clause quotes the current leginfo text and its
  gap field discloses that the earlier version is not reproduced.
- **Vacco Industries and Stilwell described, not retrieved**: the
  third trigger (willful and malicious misappropriation) is illustrated
  only by CytoDyn's footnote description of Vacco; Stilwell is an
  unpublished federal order described only as Gemini described it.
  Both gap fields say so.
- **Knight and Summers are non-§ 3426.4 sanctions cases**: Gemini's
  "subjective state of mind" and "improper motive" language is quoted
  from decisions under other statutes; Cypress v. Maxim's later
  clarification that neither holds that a belief in the merits bars an
  award is recorded in the same clause.

## Method

- Fetched Cal. Civ. Code § 3426.4 from `leginfo.legislature.ca.gov` via
  curl through the sandbox proxy, twice with distinct User-Agent
  strings; the extracted section bodies were identical across both
  fetches after markup stripping and whitespace normalization.
- Fetched 18 U.S.C. § 1836 from two independent hosts (govinfo.gov 2023
  edition, uscode.house.gov) under two User-Agent strings; subparagraph
  (b)(3)(D) identical on both.
- Located the five opinions via CourtListener's public, unauthenticated
  `api/rest/v4/search/` endpoint (outside the MCP quota): Gemini
  (cluster 2299875), FLIR (2267114), SASCO (5811198), CytoDyn (2279652),
  Cypress v. Maxim (2797175). Two of the searches returned HTTP 429 on
  the first pass and succeeded after a wait. **No CourtListener MCP
  call was made** for this document.
- Fetched each opinion's public HTML page twice, each fetch with a
  `Referer: https://www.courtlistener.com/` header and a distinct
  browser User-Agent / Accept / Accept-Language header set (the pages
  returned HTTP 200 with these headers). The raw HTML of the two fetches
  differed (per-request tokens), but after markup stripping and
  whitespace normalization the two text versions of every opinion were
  byte-identical (`totext.py`, `fetch3.sh`).
- All 61 quotes programmatically confirmed as exact substrings of the
  saved, whitespace-normalized fetched texts (`build.py`).
- Phrase-level third check against CourtListener's public search index
  under a third User-Agent (`phrase.sh`): one distinctive quoted phrase
  per opinion submitted as `cluster_id:<id> AND "<phrase>"`; each
  returned its own opinion as the sole hit (count 1), and a fabricated
  negative-control phrase against the FLIR cluster returned zero.
