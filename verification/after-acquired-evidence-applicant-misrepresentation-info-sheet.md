# Hiring, new document: After-Acquired Evidence of Applicant Misrepresentation (McKennon; Cooper v. Rykoff-Sexton; Camp v. Jeffer, Mangels; Murillo; Salas v. Sierra Chemical; Horne) — Information Sheet

## Why this document

Part of wave 161 (Hiring slot), continuing the even-round-robin rotation
that waves 145/147/.../159 also covered (Hiring, During employment,
Estate Planning, Family Law). Checked `data/documents.json` for the 83
existing Hiring-category documents first, then grepped both data files
and the `verification/` directory for every candidate the task brief
suggested. Already covered and therefore ruled out: polygraph and
lie-detector limits (`polygraph_lie_detector_prohibition_info_sheet`,
Lab. Code §432.2 and the federal EPPA); employment agencies and

sheet); and the rescinded-offer, promissory-fraud, negligent-hiring,
cash-bond, and Fair Chance Act sheets named in the brief.

A full-text search of both data files for "after-acquired" found only
unrelated hits (the after-acquired-title rule in the grant deed sheet,
the after-acquired-collateral rule in the UCC-1 sheet, and a one-line
mention in the compelled-self-publication defamation sheet's gap noting

preemption holdings (pp. 419-420, 424) — not for Part III of the
opinion (pp. 427-432), which is the after-acquired-evidence and
unclean-hands holding this sheet is about. This document fills that
gap. It is a hiring-stage topic: every case turns on what the applicant
said (or did not say) on the application or resume or on documents
presented at hire, and Salas defines the doctrine in terms of a
"refusal to hire" as well as a termination; Horne applies it to a
refusal to hire. The intro and cross-reference clauses point to the
Fair Chance Act, §432.7, §1171.5, mixed-motive, background-check,
ICRAA, adverse-action, and fraudulent-inducement sheets without
restating them.

## Genuine findings

- **The decisions form a sequence, and the sheet is organized on it.**
  Cooper (1994) refused a blanket rule on its facts; McKennon (1995)
  supplied the liability/remedies split; Camp (1995) found a complete
  bar on distinctive facts — a government-imposed qualification — and
  through unclean hands; Murillo (1998) put the burden of proving a
  "settled" policy on the employer and carved out claims arising during
  employment; Salas (2014) held neither doctrine a complete defense to
  a FEHA claim; Horne (2015) applied Salas to a hiring decision. Each
  clause says what the case did and did not decide.
- **Camp is not overruled, but its reach is narrowed.** Salas declined
  to treat Camp and Murillo as codified by SB 1818 and rejected reading
  them as "creating a complete defense," but it never says Camp is
  disapproved. The Camp clause's gap and the Salas clause's gap both
  disclose this and say the sheet does not resolve what remains of
  Camp's complete-bar reasoning outside FEHA.
- **Horne is a refusal-to-hire case with no misrepresentation at all.**
  The union never asked about convictions and Horne never volunteered
  one. The clause says so expressly ("after-acquired evidence of
  ineligibility rather than of a false statement") so the sheet's title
  is not read as narrower than the doctrine.
- **The applicant-specific language in Salas is quoted, not inferred.**
  The court wrote "refusal to hire" into the definition (p. 428) and
  the remedial rule (p. 431) and wrote "employee or job applicant" into
  the equities passage (p. 431). Those are the sentences quoted for the
  proposition that the doctrine reaches hiring.
- **Cross-page sentences are split honestly.** McKennon's
  threshold-burden sentence (pp. 362-363), its "general rule" sentence
  (pp. 361-362), Cooper's "unacceptably undermined" sentence (pp.
  618-619), Murillo's "must establish" passage (pp. 845-846), and
  Horne's "We doubt" sentence (pp. 540-541) each cross a reporter page
  break in the source; in every case only the words on one page are
  quoted and the rest is paraphrased, and the clause's gap says so. The
  full McKennon threshold sentence is quoted once, from Camp's fn. 16,
  where it appears in full on one page.

## Method

**Case law only** — this sheet cites no statute directly (Gov. Code
§12940(a) and Lab. Code §1171.5 are quoted in existing sheets and are
cross-referenced, not restated).

Cluster ids were located without any MCP call through CourtListener's
public unauthenticated v4 search JSON
(`/api/rest/v4/search/?type=o&q="<case name>"`), reachable via curl
through the proxy: Camp 2257045, Cooper 2263818, Murillo 2249236,
McKennon 117894 (the 513 U.S. 352 merits opinion, distinguished from
the three certiorari-stage entries), Salas 2680642 (the same cluster
the existing immigration-status sheet cites), and Horne 2779975 (found
on a second query after the first returned 429).

**Opinion text, two independent fetches per opinion:** each
CourtListener opinion HTML page was fetched twice through the proxy
with two distinct full browser header sets (A: Chrome/Windows
User-Agent, `Accept-Language: en-US`; B: Safari/macOS User-Agent,
`Accept-Language: en-GB`), both with `Referer:
https://www.courtlistener.com/`. All twelve fetches returned 200 on the
first attempt with full opinion content (raw sizes: Camp 116,402;
Cooper 60,894; Murillo 112,884; McKennon 77,141; Salas 191,210; Horne
136,128 bytes, identical within each pair). The raw HTML MD5s differ
within each pair (e.g., Camp c1e4c900… vs cbaecde6…; Salas 3583246d…
vs 8cb19d86…) because of per-request page chrome; the
`opinion-content` div was extracted from each copy (tags stripped,
entities unescaped, U+00A0 mapped to a space, reporter page markers
kept as `*NNN`) and the extracted opinion text was byte-identical
between the A and B copies for all six opinions (46,130; 11,620;
40,400; 20,597; 97,843; and 56,936 characters respectively). Saved as
`<cluster>.txt` in the scratch directory alongside `<cluster>.A.html`
and `<cluster>.B.html`. The MCP quota was not used at all for this
document.

**Second, independent path (phrase-level):** six exact-phrase queries,
one per opinion, were submitted to CourtListener's public search index
restricted to the cluster (`q=cluster_id:<id> AND "<phrase>"`,
User-Agent "curl/8 groundtruth-verifier (wave161)"), which runs against
the separate search index rather than the REST API and is not subject
to the MCP quota. All six returned `count: 1` with the correct cluster
as the sole result (log: `phrasecheck_results.json`). The McKennon
query hit 429 twice and succeeded on the third attempt after 75- and
150-second waits inside the turn. The public endpoint throttles hard,
so the phrase check was deliberately limited to one distinctive
passage per opinion rather than one per quotation.
