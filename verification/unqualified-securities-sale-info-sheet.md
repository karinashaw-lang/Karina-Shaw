  already appears in 14 places, across `partnership_formation_default`
  (General Partnership Agreement) and three clauses of
  `joint_venture_formation_info_sheet`; dropped as a near-duplicate.

That left a real hole on the other side of the securities question the
corpus already addresses in part. The corpus covers two *exemptions*
(§ 25102(f) and the compensatory-plan exemption) and mentions § 25110 in
passing in four documents, but greps of both data files found **zero**
hits for § 25501, § 25503, § 25504, § 25504.1, § 25507, § 25540,
§ 25163, § 25008, "risk capital" and "Silver Hills". ("Williamson" has
29 pre-existing hits, all of them the Williamson Act in the Real Estate
documents, unrelated to Williamson v. Tucker.) The only pre-existing
mentions of "investment contract" and "Howey" are in
`preorgcontract_preorganization_subscription_is_a_security`, which
quotes the § 25019 definition for preorganization subscriptions and
whose own gap expressly disclaims the rest: it says that § 25019 "does
not state the qualification requirement … (Corporations Code section
25110), the exemptions from it … or the antifraud provision …; and it
does not decide whether any particular arrangement is a security, a
question California courts analyze under the risk-capital and Howey
tests not quoted here." This document supplies exactly that missing
material — the qualification requirement itself, what makes an
ownership interest a security, and what California law does when
shares, LLC units, or investor notes are sold without qualification or
an exemption. (One consequence of the overlap: the opening sentence of
§ 25019 is quoted in both documents, for different purposes — there for
preorganization subscriptions, here for the scope of § 25110.) That
is the situation founders actually land in (friends-and-family rounds,
units sold to passive investors, notes given for start-up money), so
this document states the requirement, the definition, the two tests, the
territorial rule, the burden of proof, the buyer's remedy, secondary
liability, the limitations period and statutory rescission offer, and
the criminal provision.

## What this document covers

Document id `unqualified_securities_sale_info_sheet`. 12 clauses: 2
drafting (purpose/scope declaration; preparation and signature) and 10
authority clauses, 26 citations in total.

- **`unqualsec_qualification_requirement`** — Corp. Code § 25110, both
  sentences: unlawful to offer or sell a security in this state in an
  issuer transaction unless qualified under § 25111, 25112, or 25113 or
  exempt/not subject to qualification under the chapter beginning at
  § 25100; and an offering conducted in a manner that varies from a
  material term or condition of qualification is itself unqualified.
- **`unqualsec_what_counts_as_a_security`** — Corp. Code § 25019: the
  enumerated definition (note, stock, membership in an incorporated or
  unincorporated association, evidence of indebtedness, certificate of
  interest or participation in a profit-sharing agreement,
  preorganization certificate or subscription, investment contract); the
  LLC-interest entry with its active-management exception and the
  proviso that voting, information, and management rights do not
  establish active management "without more"; the "commonly known as a
  security" catchall and "securities whether or not evidenced by a
  written document".
- **`unqualsec_risk_capital_and_howey_tests`** — Silver Hills Country
  Club v. Sobieski (1961) 55 Cal.2d 811, 815 (risk capital; "The
  purchaser's risk is not lessened merely because the interest he
  purchases is labelled a membership"); People v. Figueroa (1986) 41
  Cal.3d 714, 736–737 and 739 (Silver Hills "introduced the concept of
  'risk capital'"; an investor who retains substantial power to affect
  the enterprise's success has not risked capital); Consolidated
  Management Group, LLC v. Department of Corporations (2008) 162
  Cal.App.4th 598, 609–610 (the two tests, applied separately or
  together; "A transaction is a security if it satisfies either test";
  the Howey formulation).
- **`unqualsec_partnership_and_llc_interests`** — Consolidated
  Management at 610–611: the treatise criterion distinguishing limited
  from general partnership interests, and the Williamson v. Tucker
  three-example framework for when a general-partnership-form interest
  is a security.
- **`unqualsec_offer_or_sale_in_this_state`** — Corp. Code § 25008(a),
  (b) (and (c) summarized): when an offer or sale is made "in this
  state".
- **`unqualsec_exemption_burden_of_proof`** — Corp. Code § 25163: the
  burden of proving an exemption or an exception from a definition is on
  the person claiming it.
- **`unqualsec_buyer_rescission_remedy`** — Corp. Code § 25503: the
  purchaser's action to recover the consideration paid with interest and
  attorney's fees on tender, or damages; tender may be made any time
  before entry of judgment; no liability if the sale is qualified before
  any consideration is paid or received.
- **`unqualsec_who_else_is_liable`** — Corp. Code § 25504 (controlling
  persons, partners, principal executive officers, directors, employees
  and broker-dealers/agents who materially aid, jointly and severally,
  subject to the no-knowledge defense) and § 25504.1 (material
  assistance with intent to deceive or defraud).
- **`unqualsec_limitations_and_rescission_offer`** — Corp. Code
  § 25507(a) (two years after the violation or one year after discovery,
  whichever first expires) and § 25507(b) (a written repurchase or
  rescission offer approved as to form by the Commissioner, open at
  least 30 days, bars suit if not accepted), with (c) summarized.
- **`unqualsec_criminal_exposure`** — Corp. Code § 25540(a) (willful
  violation; up to $1,000,000 and imprisonment; no imprisonment for a
  rule or order violation on proof of no knowledge of the rule or order)
  and People v. Figueroa at 718 and 741 (a § 25110/§ 25540 prosecution;
  instructing that the instruments were securities as a matter of law
  "erroneously removed an element of the section 25110 charge from the
  jury's consideration").

## Sources and independent double-fetch

Every primary source was fetched twice by two genuinely different
routes, and every `citations[].quote` was then checked to appear
**exactly once** in *both* fetched texts after whitespace/nbsp
normalization (`verify.py`, re-run against the merged
`data/clauses.json` as `postverify.py`: 26 citations, 0 failures).

Statutes (Corp. Code §§ 25008, 25019, 25110, 25163, 25503, 25504,
25504.1, 25507, 25540) — all on leginfo.legislature.ca.gov, via the
environment proxy with curl:

- Fetch A: the per-section endpoint
  `codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=CORP&sectionNum=<n>`, Chrome
  user-agent.
- Fetch B: the whole-chapter endpoint `codes_displayText.xhtml`
  (CORP, Title 4, Div. 1 — Part 1 definitions; Part 2 ch. 2; Part 2
  ch. 6; Part 6 ch. 1; Part 6 ch. 3), Firefox user-agent.

These are two different endpoints returning independently rendered
documents (single section vs. full chapter), not the same page fetched
twice. Disclosure: both are served by the same publisher, the California
Legislative Counsel's official site; no second publisher of California
statutory text was used, because the alternatives that were available
are either egress-blocked here (Justia, FindLaw) or unofficial.

Case law — no CourtListener MCP tool was used at any point (the shared
quota was left untouched); the REST search API returned HTTP 429 on the
first attempt and was abandoned in favor of quota-free paths:

- Citation resolution used the quota-free `/c/<reporter>/<volume>/<page>/`
  redirect, which independently mapped **55 Cal.2d 811** →
  `/opinion/1296034/silver-hills-country-club-v-sobieski/`, **162
  Cal.App.4th 598** →
  `/opinion/2291082/consolidated-management-group-v-department-of-corporations/`,
  and **41 Cal.3d 714** → `/opinion/1387758/people-v-figueroa/`.
- Fetch A: that redirect, Chrome user-agent, `Referer:
  https://www.courtlistener.com/`, browser `Accept` headers.
- Fetch B: the resolved opinion URL directly, Firefox user-agent,
  different `Referer`, `Accept`, and `Accept-Language`. Both fetches of
  each opinion returned byte-identical extracted text.
- Phrase-level third check, through a different index entirely: the
  public search feed, `cluster_id:<id> AND "<exact phrase>"` —
  `cluster_id:1296034 AND "soliciting the risk capital with which to
  develop a business for profit"`, `cluster_id:2291082 AND "A
  transaction is a security if it satisfies either test"`, and
  `cluster_id:1387758 AND "erroneously removed an element of the section

  Three feed queries were spent in total.

Pin cites for all eight case quotations were computed from the

## Quote handling and disclosures

- No quote was edited. Nothing was normalized except run-of-whitespace
  and non-breaking spaces during the match; curly apostrophes and
  quotation marks ("purchaser's", "Security", "risk capital") are
  preserved as the sources have them, including the British-style
  "labelled" in the 1961 Silver Hills text.
- **Silver Hills refers to former § 25008.** The Silver Hills sentence
  "Section 25008 defines a security broadly…" construes the pre-1968
  Corporate Securities Act, where § 25008 was the definition of
  "security"; under the 1968 law the definition is § 25019 and § 25008
  is the territorial rule. Rather than quote that sentence and risk
  confusion with the current § 25008 clause in the same document, the
  clause quotes the risk-capital passage at p. 815 instead, and the gap
  of `unqualsec_risk_capital_and_howey_tests` discloses the renumbering
  and explains why Figueroa and Consolidated Management are cited for
  the test's continuing use under the current law.
- **Figueroa p. 739 quote begins mid-sentence.** The sentence "When an
  investor entrusts money or other consideration to a promoter…" is
  split by the reporter page break (`*739`) inside the word
  "consideration" in the fetched text, so the citation quotes only the
  contiguous remainder that begins page 739, and the gap says so.
- **Figueroa's burden-of-proof holding is not quoted.** The passage
  stating it appears in the fetched text with editorial brackets
  (`[that fact]`, `[The trial court properly placed]`), so the body
  states that holding in its own words and the gap of
  `unqualsec_criminal_exposure` discloses that the passage is bracketed.
- **Quotations of quotations.** Three Consolidated Management citations
  reproduce material that decision itself quotes — Reiswig v. Department
  of Corporations (2006) 144 Cal.App.4th 327, 334 (the two tests), II
  Loss et al., Securities Regulation (4th ed. 2007) pp. 985–986 (limited
  vs. general partnership interests), and Williamson v. Tucker (5th Cir.
  1981) 645 F.2d 404, 424 (the three examples). Reiswig, Loss, Williamson
  and Howey were **not** independently fetched; each gap says so, and the
  citations are attributed to the California decision in which the
  quoted text was verified.
- **§ 25540(a) post-dates Figueroa.** The quoted penalties reflect
  amendments made after 1986, so the penalties in force in Figueroa were
  different; the gap discloses this.
- Every authority clause carries a non-empty `gap`; each one also states
  what the clause does *not* decide (no clause evaluates whether any
  particular interest is a security, whether an exemption applies, or
  whether anyone is liable), consistent with the corpus's
  inform-never-advise rule.
