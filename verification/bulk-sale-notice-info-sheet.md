notice and creditor-claims machinery that Division 6 imposes on the
buyer of a restaurant's or retailer's inventory. That machinery is the
single most commonly encountered creditor-protection statute in the
sale of a small California business, and it is the statute an escrow
holder and a buyer actually have to satisfy before closing.

## What this document covers

16 clauses: 2 drafting (applicability note, signature block) and 14
authority clauses, with 84 citation-quote instances drawn from Cal. U.
Com. Code §§ 6101 (1), 6102 (12), 6103 (12), 6104 (3), 6105 (10),
6106.2 (6), 6106.4 (7), 6107 (8), 6108 (4), 6110 (3), Bus. & Prof. Code
§ 24074 (3), Gifford v. J & A Holdings (1997) 54 Cal.App.4th 996 (10),
and Monastra v. Konica Business Machines, U.S.A., Inc. (1996) 43
Cal.App.4th 1628 (5):

- **`bulksale_declaration`** (drafting) — applicability note naming
  buyer, seller, business, and closing date while disclaiming any
  determination that the division applies to the transaction.
- **`bulksale_what_is_a_bulk_sale`** — § 6101's citation name;
  § 6102(a)(3)(ii)'s "more than half the seller's inventory and
  equipment" definition; "value" = fair market value; the
  "ordinary course" test; § 6103(a)(1)–(2)'s two conditions
  (inventory-from-stock or restaurant seller; seller located in this
  state); § 6103(b)'s locating rule; the two 10-percent triggers that
  fix the "date of the bulk sale."
- **`bulksale_excluded_transactions`** — § 6103(c)'s sixteen
  categories, with the $10,000 net-value floor, the $5,000,000
  ceiling, § 6103(f)'s valuation presumption, the paragraph (10)
  debt-assumption exclusion quoted in full, and the judicial-sale and
  required-by-statute exclusions.
- **`bulksale_who_is_a_claimant`** — §§ 6102(a)(4), (5), (6): "claim,"
  "creditor," and the three kinds of claims excluded from "claimant"
  (unmatured employment compensation; personal injury/property/warranty
  claims unless accrued, asserted, and known; tax claims noticed under
  another statute).
- **`bulksale_buyer_three_duties`** — § 6104(a)–(c): the three-year
  business-name list, notice under § 6105, compliance with § 6106.2.
- **`bulksale_notice_contents_and_timing`** — § 6105(a)(1)–(5),
  (b) and (b)(1)–(3), (d): what the notice says, recording with the
  county recorder, publication in the public notice district, registered
  or certified mail to the county tax collector (with the January 1–May 7
  business property statement), the 12-business-day deadline, and the
  definition of "business day."
- **`bulksale_gifford_12_business_days`** — Gifford: independent review;
  the purpose of the scheme; the holding that the notice must be
  recorded and published at least 12 business days before the
  *anticipated* sale date stated in the notice; the facts of the
  untimely October 4 recording; and the court's express limit (no new
  notice needed for every escrow glitch).
- **`bulksale_escrow_claims_procedure`** — § 6106.2(a), (b), (c), (e),
  (f), (g): the $2,000,000 substantially-all-cash trigger, the
  buyer's/escrow agent's duty to pay timely-filed claims, the
  claim-filing deadline and "actually received" rule, the 45-day
  pay-or-interplead duty, the 125 percent withholding for disputed
  claims and 25-day attachment window, and the non-release of liens.
- **`bulksale_insufficient_consideration_priorities`** — § 6106.4: full
  deposit with the escrow agent; the 25–30 day delay and five-business-day
  notice; the good-faith-error immunity; and four of the six priority
  categories (United States obligations, escrow/professional/brokerage
  charges, CCP § 1205 wage claims, other unsecured claims pro rata).
- **`bulksale_noncompliance_liability`** — § 6107(a), (b), (d)(1), (g),
  (h), (i), (j): damages in the amount of the claim less what the
  claimant would not have realized; split burdens; the cap at twice the
  net contract price; no action for unliquidated or contingent claims;
  the four things noncompliance does *not* do (title unimpaired, sale
  not void or voidable, no double satisfaction, no liability outside the
  division); pro tanto discharge; immediate reimbursement from the seller.
- **`bulksale_good_faith_defense`** — § 6107(c) and Gifford's
  application: commercial reasonableness defined as commonly accepted
  practices affording fair treatment; questions of fact on substantial
  evidence; no substantial evidence of the defense there; postponing
  escrow does not cure an untimely recording; the late-claim point.
- **`bulksale_auctions_and_liquidators`** — § 6102(a)(3)(i) and
  § 6108(a)(1)–(4), (c): auctioneer/liquidator substituted for "buyer,"
  net proceeds for net contract price, the extra notice statement, the
  net-proceeds liability cap, and the immunity of persons who buy at the
  auction.
- **`bulksale_limitation_period`** — § 6110(a)–(b): one year from the
  date of the bulk sale; concealment tolling to the earlier of one year
  after discovery or constructive discovery, capped at two years; and
  the express statement that complete noncompliance is not itself
  concealment.
- **`bulksale_fraudulent_transfer_independent`** — Monastra: the Bulk
  Sales Act and the fraudulent transfer laws are cumulative independent
  statutory schemes and compliance with one does not satisfy the other;
  the "central purpose" and the emphasis on "before"; recorded and
  published notice is not all the protection creditors need.
- **`bulksale_liquor_license_escrow`** — § 6103(c)(15) and Bus. & Prof.
  Code § 24074: the alternative route for alcoholic beverage license
  transfers, the mandatory pre-application escrow and full deposit, the
  description-of-consideration requirement, the eight-step creditor
  priority, and the 25-day hold on disputed claims.
- **`bulksale_ack`** (drafting) — preparation and signature block,
  restating that the sheet determines nothing about this transaction.

## Sources and how they were fetched

**Statutes.** Cal. U. Com. Code §§ 6101, 6102, 6103, 6104, 6105,
6106.2, 6106.4, 6107, 6108, 6110, 6111 and Bus. & Prof. Code § 24074
were fetched from `leginfo.legislature.ca.gov` by curl through the
environment proxy, **twice each with distinct User-Agent strings** (a
Chrome/macOS string and a Firefox/Linux string); the extracted section
text of every pair was byte-identical. Three fetches were reset by the
network mid-transfer (§§ 6107, 6108, 6109) and were retried
successfully. The § 6109 request returned a page with no section text,
and the whole-division listing described below confirmed why: Division 6
today consists of §§ 6101, 6102, 6103, 6104, 6105, 6106.2, 6106.4, 6107,
6108, 6110, and 6111 — there is no § 6109, and §§ 6106 and 6106.1 of the
pre-1991 law are likewise gone. As a **third path on a different page of the
same official site**, the whole-division display page
(`codes_displayText.xhtml?lawCode=COM&division=6.`) was fetched and
every one of the 66 Commercial Code quotes was confirmed present there
as well. No independent publisher of the official California code text
was reachable from this environment (Justia, FindLaw, and case.law are
egress-blocked), and that limitation is disclosed in each statutory
clause's `gap` under a "Source note."

**Cases.** Gifford (cluster 2265082) and Monastra (cluster 2247762)
were located through CourtListener's public, unauthenticated v4 search
JSON via curl — **no CourtListener MCP tool was called for this
document**, so no MCP quota was consumed. Each opinion page was fetched
**twice with two distinct full browser header sets** (Chrome/macOS with
`Referer: https://www.courtlistener.com/`, and Firefox/Windows with a
different Accept-Language), and the extracted opinion text was
identical in both fetches. The second Monastra fetch first returned an
empty HTTP 202 challenge response and was retried on a 45-second
backoff loop, after which both fetches matched exactly (47,298
characters each).

**Phrase-level third check (cases).** A distinctive phrase from each
opinion was queried against CourtListener's public search feed with a
`cluster_id:<id> AND "<phrase>"` filter:
`cluster_id:2265082 AND "we hold the notice must be published and
recorded at least 12 business days"` → `count: 1`, Gifford; and
`cluster_id:2247762 AND "cumulative independent statutory schemes"` →
`count: 1`, Monastra. The endpoint returned HTTP 429 twice on the first
query; each was handled by a 120-second wait and retry inside the turn.

## Inform-never-advise check

Every authority clause states what the statute or the opinion says and
attributes it. The document nowhere tells a reader to record a notice,
to open an escrow, to file a claim, to sue, or to choose one route over
another; where the statute imposes a duty, the clause reports the
statutory duty rather than directing the reader. The declaration and
signature clauses state expressly that the sheet does not determine
whether the sale described is a bulk sale, whether any exclusion
applies, whether any notice was timely, or what anyone owes.
