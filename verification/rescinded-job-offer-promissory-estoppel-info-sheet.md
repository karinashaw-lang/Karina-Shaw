## What this document covers

9 clauses: 3 drafting (intro, cross-reference, certification) and 6
authority clauses, citing Lab. Code §2922 (full text); Gov. Code §12940
(introductory clause and subd. (a)); Sheppard v. Morgan Keegan & Co.,
218 Cal.App.3d 61 (1990) (16 passages); and Toscano v. Greene Music,
124 Cal.App.4th 685 (2004) (19 passages):

- **`offerrescind_at_will_presumption_lab_2922`** — §2922; Sheppard's
  description of the presumption and how it is overcome; its application
  to an accepted-but-unstarted offer (indefinite term, no agreed period,
  salary/bonus statements not an assurance of ongoing employment);
  Toscano's footnote quoting Guz on at-will termination.
- **`offerrescind_promissory_estoppel_doctrine`** — Toscano's statement
  of the doctrine (Restatement §90 as quoted from Kajima/Ray Wilson),
  the "equitable principles" substitute-for-consideration rationale, the
  four elements; Sheppard's adoption of the same Restatement text; the
  procedural fact that only the promissory estoppel claim reached trial
  in Toscano.
- **`offerrescind_sheppard_rescission_before_start`** — Sheppard's
  facts (offer, acceptance, same-day resignation, Memphis trips, lease,
  "separate[d]" before the first day, expenses partly reimbursed); the
  holding that the implied covenant carries the understanding that an
  employer cannot expect a new employee to sever his former employment
  and move across the country only to be terminated before the ink dries
  on his new lease; promissory estoppel; the Grouse reasoning; reversal
  of summary judgment because the stated reasons were conclusory or
  disputed.
- **`offerrescind_toscano_reliance_damages_former_job`** — Toscano's
  facts; the plaintiff's concession that wages from the prospective
  employer are not recoverable; the Kajima/Ray "no better position"
  limit and why it does not preclude relinquished future income; the
  holding that lost wages from the former employer are recoverable on an
  appropriate showing; rejection of the at-will-former-job objection by
  analogy to interference with at-will contracts; damages assessed
  against the third-party promisor do not undermine at-will doctrine.
- **`offerrescind_toscano_speculative_damages_limit`** — the
  not-speculative / substantial-evidence condition; the "settled rule"
  against speculative, remote, contingent, or merely possible awards;
  reasonable certainty as the only limitation; why the expert's
  through-retirement projection failed; the conceded one month of
  salary; disposition (future-earnings award vacated, retrial on amount
  only, otherwise affirmed).
- **`offerrescind_unlawful_reasons_feha_12940a`** — Gov. Code §12940's
  introductory clause and subd. (a) ("refuse to hire or employ"), with a
  gap note that the statute does not use the words "offer" or "rescind"
  and that the sheet does not characterize any withdrawn offer as a
  refusal to hire.

## Genuine findings

- **The two cases answer different questions.** Sheppard is a
  summary-judgment reversal about *whether* an employer that withdraws an
  accepted offer before the start date can be liable at all despite the
  at-will presumption; it never reaches damages. Toscano assumes
  liability (the promissory estoppel claim was tried and the judgment
  affirmed except as to amount) and is about the *measure*. The clauses
  are organized on that axis rather than case-by-case, and the
  cross-reference clause says which theories each plaintiff pleaded.
- **Toscano's rule cuts both ways, and both edges are quoted.** The
  holding that lost wages from the former at-will job are recoverable is
  paired, in the same opinion, with the vacatur of the actual award as
  wholly conjectural. Two separate clauses carry the two halves so that
  neither can be read without the other; the "one month" recovery is
  reported as the defendant's concession, not a holding.
- **The prospective-employer-wages point was conceded, not decided.**
  Toscano records that the plaintiff "concedes the weight of authority
  prevents" recovery of wages from the job he never started. The body
  and gap say so explicitly rather than presenting it as a holding.
- **Sheppard quotes §2922 in a form that no longer matches leginfo.**
  The opinion renders the statute as "[a]ll employment, having no
  specified term..."; the current text reads "An employment...". The
  statutory quotation is therefore taken from leginfo, and the
  discrepancy is disclosed in the clause's gap field.
- **The FEHA clause is deliberately narrow.** Whether a withdrawn offer
  is a "refus[al] to hire" is an application question; the clause quotes
  the statute, notes that its verbs span application through discharge,
  and stops there.

## Honest gap(s) disclosed

Each authority clause's `gap` field discloses what it does not cover:
the §2922 wording discrepancy in Sheppard, that Guz is quoted only as
Toscano reproduces it, and that fixed-term or for-cause offers are not
addressed (`offerrescind_at_will_presumption_lab_2922`); that the
Restatement and "equitable principles" passages are Kajima/Ray Wilson as
quoted in Toscano, that other decisions phrase the elements differently,
and that pleading and limitations rules are not described
(`offerrescind_promissory_estoppel_doctrine`); that Sheppard reversed a
summary judgment without deciding liability or damages, that the two
asserted reasons are summarized rather than quoted, and that the
remand outcome is unknown (`offerrescind_sheppard_rescission_before_start`);
that the prospective-employer-wages point was a concession, that the
out-of-state cases are not summarized, and that relocation costs,
benefits, emotional distress, and mitigation are not addressed
(`offerrescind_toscano_reliance_damages_former_job`); that the opinion
does not prescribe what proof would satisfy reasonable certainty and
that the retrial outcome is not reported
(`offerrescind_toscano_speculative_damages_limit`); and that §12940(a)'s
exceptions, the remaining subdivisions, the coverage threshold, the
administrative prerequisites, and federal law are not restated
(`offerrescind_unlawful_reasons_feha_12940a`).

## Method

**Statutes** — fetched directly from leginfo.legislature.ca.gov via curl
through the environment's proxy (raw HTML, the `codeLawSectionNoHead`
statutory-text div parsed directly, as in prior waves):

- Lab. Code §2922 and Gov. Code §12940: each fetched twice with distinct
  User-Agents ("Mozilla/5.0 (X11; Linux x86_64) Chrome/128.0" and
  "curl-groundtruth-verify/2"), all four fetches 200 OK. Raw page sizes
  differed between fetches (162,228 vs 162,900 and 183,468 vs 183,980
  bytes) because of session-specific JSF ViewState tokens; the extracted
  statutory-text div was byte-identical between the two fetches for both
  sections (544 and 19,022 characters including the heading breadcrumb
  and enactment note; MD5 7d461ba7… and 79429048… respectively). §2922
  was additionally fetched a third time through WebFetch, which returned
  the same two sentences and the same history note (Stats. 1971, Ch.
  1607).

**Case law** — cluster ids were located without API calls through
CourtListener's public unauthenticated v4 search JSON
(`/api/rest/v4/search/?type=o&q=...`), reachable via curl through the
proxy: Toscano 2274753 (D043281; 124 Cal.App.4th 685) and Sheppard
2171353 (A043356; 218 Cal.App.3d 61). Comeaux v. Brown & Williamson
(9th Cir. 1990) was searched for but not located in the index and is not
cited.

The non-quota paths for opinion text were all tried and all failed for
these two opinions: the CourtListener opinion HTML page returned a 202
AWS WAF JavaScript challenge (2,006 bytes, `challenge-container`) on
every one of eight attempts across two full browser header sets
(Chrome/Windows and Safari/macOS, with Accept, Accept-Language,
Sec-Fetch-*, Upgrade-Insecure-Requests), retried over a minute; the
`local_path` reported by the search index
(`california/court_of_appeal_opinions/documents/e2744011aff570b5.xml`)
returned 404 NoSuchKey on storage.courtlistener.com; the v3 and v4 REST
opinion and cluster endpoints returned 401 without credentials; and
courts.ca.gov's opinion archive has no PDF for a 2004 (or 1990) docket
number. The shared MCP quota was therefore used for exactly two calls,
one per opinion — `get_endpoint_item` on `opinions` with `fields`
restricted to `id, plain_text, html_with_citations` — both of which
succeeded on the first attempt (no 429). `plain_text` was empty for
both; `html_with_citations` (Harvard/LU-sourced XML) carried the full
opinions. The paragraphs quoted in the corpus were transcribed from
those results into `toscano_api_paragraphs.txt` and
`sheppard_api_paragraphs.txt` in the scratch directory (tags removed,
entities unescaped, whitespace collapsed, reporter page markers kept as
`*NNN`), and every quote was checked as an exact substring of that text.

**Second, independent path for the case quotations:** each quoted
passage was split at quotation-mark characters and bracketed editorial
insertions and each resulting fragment of five or more words was
submitted to CourtListener's public search index as an exact-phrase
query restricted to the cluster (`q=cluster_id:<id> AND "<fragment>"`,
User-Agent "curl/8 groundtruth-verifier (wave159)"), which runs against
the separate search index rather than the REST API and is not subject
to the MCP quota. The public search endpoint has its own throttle
(intermittent 429s, handled by 60-second waits inside the turn) and
rejects some punctuation with 400, so fragments were kept free of
parentheses and citations. Results (logs `feed_check_run1.log`, `feed_check2.log`,
`feed_check3.log` and `feedverify_results.json` in the scratch
directory): 52 fragments were generated from the 35 case quotations;
48 returned exactly one entry (the correct case) on the first pass. The
4 that did not were re-queried as punctuation-free sub-phrases in a
follow-up pass: the Sheppard Grouse passage (the bracketed
[plaintiff]/[defendant] insertions and the "(Id., at p. 116.)" citation
had broken the phrase) matched as four sub-phrases, each exactly once;
the Toscano Kajima/Ray "[u]nless there is unjust enrichment" passage
matched as two sub-phrases, each exactly once; and the Toscano
"Toscano concedes the weight of authority..." sentence matched as five
sub-phrases covering every word except the three words "from the
prospective" (set in italics in the API text), which the index did not
return as part of any phrase that could be constructed around them. That
residual is disclosed in the gap field of
`offerrescind_toscano_reliance_damages_former_job`; the Grouse
sub-phrase method is disclosed in the gap field of
`offerrescind_sheppard_rescission_before_start`. A phrase search
confirms word sequence rather than punctuation characters; punctuation
was checked against the API text.
