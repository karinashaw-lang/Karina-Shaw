## Genuine findings

- **Statutory wording has changed since Snukal.** The Supreme Court
  quoted § 313 and § 312 as they read in 2000 ("chairman of the
  board"; "a chairman of the board or a president or both"). § 313 was
  amended by Stats. 2015, ch. 98 (effective January 1, 2016) and § 312
  by Stats. 2022, ch. 617 (effective January 1, 2023); both now read
  "chairperson," and § 312 adds the alternative titles "chair of the
  board" and "chairperson." The clauses quote the current text from
  leginfo and the court's text from CourtListener, and each affected
  gap says which is which and that the difference is one of wording.
- **The court's "treasurer" versus the statute's "chief financial
  officer" / "assistant treasurer."** At 23 Cal.4th 784 the court
  lists the financial category as "secretary, assistant secretary,
  chief financial officer, or treasurer," and at 786 says § 312
  requires "a secretary and a treasurer," while the statutes say
  "assistant treasurer" and "chief financial officer." The quotations
  reproduce the court's words; the clause bodies follow the statute;
  the gaps flag the discrepancy and do not resolve it.
- **Footnote 10 is the only place the opinion preserves other
  defenses.** The body's statement that fraud, mistake, and failure
  of consideration remain available is drawn from footnote 10, quoted
  in full, and the gap says the list is the court's parenthetical
  illustration, not exhaustive.
- **§ 17703.01(d) has no located case law.** The LLC two-manager rule
  parallels § 313 in wording, but no published decision construing it
  was located through the sources consulted; the gap says so and
  expressly declines to say whether Snukal's one-person-two-offices
  reasoning would carry over.
- **Italic line breaks in CourtListener's text.** The source renders
  italicized words ("despite," "actual knowledge," "justifiable,"
  "and," "when … signed by … of such corporation") on their own lines.
  Every affected quotation is reproduced with ordinary spacing and the
  clause's gap discloses the normalization. One source artifact — a
  stray space in "designated— execute" at 23 Cal.4th 785 — is
  reproduced as published and disclosed.
- **Leginfo tab-indented line breaks.** § 313's final words ("had no
  … authority") and several passages of § 17703.01 break across lines
  with tab indentation on the Legislature's page; reproduced as single
  spaces and disclosed in the gaps.
- **Star-page split avoided.** The opinion's concluding sentence on
  the substantive issue is split by the "*788" marker ("chief
  financial *788 officer and secretary"); rather than quote across the
  marker, the clause quotes the court's parallel holding sentence at
  page 787 and the introductory holding at page 759, neither of which
  crosses a page break.

## Honest gaps disclosed

The opinion does not define "actual knowledge," and the sheet supplies
no definition. It does not address a signer who falsely claims an
office. Ratification and benefit-based estoppel, which the court
mentions only inside a quotation from Memorial Hospital, are not
separately treated. The nonprofit and cooperative counterparts of
§ 313 (§§ 5214, 7214, 9214, 12354), the Legislative Committee and
Assembly committee comments, Taormina, Black v. Harrison Home Co.,
Mariposa, Memorial Hospital, Associates Discount, Avery, and Collins
were not fetched; their words are attributed to Snukal as the opinion
that quoted or cited them. Chapters 18–20 of the General Corporation
Law, cross-referenced in § 208(a), are not quoted. The sheet does not
describe incumbency or secretary's certificates, the statement of
information's disclosure of officers and managers, or an operating
agreement's allocation of authority.

## Method

Fetched Corp. Code §§ 208, 312, 313, 17703.01 and Civ. Code §§ 2317,
2330, 2334 from `leginfo.legislature.ca.gov` via curl through the
environment proxy, each at least twice with distinct User-Agent
strings (a Chrome desktop string, a Safari/macOS string, and a
non-browser "Groundtruth-verify" string — the four Corporations Code
sections were fetched three times); the extracted section text of
every set was confirmed identical. (WebFetch was not used for
statutes; no fetch required a retry.) § 2330 and § 17703.04 were
fetched but not used. Snukal was located through CourtListener's
public, unauthenticated v4 search JSON (cluster id 2545675 — no MCP
quota used) and its opinion page fetched via curl with a full browser
header set (Referer, Accept, Accept-Language), twice with distinct
User-Agent strings (Chrome/Windows and Safari/macOS); the extracted
texts were byte-identical (96,930 characters). All 38 citation quotes
were programmatically confirmed as exact whitespace-normalized
substrings of every fetch of their source, and each Snukal quote's pin
cite was checked against the nearest preceding star-page marker in
the CourtListener text (footnote 10, which sits after the body text,
was placed by its call-out between the *783 and *784 markers). As an
additional phrase-level check, a nine-word window from each of the 23
Snukal quotes was queried against CourtListener's public search feed
with a `cluster_id:2545675 AND "<phrase>"` filter; results are recorded
in the "Phrase check" section below. The anonymous search endpoint
returned HTTP 429 repeatedly during this pass (the quota is shared
with other parallel agents); each was handled by a 300-second wait and
retry, which is why the 23 queries took roughly an hour. No
CourtListener MCP tool was called for this document.

## Phrase check

23 queries: 21 returned the Snukal cluster (count=1) on the first
successful query; 2 did not, and — under the coordinator's instruction
to record the tally as it stood rather than wait further — neither was
re-run:

- `signauth_snukal_two_categories_operational_and_financial_officer`,
  first quotation (the "two officers—one officer having general,
  operational responsibilities" sentence at 23 Cal.4th 784): the
  nine-word window "the board, president, or any vice-president) and
  one officer" contained an unbalanced parenthesis and the search
  endpoint returned HTTP 400 (query-syntax error), so no phrase-level
  result was obtained.
- `signauth_common_law_express_and_ostensible_authority_when_313_does_not_apply`,
  fifth quotation (the "At common law, the party seeking to enforce a
  contract" sentence at 23 Cal.4th 779): the window "generally has the
  burden of establishing the contracting officer’s" returned count=0,
  most likely because of the curly apostrophe in "officer’s".

Both quotations were, like all the others, confirmed as exact
whitespace-normalized substrings of two independent full-text fetches
of the opinion, and the corresponding gap fields now disclose that the
phrase-level check did not cover them. The remaining 21 phrase queries
(covering every other Snukal quotation in the sheet) each returned
exactly one result, Snukal v. Flightways Manufacturing, Inc.
