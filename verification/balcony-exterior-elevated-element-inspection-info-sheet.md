# Real Estate, new document: Balcony and Exterior Elevated Element Inspections (Health & Saf. Code § 17973; Civ. Code § 5551) — Information Sheet

Wave 158 (Real Estate). Continues the even round-robin of waves 146,
148, 150, 152, 154, and 156.

## Why this document

Before this document, the corpus's 82 Real Estate documents had no
clause on California's two statutory inspection programs for
wood-framed balconies, decks, stairways, and walkways on multifamily
buildings — the "SB 721" apartment-building program in Health and
Safety Code § 17973 and the "SB 326" condominium program in Civil
Code § 5551. A grep of both data files for `balcon`, `elevated
element`, `5551`, `17973`, `SB 721`, `SB 326`, and `SB 410` returned
only two passing mentions: the Landlord's Right of Entry sheet notes
that Civ. Code § 1954(a)(6) lists "compliance with a specific article
of the Health and Safety Code beginning at section 17973" as an entry
purpose it does not cover, and the Solar Shade Control Act sheet's
`5986` hit is a Public Resources Code number. No existing clause
describes what either inspection statute requires.

The topic is timely: the § 17973 initial deadline (extended by AB 2579
from January 1, 2025 to January 1, 2026) and the § 5551 initial
deadline (January 1, 2025) have both passed as of the check date, and
SB 410 (Stats. 2025, Ch. 516), effective January 1, 2026, added the
first-page report summary to § 5551 and made the report a resale
disclosure document (§ 4525(a)(11)) and an association record
(§§ 5200(a)(15), 5210(a)(3)).

## Genuine corrections and findings

- **Amendment history verified by diff, not assumed.** The § 17973
  text enacted by AB 2579 (chaptered bill text, 2024) was compared
  sentence-by-sentence against the current section text. The only
  differences are the renumbering of subdivision (d) into (d)(1) and
  the addition of the asbestos paragraph (d)(2). The section's history
  note attributes the latest amendment to Stats. 2025, Ch. 22, Sec. 32
  (AB 130), effective June 30, 2025. The deadlines clause states the
  2025 addition on that basis and says so in its `gap`.
- **§ 5551 has changed since SB 326 in ways the SB 410 digest does not
  fully explain.** Diffing the SB 326 chaptered text against the
  current section shows four changes: (1) "a licensed structural
  engineer or architect" became "a licensed structural or civil
  engineer or architect"; (2) "containing the following information"
  became "containing the following"; (3) paragraph (e)(5) (first-page
  summary and certification) was added; and (4) subdivision (l) gained
  the word "attached." The SB 410 digest accounts for (3) and (4). The
  amending act for (1) was not identified — an attempt to locate it
  (bill AB 2114 of 2021-2022) turned out to be an unrelated bill about
  urban forestry — and the scope clause's `gap` says the amending act
  for the civil-engineer addition was not identified rather than
  guessing.
- **Deadline dates.** The § 17973 initial deadline is stated as
  January 1, 2026 (current text) with the original January 1, 2025
  date attributed to SB 721 via the AB 2579 digest's "Existing law"
  sentence and the extension attributed to AB 2579 via its "This bill
  would" sentence — both quoted. The § 5551 deadline (January 1, 2025)
  is unchanged from SB 326.
- **No case law exists.** A public (non-MCP) CourtListener opinion
  search for the exact phrase "exterior elevated element" returned
  `count: 0`. The first authority clause's `gap` discloses this. No
  opinion is cited and no CourtListener MCP quota was consumed.
- **Line-wrap artifacts.** The Legislature's section pages render
  some sentences with a line break at an HTML span boundary in
  mid-sentence (e.g., "The purpose of / the inspection"). Every
  authority clause's `gap` discloses that quotes join those with a
  single space. No quote needed any other change; the statutes' curly
  quotation marks and apostrophes are reproduced as they appear.

## Honest gaps disclosed

- Civ. Code §§ 1134, 4100, 5550, 6000, Health & Saf. Code § 17922,
  Bus. & Prof. Code § 11018.6, and the Right to Repair Act are
  cross-referenced but not fetched; each clause that leans on one says
  so.
- § 17973 does not define "multifamily dwelling unit," "immediate
  threat," or "ground level," and does not say when the 180-day period
  in (i)(2) begins; the clauses report the text without resolving
  those questions.
- § 5551 contains no per-day penalty or lien mechanism; the
  immediate-threat clause says so expressly rather than importing
  § 17973's remedies.
- Whether any deadline has been met for the building named in the
  fields is flagged as a question of fact the sheet does not address.
- The § 5986 clause is labeled as a related SB 326 provision, not part
  of the inspection requirement, and its inclusion is explained in its
  `gap`.

## Method

Fetched Health & Saf. Code § 17973 and Civ. Code §§ 5551, 4525, 5200,
5210, 5986, 6150, and 1954 from `leginfo.legislature.ca.gov` twice
each with two distinct User-Agent strings (a browser-style UA and a
`groundtruth-corpus-verifier` UA), via curl through the environment
proxy; extracted the `codeLawSectionNoHead` div from each fetch with a
small HTML parser and confirmed the two extractions byte-identical for
every section after nbsp/whitespace normalization. Several first
attempts were reset by the proxy (`curl: (35) Recv failure`) and were
retried until both fetches succeeded; no section was accepted on a
single fetch.

Fetched the chaptered bill text pages for SB 721 (2017-2018), SB 326
(2019-2020), AB 2579 (2023-2024), AB 130 (2025-2026), and SB 410
(2025-2026) from `leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/billTextClient.xhtml`
twice each with the same two UAs; confirmed identical after tag
stripping and whitespace normalization. Legislative Counsel's Digest
quotes come from those pages (the corpus already cites bill-text pages
in this form for AB 1513 and SB 807). AB 130 was fetched to confirm it
amends § 17973 (its title lists 17973 among the Health and Safety Code
sections amended) but its digest is not quoted.

A build script verified all 61 `citations[].quote` strings as
whitespace-normalized substrings of both fetched copies of the cited
source before writing anything; the merge step ran only after every
quote passed. Schema keys were asserted exactly. A corpus-wide
validation confirmed both data files parse, all 5,345 clause ids and
648 document ids are unique, every `clauseOrder` id exists, and every
`{{placeholder}}` in every document resolves to a declared field.

The public CourtListener search endpoint returned HTTP 429 on the
first attempt (shared rate limit); waited in-turn and retried, after
which the exact-phrase query returned zero results. No CourtListener
MCP tool was called for this document.
