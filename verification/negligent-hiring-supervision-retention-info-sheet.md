employer liability for negligent hiring, supervision, and retention was

the employer's own duty of care in selecting employees (and the follow-on

employer may and may not ask about criminal history, consumer reports, and

restating them.

## What this document covers

11 clauses: 2 drafting (intro, acknowledgment) and 9 authority clauses,
citing Civ. Code §1714(a); Gov. Code §815.2(a); Code Civ. Proc. §335.1;
Evan F. v. Hughson United Methodist Church, 8 Cal.App.4th 828 (1992)
(four passages); Doe v. Capital Cities, 50 Cal.App.4th 1038 (1996) (three
passages); Federico v. Superior Court, 59 Cal.App.4th 1207 (1997) (three
passages); Delfino v. Agilent Technologies, Inc., 145 Cal.App.4th 790
(2006) (three passages); Phillips v. TLC Plumbing, Inc., 172 Cal.App.4th
1133 (2009) (two passages); Diaz v. Carcamo, 51 Cal.4th 1148 (2011) (two
passages); and C.A. v. William S. Hart Union High School District, 53
Cal.4th 861 (2012) (three passages):

- **`nh_general_duty_civ_1714`** — Civ. Code §1714(a): the general duty
  of ordinary care from which the doctrine derives.
- **`nh_doctrine_recognized_evan_f`** — Evan F.: California follows the
  Restatement (Second) of Agency §213 view that an employer may be
  liable to a third person for negligently hiring an incompetent or
  unfit employee; the "knows / has reason to believe / fails to use
  reasonable care to discover" formulation; the comment-d "special duty
  of investigation" language for work likely to subject third persons
  to serious risk of great harm.
- **`nh_particular_risk_doe`** — Doe v. Capital Cities: liability rests
  on the employer knowing or having reason to know that hiring created
  a *particular* risk and that *particular* harm materializing;
  knowledge of drug use was not knowledge of a propensity to drug and
  assault.
- **`nh_specific_work_federico`** — Federico: the duty is breached only
  where the known facts would warn a reasonable person of undue risk
  "in light of the particular work to be performed"; the employer is
  not a guarantor of the safety of everyone the employee incidentally
  meets; Evan F. distinguished on the nature of the position.
- **`nh_direct_not_vicarious_delfino`** — Delfino: negligent
  supervision/retention is direct, not vicarious, liability; no breach
  without employer knowledge; no liability without causation.
- **`nh_ends_at_termination_phillips`** — Phillips: no duty for harm
  inflicted by a former employee after termination, even where the
  victim was first met during employment.
- **`nh_admission_vicarious_diaz`** — Diaz (Cal. Supreme Court): an
  employer's admission of vicarious liability makes negligent
  entrustment, hiring, and retention claims irrelevant (Armenta
  reaffirmed).
- **`nh_public_entity_ca_hart`** — Gov. Code §815.2(a) and C.A. (Cal.
  Supreme Court): a public school district may be vicariously liable
  for administrators' negligent hiring, supervision, and retention;
  liability must rest on evidence, not speculation or presumption.
- **`nh_limitations_ccp_335_1`** — Code Civ. Proc. §335.1: two-year
  period for injury caused by the neglect of another.

## Genuine findings

- **The doctrine has two very different "knowledge" failure modes, and
  the cases split cleanly between them.** Doe (demurrer) fails on the
  *type* of knowledge — the employer knew something bad about the
  employee, but not the thing that materialized. Federico (summary
  judgment) fails on the *fit* between the knowledge and the job — the
  employer may well have known exactly what materialized, but the
  position did not entrust the employee with the class of victims at
  risk. The two clauses are written so a reader sees both axes rather
  than a single "knew or should have known" slogan.
- **Diaz sits in tension with the rest of the doctrine and the gap field
  says so.** Diaz makes negligent hiring claims "irrelevant" once the
  employer admits respondeat superior — but respondeat superior is
  typically *unavailable* in the sexual-assault fact patterns of Evan
  F., Doe, Federico, and C.A. (conduct outside the scope of
  employment), which is precisely why negligent hiring matters there.
  The Diaz clause's gap field flags that the opinion arose from a
  driving case and does not address that situation.
- **For public entities, the route is vicarious, not direct.** C.A. does
  not recognize a direct negligent-hiring claim against a public entity
  (public-entity liability is exclusively statutory under Gov. Code
  §815); it reaches the entity through §815.2 for the negligence of the
  human administrators who did the hiring. The clause body and gap make
  that mechanism explicit so the reader does not conflate it with the
  private-employer direct-liability cases.
- **Federico and the corpus's Fair Chance Act document point in opposite
  directions, deliberately.** Federico assumed the employer knew of two
  sex-offense convictions and still found no breach; the Fair Chance Act
  (adopted 20 years later) restricts when that information may be
  obtained or used at all. The Federico gap field notes the later
  statutes and defers to the existing documents rather than attempting
  to reconcile them.

## Honest gap(s) disclosed

Each authority clause's `gap` field discloses what it does not cover:
that §1714(a) is the general duty only, that its firearms sentence and
subdivisions (b)–(d) are not restated, and that the Restatement is a
secondary source quoted only as reproduced in the opinions
(`nh_general_duty_civ_1714`); Evan F.'s summary-judgment posture,
religious-employer and minor-care context, the un-fetched Restatement,
and the opinion's spacing artifacts within its Restatement quotations
(`nh_doctrine_recognized_evan_f`); Doe's demurrer posture and its
separate FEHA holding (`nh_particular_risk_doe`); Federico's writ
posture, its express assumption of knowledge, the later-enacted
criminal-history statutes, and the un-fetched Roman Catholic Bishop
opinion from which it quotes the Restatement
(`nh_specific_work_federico`); Delfino's principal CDA §230 holding and
the tentative character of its duty analysis
(`nh_direct_not_vicarious_delfino`); Phillips's alternative grounds and
its silence on duty-to-warn theories (`nh_ends_at_termination_phillips`);
Diaz's driving-case origin and its inapplicability where respondeat
superior is unavailable (`nh_admission_vicarious_diaz`); C.A.'s
school-district and special-relationship context, its reservation of
immunity questions, and the un-quoted §815.2(b)
(`nh_public_entity_ca_hart`); and §335.1's silence on accrual, tolling,
§340.1, and the Government Claims Act (`nh_limitations_ccp_335_1`).

## Method

**Statutes** — fetched directly from leginfo.legislature.ca.gov via curl
through the environment's proxy (raw HTML fetched and the
`codeLawSectionNoHead` statutory-text div parsed directly, following
the approach used in prior waves):

- Civ. Code §1714, Code Civ. Proc. §335.1, Gov. Code §815.2: each
  fetched twice, User-Agent "Mozilla/5.0 (Windows NT 10.0; Win64; x64)
  ... Chrome/122.0.0.0" and "Mozilla/5.0 (Macintosh; Intel Mac OS X
  10_15_7) ... Version/17.2 Safari/605.1.15". All six fetches 200 OK.
  Raw page sizes varied between the two fetches of a section
  (163,092–165,505 bytes) because of session-specific JSF ViewState
  tokens; the extracted statutory-text div was byte-identical between
  the two fetches for all three sections (2,422 / 537 / 1,110
  characters including the heading breadcrumb and enactment note).

**Case law** — located via CourtListener `search` (type "o"): the query
`"negligent hiring" "particular risk"` (courts `cal`/`calctapp`,
published only, most-cited first) surfaced Juarez, Delfino, Doe,
Phillips, Evan F., Rosencrans, Roman Catholic Bishop, Jackson v. AEG,
and the Court of Appeal decision in C.A.; `case_name` searches located
Diaz v. Carcamo (cluster 844279), the Supreme Court decision in C.A.
(cluster 844215), and Federico (cluster 2243921). For every case used,
cluster_id and opinion_id are the same number (all combined opinions).

The CourtListener API's shared rate limit (5/min, and then a 125/day
cap) was hit repeatedly during this task because four parallel agents
share it; each 429 was waited out with `time.sleep` loops inside the
turn. The daily cap forced a change in second-fetch method for the last
three cases, described below.

- Evan F. (2277473), Doe (2292640), Phillips (2287828), Delfino
  (2247530): first fetch via `get_endpoint_item` (endpoint_id
  "opinions", fields `["id","html_with_citations","plain_text"]`),
  second fetch via `read_document` (opinion_id). `plain_text` was empty
  for all four; `html_with_citations` was saved from each fetch and
  `cmp` reported the two files byte-identical for every case (50,105 /
  58,362 / 51,577 / 131,196 characters).
- Diaz (844279) and C.A. (844215): first fetch via `get_endpoint_item`
  as above (`plain_text` present but `html_with_citations` used). The
  daily cap prevented a second API fetch, so the second fetch was the
  original slip opinion PDF that CourtListener stores at the
  `local_path` reported by search
  (`storage.courtlistener.com/pdf/2011/06/23/diaz_v._carcamo.pdf`,
  181,699 bytes; `.../2012/03/08/c.a._v._william_s._hart_union_high_school.pdf`,
  155,121 bytes), fetched with the Chrome User-Agent and text-extracted
  with pdfminer. This is a stronger independence check than a second
  API call (different document, different pipeline); reporter
  pagination was taken from the API text's star-pagination markers.
- Federico (2243921): both `get_endpoint_item` and `read_document`
  returned the full opinion inline (25,915 characters) rather than
  persisting it to disk, and the CourtListener web page, the Caselaw
  Access Project mirror, and Justia were all blocked (WAF challenge,
  proxy policy, 403). The four paragraphs quoted (ids b955-6, b956-5,
  b957-5, b957-6) were transcribed verbatim from the inline output into
  a scratch file, the three quotes were confirmed as exact substrings of
  that transcription, and each of the three quotes was then
  independently confirmed by CourtListener `search_document`
  (opinion_id 2243921), which performs a literal server-side match
  against the stored opinion markup and was not subject to the daily
  cap. Because that search runs against raw markup, the two quotes that
  cross an `<em>` boundary in the source ("...third persons in / light
  of the particular work to be performed" and "...the facts in / Evan
  F. / and the present case...") were searched by their longest
  single-text-node portion; each query returned exactly one match, and
  the returned snippet showed the remainder of the quote on the other
  side of the tag boundary. The p. 1215 "guaranteeing the safety"
  quote was searched in full and returned exactly one match.
