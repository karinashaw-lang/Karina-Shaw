# Hiring, new document: Pay Scale Disclosure and Salary History Ban — Information Sheet

## Why this document

The existing Offer Letter, Job Description Template, and Wage Theft
Prevention Act Notice documents don't address California's separate
pay-transparency statutes. This document covers the salary-history
ban and pay-scale disclosure requirements under Labor Code § 432.3.

## What this document covers

9 clauses: 2 drafting (purpose, acknowledgment) and 7 authority
clauses:

- **`payscale_salary_history_ban`** (Labor Code § 432.3(a), (b), (g))
  — the two salary-history prohibitions, applicable to all employers
  regardless of size.
- **`payscale_voluntary_disclosure_and_expectations`** (§ 432.3(h),
  (i), (j), (k)) — the voluntary-disclosure and salary-expectation
  exceptions.
- **`payscale_reasonable_request_disclosure`** (§ 432.3(c)(1), (c)(2))
  — the request-triggered pay-scale disclosure duties, no size
  threshold.
- **`payscale_job_posting_requirement`** (§ 432.3(c)(3), (c)(5)) — the
  proactive job-posting requirement and its 15-employee threshold.
- **`payscale_definitions`** (§ 432.3(m)(1), (m)(2)) — the statutory
  definitions of "pay scale" and "applicant."
- **`payscale_records_penalty_enforcement`** (§ 432.3(c)(4), (d)(1)-
  (5)) — the recordkeeping duty, complaint/civil-action procedures,
  civil penalty range, and rebuttable presumption for missing records.
- **`payscale_1197_5_not_job_posting_source`** (Labor Code
  § 1197.5(a), (e)) — a dedicated correction clause confirming
  California's Equal Pay Act contains no job-posting requirement.

## A major correction caught during research

The task brief attributed the job-posting pay-scale requirement, its
15-employee threshold, the third-party-posting duty, the recordkeeping
duty, the rebuttable presumption, and the $100-$10,000 civil penalty
to Labor Code § 1197.5. Direct review shows **every one of these
provisions actually lives in § 432.3(c) and (d), not § 1197.5**. The
agent read § 1197.5 in full (subdivisions (a)-(l)) and confirmed it is
California's Equal Pay Act — a sex/race/ethnicity wage-differential
prohibition with its own separate, general 3-year recordkeeping duty —
and contains no job-posting requirement of any kind. A dedicated
clause was built specifically to correct this easy-to-propagate error
from secondary sources, reading the absence from the full statutory
text rather than a partial reading.

## Other findings

- Confirmed the salary-history ban applies to all employers regardless
  of size, and the request-triggered pay-scale disclosure duties
  (applicant and current-employee) also carry no size threshold —
  only the proactive job-posting duty and its third-party extension
  carry the 15-employee threshold.
- Found and honestly flagged a textual asymmetry the statute itself
  doesn't resolve: the recordkeeping duty (§ 432.3(c)(4)) has no
  stated size threshold either, unlike the job-posting duty it
  supports — disclosed as an open textual observation, not resolved by
  assumption.
- Confirmed the $100-$10,000 penalty range, the totality-of-
  circumstances standard, and that the first-violation cure applies
  only to subdivision (c) (pay-scale provisions), not to the
  salary-history-ban provisions in (a)/(b).
- Confirmed the missing-records consequence is a genuine rebuttable
  presumption (the statute's own word choice), not merely "an adverse
  inference."

## Honest gaps disclosed

- The statute does not define how "15 or more employees" is counted
  (part-time, out-of-state employees, measurement date) — disclosed as
  unresolved rather than assumed.
- No case law was found or invented; this is purely statute-driven.
