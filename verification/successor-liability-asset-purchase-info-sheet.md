# Business Formation, new document: Successor Liability When Buying a Business's Assets (Ray v. Alad Corp.; Corp. Code § 1107; Rev. & Tax. Code §§ 6811–6814; Unemp. Ins. Code §§ 1731–1732; Lab. Code § 200.3) — Information Sheet

## Why this document

Processed item of wave 158 (Business Formation slot, continuing the
round-robin with waves 146/148/150/152/154/156). Read all 82 existing
"Business Formation" document titles/ids in `data/documents.json`
before choosing a topic, and grepped both data files for every
candidate the brief suggested and several more. Results: cumulative
voting (§ 708) and director removal (§ 303) are already treated inside
`corporate_bylaws` (`bylaws_cumulative_voting`,
`bylaws_director_removal`); distributions (§§ 500–501), derivative

— zero hits in either file; "successor liability" — one clause hit,
inside the nonprofit-merger sheet (`npmerger_effect_successor_liability`,
which addresses statutory succession in a nonprofit merger, not the
asset-purchase doctrine); "product line" — one unrelated hit (patent
licensing); "§ 6811", "§ 6812", "§ 1731", "§ 1732", "§ 200.3", "bulk
sale" — zero hits. The chosen topic — when a buyer of a business's
assets becomes answerable for the seller's debts — was confirmed
genuinely open. It is the question every asset purchaser and every
creditor of a sold business confronts, and the corpus's existing merger,
UVTA, alter ego, and dissolution sheets each stop at its edge.

## What this document covers

11 clauses: 1 drafting (applicability note) and 10 authority clauses,
with 43 citation-quote instances drawn from Ray v. Alad Corp. (1977)
19 Cal.3d 22 (10), Franklin v. USX Corp. (2001) 87 Cal.App.4th 615
(9), Cleveland v. Johnson (2012) 209 Cal.App.4th 1315 (10), Corp. Code
§ 1107 (3), Rev. & Tax. Code §§ 6811, 6812, 6814 (6), Unemp. Ins. Code
§§ 1731–1732 (3), and Lab. Code § 200.3 (2):

- **`succliab_declaration`** (drafting) — applicability note naming
  buyer, seller, business, and closing date while disclaiming any
  determination that any rule applies to the transaction.
- **`succliab_general_rule_and_four_exceptions`** — Ray's "typically
  formulated" rule and four exceptions; the "free availability and
  transferability of capital" rationale; Franklin's Ortiz restatement.
- **`succliab_express_or_implied_assumption`** — Ray's reading of the
  Alad I/Lighting sale agreement (only ordered materials and unfilled
  orders assumed; no express or implied assumption of product
  liability); Franklin's bill-of-sale nonassumption language and its
  parol-evidence holding.
- **`succliab_de_facto_merger_and_mere_continuation`** — Ray's
  description of the merger exception (no consideration available to
  creditors, or stock promptly distributed on liquidation) and the two
  mere-continuation elements; Franklin's "crucial factor" / "common
  denominator" statements on inadequate consideration; Cleveland's
  contrary reading ("one or both"; no single element decisive;
  declining to require inadequacy of consideration); Cleveland's
  quotation of Beatrice on recourse to the debtor corporation.
- **`succliab_equitable_doctrine_not_confined_to_corporations`** —
  Cleveland: doctrine almost always couched corporation-to-corporation,
  but not confined to corporations; equitable, examined on its own
  facts; a corporation may be the mere continuation of a separately
  operated line of business of a continuing seller.
- **`succliab_fraudulent_transfer_ground`** — Ray's finding of no
  fraudulent purpose; Cleveland's alternative ground built on the
  jury's malice/oppression/fraud findings.
- **`succliab_product_line_exception`** — Ray's three justifications
  and holding; Franklin's summary of Ray's conditions and its refusal
  (following Monarch Bay II and Maloney) to extend the exception
  beyond product liability actions.
- **`succliab_statutory_merger_contrast`** — § 1107(a), (c), (d): the
  surviving corporation succeeds to all debts and liabilities, creditor
  rights and liens preserved, pending actions bind the survivor.
- **`succliab_sales_and_use_tax_successor_withholding`** — §§ 6811,
  6812(a)–(c), 6814(a), 6814(b)(1): withholding from the purchase
  price, personal liability up to the purchase price, the 60-day
  certificate-or-notice deadline and its three trigger dates, release
  on failure to mail notice, the three-year enforcement limit, notice
  of successor liability, and penalty relief for reasonable cause.
- **`succliab_unemployment_insurance_contributions_successor`** —
  §§ 1731, 1732(a)–(b): withholding in trust, the 30-day certificate or
  statement, deemed certificate on silence, cap at the purchase price,
  release of the acquirer but not the employer.
- **`succliab_wage_judgment_successor_labor_code_200_3`** — § 200.3(a)
  (final-judgment trigger and four successorship criteria) and (b)
  (other means of establishing successor liability not limited).

## Genuine findings

- **Court of Appeal disagreement on inadequate consideration.**
  Franklin (2001) states that inadequate consideration is the "common
  denominator, which must be present" for both de facto merger and
  mere continuation; Cleveland (2012) expressly notes that "[s]ome
  Court of Appeal cases have stated that inadequate consideration is
  essential," reads Ray's "one or both" language as suggesting
  otherwise, and "decline[s] to find that a plaintiff must necessarily
  establish the inadequacy of the consideration." Both are presented in
  the same clause, with the gap field stating that the sheet does not
  resolve which formulation a trial court would follow.
- **Franklin's appellate district corrected before merge.** A draft
  gap described Franklin as a Second District decision; its headmatter
  reads "First Dist., Div. Three." The gap now says so and describes
  Cleveland only as "a different Court of Appeal panel," since
  CourtListener's Cleveland page does not print the district (its
  docket number is B233762).
- **OCR defects in Ray avoided rather than corrected.** CourtListener's
  Ray page is a scanned-source text carrying a typo notice; it renders
  "Alad Fs" for "Alad I's," "Une" for "line," "Uability," "obügations,"
  "liabihties," "rulé," and "considerátion" in various sentences. Every
  sentence quoted from Ray was chosen to fall outside those defects
  and was confirmed clean in both fetches; each Ray-citing clause's gap
  discloses the page's condition. The three-justification sentence has
  italic-induced line breaks around "successor" and "tort" in the HTML,
  normalized as ordinary whitespace and disclosed in that clause's gap.
- **Ray's merger-exception sentence is split by a citation.** The
  opinion interposes "(Malone v. Red Top Cab Co. …)" between the two
  halves of the sentence; the halves are quoted as two citation entries
  and the gap explains why.
- **Nested quotations disclosed.** Franklin's four-exception sentence
  quotes Ortiz v. South Bend Lathe; Franklin's bill-of-sale sentence
  quotes the parties' contract (with the court's "[t]he"); Cleveland's
  "recourse to the debtor corporation" sentence quotes Beatrice;
  Cleveland's "own unique facts" phrase quotes CenterPoint; Franklin's
  Monarch Bay II / Maloney characterizations describe opinions not
  fetched. Each gap attributes the words to the opinion that quoted
  them and says the underlying source was not fetched.
- **"Board" versus CDTFA.** Rev. & Tax. Code §§ 6811–6814 still read
  "the board." The clause body uses "the agency" and the gap explains
  that administration moved to the California Department of Tax and
  Fee Administration under legislation the sheet does not quote; the
  quotations reproduce the statutory wording unchanged.
- **§ 200.3 is judgment-only.** The Labor Code successorship rule
  applies to amounts owed under a final, non-appealable judgment; the
  gap says it does not itself create liability for unreduced wage
  claims, and that no published appellate decision construing it was
  located through the sources consulted.

## Honest gaps disclosed

Neither Ray nor the later cases states a checklist of contract wording
that will or will not amount to an "implied" assumption; none is
supplied. Whether each of Ray's three justifications is a required
element of the product-line exception is not stated in the sentences
quoted and the sheet does not say so. The sheet does not describe the
Uniform Voidable Transactions Act (covered by its own sheet), alter ego
(likewise), the bulk sales article of the Commercial Code, successor
provisions for other state taxes and fees, EDD experience-rating
transfer, or the mechanics of the certificate requests. Monarch Bay II,
Maloney, Ortiz, Beatrice, CenterPoint, and Fotouhi were not fetched.

## Method

Fetched Lab. Code § 200.3, Rev. & Tax. Code §§ 6811, 6812, 6813, 6814,
6815, Unemp. Ins. Code §§ 1731, 1732, and Corp. Code § 1107 from
`leginfo.legislature.ca.gov` via curl through the environment proxy,
each twice with distinct User-Agent strings (a Chrome desktop string
and a non-browser "Groundtruth-verify" string); the extracted section
text of every pair was confirmed identical. (WebFetch was not used for
statutes; one curl connection reset on § 200.3 and one on § 6814 were
retried successfully.) Ray, Franklin, and Cleveland were located
through CourtListener's public, unauthenticated v4 search JSON
(cluster ids 1221982, 2287235, 5811374 — no MCP quota used) and their
opinion pages fetched via curl with a full browser header set, twice
each with distinct User-Agent strings (Safari/macOS and Firefox/Windows)
and separate cookie jars; a plain-header request returns an AWS WAF
challenge page (HTTP 202), and WebFetch returned an empty page for the
same URL, so the second path is the second browser-header fetch. All
43 citation quotes were programmatically confirmed as exact
whitespace-normalized substrings of both fetches of their source, and
each case quote's pin cite was checked against the nearest preceding
star-page marker in the CourtListener text. As an additional
phrase-level check, a nine-word window from each of the 29 case quotes
was queried against CourtListener's public search feed with a
`cluster_id:<id> AND "<phrase>"` filter; 28 of 29 returned the opinion on
the first query, and the one miss (the Cleveland sentence whose first
word is fused to a star-page marker in the source text, "*1334The
Supreme Court has not addressed…") was confirmed on a second query
using a window from inside the same sentence, for 29 of 29. The
anonymous search endpoint returned HTTP 429 several times during this
pass; each was handled by a 300-second wait and retry. No
CourtListener MCP tool was called for this document.
