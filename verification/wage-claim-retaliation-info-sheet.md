# Ending employment, new document: Retaliation for Filing a Wage Claim — Information Sheet

## Why this document

This topic was previously only a passing mention inside an existing
clause, `wrongfulterm_98_6_wage_retaliation`, in the corpus's existing
Wrongful Termination in Violation of Public Policy document — limited to
subdivision (a)'s core prohibition together with (b)(1) and (b)(3). This
document independently re-verifies Labor Code § 98.6 from primary
sources and genuinely deepens coverage: subdivision (b)(2)'s separate
misdemeanor, subdivision (c)'s applicant protections, subdivision (e)'s
family-member protection, and the related administrative complaint
process and deadline under the separate Labor Code § 98.7 — none of
which the existing passing-mention clause covers. Second document of
wave 54 (the "1×4" pattern's Ending employment slot).

## What this document covers

7 clauses: 2 drafting (background declaration, closing/signature) and 5
authority clauses, citing Cal. Lab. Code §§ 98.6, 98.7, and (for
comparison) § 132a:

- **`wageretaliation_core_prohibition_and_protected_conduct`**
  (§ 98.6(a)) — the core prohibition and the statute's own list of
  distinct categories of protected conduct (not just wage claims).
- **`wageretaliation_remedies_reinstatement_reimbursement_and_penalty`**
  (§ 98.6(b)(1)-(3)) — reinstatement/reimbursement, a standalone
  misdemeanor for willful refusal to rehire, and a civil penalty up to
  $10,000 per employee per violation.
- **`wageretaliation_presumption_and_administrative_deadline`**
  (§ 98.6(b)(1), § 98.7(a)(1), (g)) — the 90-day rebuttable presumption,
  and the separate one-year administrative-complaint deadline (with no
  exhaustion requirement) under a different Labor Code section.
- **`wageretaliation_applicant_and_family_member_protections`**
  (§ 98.6(c)(1), (e)) — protections beyond the already-discharged
  employee: job applicants and family members of a person who engaged in
  protected conduct.
- **`wageretaliation_distinguishing_from_132a_and_civil_action_path`**
  (§ 132a) — a comparative clause distinguishing § 98.6's civil-action-
  plus-optional-administrative-complaint path from the corpus's existing
  Workers' Compensation Retaliation (§ 132a) document's exclusive WCAB
  petition procedure.

## Genuine corrections and findings

- **Protected conduct is broader than "wage claims" alone**: the statute
  lists five distinct categories — chapter-delineated conduct (including
  § 96(k) whistleblowing and Labor Code §§ 1101 et seq.), Labor
  Commissioner-jurisdiction complaints/proceedings, written/oral unpaid-
  wage complaints, PAGA (§ 2699) actions/testimony, and general exercise
  of afforded rights.
- **The (b)(2) misdemeanor is disclosed as standalone**, not assumed to
  carry the (b)(1)/(b)(3) remedies — the statute's own text does not
  cross-reference them.
- **The 90-day presumption is rebuttable, not conclusive** — burden-
  shifting only.
- **A real filing-deadline distinction, honestly disclosed as a gap**:
  § 98.6 itself has no stated civil-action statute of limitations; the
  one-year deadline belongs to the separate, optional § 98.7
  administrative complaint (extendable for good cause, no exhaustion
  requirement under (g)).
- **Structural comparison to § 132a**: § 98.6 uses civil action plus an
  optional administrative complaint; § 132a uses an exclusive WCAB
  petition procedure with its own one-year deadline and no misdemeanor
  jurisdiction — independently re-verified rather than copied from the
  existing § 132a document's already-verified quote.

## Honest gap disclosed

No civil-action statute of limitations specific to Labor Code § 98.6
itself was located or asserted — only the separate § 98.7 administrative
deadline. Subdivision (e)'s family-member protection has a further
qualification (regarding certain lawful off-duty-conduct claims) noted
for completeness but not independently analyzed.
