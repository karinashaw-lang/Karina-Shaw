What "Tier A/B/C" and "Cited/Verified/Adversarially checked" mean

A document's tier and a clause's verification level are computed, never hand-assigned — see methodology for the exact, nine-item checklist a clause has to pass to count as "Verified," and what "Adversarially checked" adds on top of that. The counts above are the same figures that checklist produces, corpus-wide.

Check it yourself, per document

Every document has its own public verification record: every legal-authority clause it uses, each with its level, its citation, the exact quote checked, and a live link to the primary source — so a claim is checkable against the government's own text, not just this product's say-so.

What enforces the paywall

Paid features (cloud save, sharing, e-signature, exports with a sources appendix) are gated by a Postgres row-level-security policy on the server, not just hidden in the browser's UI — a signed-in user without an active subscription cannot write those rows at all, regardless of what the client-side code does or doesn't show. See Privacy Policy for what account data that involves.

What this page doesn't claim

A high tier or verification level describes how a clause's sourcing was checked — it is not legal advice, and it is not a claim that a document is right for your situation. See methodology and each document's own notices for exactly what is and isn't claimed.
